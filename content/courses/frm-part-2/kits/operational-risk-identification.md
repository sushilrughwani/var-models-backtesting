## Slides
### Slide 1: The blind-spot problem
- Loss data: objective but backward-looking
- Expert judgement: forward-looking but subjective
- Use four tools and look for disagreements
### Slide 2: RCSA in three steps
- Rate inherent risk: likelihood x impact (1-25)
- Assess control design and operating effectiveness
- Rate residual risk and compare with appetite
### Slide 3: Controls and what they reduce
- Preventive controls cut likelihood
- Detective and corrective controls cut impact
- Insurance transfers impact
### Slide 4: Key risk indicators
- Metrics that signal changing exposure
- Leading (vacancies, backlogs) versus lagging (loss counts)
- Green / amber / red thresholds with owners
### Slide 5: Internal loss data
- Dates: occurrence, discovery, accounting
- Gross loss, recoveries, event type
- Collection threshold; record near misses and gains
### Slide 6: External loss data
- Fills the tail with rare large events
- Must be scaled for size and business mix
- Consortium versus public sources
### Slide 7: Scenario analysis
- Severe but plausible events
- Frequency per year x severity per event
- 1-in-20 years means frequency 0.05
### Slide 8: Workshop biases
- Anchoring, availability, groupthink, overconfidence
- Independent facilitator; individual estimates first
- Benchmark against external data
### Slide 9: Worked numbers
- RCSA: 4 x 5 = 20 inherent, 2 x 5 = 10 residual
- Scenarios: 2 + 3 + 3 = USD 8m expected annual loss
- Rare scenarios dominate the tail
### Slide 10: Exam traps
- Inherent versus residual
- Loss counts are lagging
- Frequency is 1/N, not N

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - RCSA.** Wrong-beneficiary payment risk: inherent likelihood 4 x impact 5 = 20. Controls cut likelihood to 2: residual 2 x 5 = 10, within an appetite of 12. Variation: if controls fail testing and likelihood returns to 3, residual is 15, outside appetite - add controls or accept with sign-off.

**Example 2 - Scenario expected loss.** Processing error 0.25 x 8 = 2.0; ransomware 0.05 x 60 = 3.0; mis-selling 0.02 x 150 = 3.0. Total USD 8.0m per year. Point out that tail scenarios supply 6.0 of the 8.0.

## Questions to ask students
- A KRI tracks the number of open audit findings older than 90 days. Leading or lagging? (Leading for future losses - it signals unresolved control weaknesses)
- What is the annual frequency of a 1-in-25-year event? (0.04)
- Why is external loss data scaled before use? (Loss sizes depend on firm size and business mix)
- Which bias is at work if a workshop fixates on last month's cyberattack in the news? (Availability bias)
- What reduces residual impact rather than likelihood? (Detective/corrective controls and insurance)

## Analogies
- RCSA as a home inspection: the hazard (inherent risk), the smoke detectors and locks (controls), and what you would still lose if things went wrong (residual risk).
- KRIs as a car dashboard: the temperature gauge rising (leading) is more useful than the engine seizing (lagging).
- Scenario workshops as a jury: you want independent votes before deliberation, or the loudest juror decides.

## Recording tips
- Draw the 5x5 heat map on screen and plot the payments risk moving from 20 to 10.
- Spend time on frequency conversion; many candidates write 20 instead of 0.05.
- Stress that tools are complements - exam questions often ask which tool covers which weakness.
