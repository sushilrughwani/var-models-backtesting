## Slides
### Slide 1: Runs are faster now
- Mobile banking and social media accelerate outflows
- Concentrated uninsured deposits leave first
- Historical run-off rates may understate stress
### Slide 2: Beyond the LCR
- LCR: one standard 30-day scenario
- Internal tests: bank-specific vulnerabilities
- Multiple horizons and currencies
### Slide 3: Scenario types
- Idiosyncratic: downgrade, fraud, bad press
- Market-wide: freeze, sovereign stress, rate shock
- Combined: usually the most severe
### Slide 4: Behavioural assumptions
- Deposit run-off and wholesale non-rollover
- Committed line drawdowns and collateral calls
- Wider haircuts, intraday and trapped liquidity
### Slide 5: Counterbalancing capacity
- CBC = cash + sum of MV x (1 - stressed haircut)
- Only unencumbered assets count
- Plus reliable committed funding
### Slide 6: Survival horizon
- Cumulative net outflows versus CBC
- First time CBC is exhausted
- Board sets a minimum, e.g. 30 or 90 days
### Slide 7: Worked table
- CBC 1,200; outflows 350, 250, 200, 180, 150, 120
- Remaining 70 after week 5, -50 after week 6
- Survival about 5.6 weeks
### Slide 8: CFP components
- Governance and crisis team
- Early warning indicators and escalation stages
- Ranked funding actions, communication, testing
### Slide 9: Ranking funding actions
- Speed, certainty, cost, reputational impact
- Repo HQLA, sell assets, draw lines, slow growth
- Central bank facilities as a last line
### Slide 10: Reverse stress tests and traps
- What breaks us in two weeks?
- Use stressed haircuts and unencumbered assets only
- Test the plan with real transactions

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Survival horizon.** CBC 1,200. Week 1 outflow: 40% x 500 + 3% x 5,000 = 200 + 150 = 350. Cumulative: 350, 600, 800, 980, 1,130, 1,250. Remaining: 850, 600, 400, 220, 70, -50. Exhausted in week 6; interpolation 5 + 70/120 = 5.58 weeks.

**Example 2 - CBC after haircuts.** Cash 100, government bonds 500 (2% haircut), corporate bonds 300 (20% haircut): CBC = 100 + 490 + 240 = 830, not 900.

**Example 3 - Daily survival.** CBC 900; net outflows 60 per day for 10 days, then 30 per day. After 10 days: 300 left; 300 / 30 = 10 more days. Survival 20 days. Using 60 per day throughout would give 15 days; using 30 per day throughout would give 30.

## Questions to ask students
- Why run several horizons rather than just 30 days? (Different funding sources run at different speeds; a bank can survive 30 days but fail at day 3 or day 60)
- Does an encumbered government bond count in CBC? (No - it is already pledged)
- Give two internal and two external early warning indicators. (Internal: deposit outflows, rising funding costs; external: CDS spread widening, share price fall)
- Why is central bank borrowing usually ranked last? (Stigma and signalling, plus eligibility and operational constraints)
- What does a reverse stress test answer? (Which scenario would exhaust the liquidity buffer within a given time)

## Analogies
- Survival horizon as a submarine's oxygen supply: you know how many days you have before you must surface.
- A CFP as a fire drill: the plan on the wall is useless unless people have practised the exits.
- Early warning indicators as smoke detectors - cheap, sensitive and most useful when they sound before the flames.

## Recording tips
- Fill in the cumulative table live; the interpolation step is where students hesitate.
- Contrast a test transaction with a paper plan - real examples resonate.
- Link back to the LCR lesson: internal stress tests complement, not replace, the ratio.
