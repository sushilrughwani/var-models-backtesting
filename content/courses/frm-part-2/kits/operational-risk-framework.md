## Slides
### Slide 1: Operational risk is everywhere
- Not chosen for a return - a by-product of doing business
- Processes, people, systems, external events
- Legal risk included; strategic and reputational excluded
### Slide 2: Seven event types
- Internal fraud; external fraud; employment practices and workplace safety
- Clients, products and business practices
- Damage to physical assets; business disruption and system failures
- Execution, delivery and process management
### Slide 3: Frequency versus severity
- Execution and process errors: most events, small losses
- Clients, products and business practices: biggest loss amounts
- Fraud and disruption: rare but potentially severe
### Slide 4: Governance at the top
- Board approves framework and appetite
- Senior management implements policies, systems and accountability
- Risk culture: speak-up, incentives, learning from incidents
### Slide 5: Three lines of defence
- First line: business owns and controls its risks
- Second line: risk function designs tools and challenges
- Third line: internal audit gives independent assurance
### Slide 6: Effective challenge
- Second line must question, not just collect, self-assessments
- Needs independence, stature and resources
- Evidence of challenge: changed ratings, escalations, reports
### Slide 7: Risk appetite and tolerance
- Zero tolerance for fraud, sanctions breaches, misconduct
- Quantitative loss limits, often as % of gross income
- Amber triggers below the limit prompt early action
### Slide 8: Utilisation arithmetic
- Limit = 2% x 2,000m = 40m
- Trigger = 80% x 40m = 32m
- Losses 28m: 70% used, green
### Slide 9: Framework building blocks
- Identification and assessment; change management
- Monitoring and reporting; control and mitigation
- Resilience and continuity; disclosure
### Slide 10: Exam traps
- Reputational risk is not in the definition
- First line owns; second line challenges; audit assures
- Appetite is a ceiling, not a budget to spend

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Loss limit utilisation.** Gross income USD 2,000m, tolerance 2%: limit USD 40m. Amber trigger at 80%: USD 32m. Year-to-date losses USD 28m: utilisation 28/40 = 70%, green, USD 4m headroom to amber. Add a USD 6m settlement: 34/40 = 85%, amber, escalate.

**Example 2 - Classify the event.** Ask the class to classify: (a) a teller steals cash - internal fraud; (b) phishing drains client accounts - external fraud; (c) a bank mis-sells payment protection insurance - clients, products and business practices; (d) a payment is sent twice due to keying error - execution, delivery and process management; (e) a server outage halts online banking - business disruption and system failures.

## Questions to ask students
- Is a fall in share price after a scandal an operational loss? (No - reputational impact is excluded from the definition; fines and settlements would be included)
- Who owns the risk of a payments error in the operations department? (The first line - operations itself)
- What makes the second line effective rather than administrative? (Independence and evidence of challenge to first-line assessments)
- Why set an amber trigger below the loss limit? (To prompt management action before the limit is breached)
- Which event type usually produces the largest total losses? (Clients, products and business practices)

## Analogies
- Three lines of defence as a football team: players (first line) defend every attack, the coach (second line) designs tactics and shouts when players drift out of position, and the league inspector (third line) checks the whole club follows the rules.
- Risk appetite as a car's fuel warning light: the light (trigger) comes on well before the tank is empty (limit).
- Operational risk as friction: every machine that moves generates it; you manage it, you never eliminate it.

## Recording tips
- Show the seven event types on screen and keep them visible while working through classification questions.
- Emphasise "own, oversee, assure" as a three-word summary of the three lines - candidates mix these up constantly.
- Pause after the definition to stress legal in, reputational and strategic out.
