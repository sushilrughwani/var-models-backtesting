## Slides
### Slide 1: The missing rule
- Capital rules did not stop liquidity runs
- Basel III added LCR and NSFR
- Both must be at least 100%
### Slide 2: Two horizons
- LCR: survive 30 days of severe stress
- NSFR: stable funding over one year
- Short-term buffer plus structural balance
### Slide 3: HQLA levels
- Level 1: cash, reserves, top sovereigns, 0% haircut
- Level 2A: 15% haircut; Level 2B: 25-50%
- Caps: Level 2 at most 40%, Level 2B at most 15%
### Slide 4: Outflow run-off rates
- Stable retail 5%, less stable retail 10%
- Operational 25%, corporate non-operational 40%
- Financial unsecured 100%, undrawn corporate facilities 10%
### Slide 5: Inflows and the cap
- Non-financial loans 50%, financial 100%
- Net outflows = outflows - min(inflows, 75% x outflows)
- Always hold HQLA for at least 25% of outflows
### Slide 6: LCR result
- HQLA 1,905; outflows 1,675; inflows 500
- Net outflows 1,175
- LCR = 162.1%
### Slide 7: NSFR structure
- ASF / RSF at least 100%
- ASF: capital 100%, stable retail 95%, corporate short-term 50%, financial short-term 0%
- RSF: cash 0%, Level 1 5%, mortgages 65%, long loans 85%, fixed assets 100%
### Slide 8: NSFR result
- ASF 9,050
- RSF 6,485
- NSFR = 139.6%
### Slide 9: How they bind
- LCR punishes short-term wholesale funding
- NSFR punishes long illiquid assets funded short
- Cost: lower margins from holding HQLA and term funding
### Slide 10: Exam traps
- Remember the 75% inflow cap
- No haircut on Level 1
- Higher ASF good, higher RSF demanding

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - LCR.** HQLA = 1,600 + 300 x 0.85 + 100 x 0.5 = 1,905. Outflows: 200 + 200 + 375 + 400 + 300 + 200 = 1,675. Inflows 300 + 200 = 500 < 1,256.25 cap. LCR = 1,905 / 1,175 = 162.1%.

**Example 2 - Inflow cap binds.** HQLA = 350 + 200 x 0.85 + 60 x 0.5 = 550. Outflows 900, inflows 720. Cap = 675, so net outflows = 225. LCR = 244.4%. Without the cap: 550 / 180 = 305.6% - overstated.

**Example 3 - NSFR.** ASF = 1,000 + 3,800 + 1,800 + 1,250 + 0 + 1,200 = 9,050. RSF = 60 + 45 + 50 + 30 + 2,600 + 2,550 + 750 + 300 + 100 = 6,485. NSFR = 139.6%.

## Questions to ask students
- A bank replaces USD 100m of retail deposits with overnight interbank funding. What happens to the LCR? (Outflows rise from about 5-10 to 100, so the LCR falls)
- Why cap inflows at 75%? (So a bank always holds a minimum HQLA buffer and does not rely on expected inflows in stress)
- What is the ASF factor on six-month funding from a pension fund? (0% - short-term funding from financial institutions)
- What RSF factor applies to cash? (0%)
- Which ratio limits maturity transformation over a one-year horizon? (The NSFR)

## Analogies
- LCR as an emergency fund covering a month without income; NSFR as making sure your mortgage is funded by a long-term loan, not a credit card.
- HQLA haircuts as a pawn shop: it lends full value on gold coins, less on jewellery, much less on electronics.
- The 75% inflow cap as not counting your entire next paycheck when your car breaks down - you need some cash on hand.

## Recording tips
- Put the factor tables on screen and leave them visible during both calculations.
- Emphasise the 75% cap with the second example where it binds; it is a favourite exam trap.
- Remind students that exact run-off rates vary slightly by jurisdiction; exam questions provide the factors.
