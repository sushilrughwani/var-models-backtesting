## Slides
### Slide 1: Climate-Related Financial Risk
- Climate changes cash flows, collateral and default rates
- Physical and transition risk
- Scenario analysis as the main tool

### Slide 2: Physical Risk
- Acute: floods, storms, wildfires, heatwaves
- Chronic: sea-level rise, higher temperatures, changing rainfall
- Hits property, agriculture, supply chains

### Slide 3: Transition Risk
- Policy and legal: carbon prices, standards, litigation
- Technology: renewables strand fossil assets
- Market and reputation: shifting demand and investor preferences

### Slide 4: The Trade-Off
- Fast transition: higher near-term transition cost, lower physical damage
- Slow transition: the reverse
- No scenario has zero climate risk

### Slide 5: Transmission Channels
- Credit: earnings and collateral; PD and LGD rise
- Market: repricing of carbon-intensive assets
- Liquidity, operational and underwriting effects

### Slide 6: Why Measurement Is Hard
- Long horizons and no relevant history
- Tipping points and non-linear damage
- Missing emissions and location data

### Slide 7: Scenario Families
- Orderly: early, gradual action
- Disorderly: late, abrupt action, highest transition risk
- Hot house world: current policies, highest physical risk

### Slide 8: Measuring Exposure
- WACI = Σ wᵢ × emissionsᵢ / revenueᵢ
- Sector and geographic heat maps
- Scenario-based PD and LGD shifts

### Slide 9: Governance
- Board oversight and clear responsibilities
- Risk appetite, credit policy, capital planning
- Disclosure of governance, strategy, risk management, metrics

### Slide 10: Traps and Takeaways
- Climate is a driver of existing risks
- Disorderly: transition; hot house: physical
- Only unabsorbed carbon costs hit margins

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Carbon-price stress.** EBITDA USD 500m, debt USD 1,900m, interest USD 95m, emissions 2m tonnes, carbon price USD 100/t, pass-through 40%.
- Gross cost = 2m × 100 = USD 200m. Absorbed = 200 × 0.6 = USD 120m (24% of EBITDA).
- Stressed EBITDA = 380.
- Leverage: 3.8x → 5.0x. Coverage: 5.26x → 4.0x.
- With no pass-through, EBITDA would fall to 300 and leverage to 6.33x.

**Example 2 - WACI.** Three holdings: weights 50%, 30%, 20%; emissions 100,000 t, 400,000 t, 50,000 t; revenues USD 500m, 200m, 250m.
- Intensities: 200, 2,000, 200 tonnes per USD million.
- WACI = 0.5 × 200 + 0.3 × 2,000 + 0.2 × 200 = 740 tonnes per USD million.
- Cutting the second holding to 10% (moving 10% to the first and 10% to the third: 60/10/30) gives WACI = 380, showing how one issuer can dominate.

## Questions to ask students
- Is a wildfire destroying a warehouse acute or chronic physical risk? (Acute.)
- Which scenario stresses transition risk most? (Disorderly: delayed then abrupt policy.)
- Why can't we simply estimate climate PDs from historical defaults? (History does not contain future climate, policy or technology paths.)
- A firm passes 100% of its carbon cost to customers. What is the direct hit to EBITDA? (Zero, though volumes may fall.)
- Name a liquidity channel for climate risk. (Disaster-driven drawdowns on credit lines, or assets becoming hard to value and sell.)

## Analogies
- Climate is a lens, not a new bucket: it changes the colour of credit, market and operational risk rather than adding a new risk type.
- Transition versus physical risk is like paying for a diet now versus paying for health problems later.
- Scenario analysis is a fire drill, not a weather forecast: it tests whether you can cope, not what will happen.

## Recording tips
- Keep a two-column table (physical / transition) on screen while classifying examples.
- Students mix up which scenario stresses which risk; repeat the disorderly/hot house mapping twice.
- Show the leverage before-and-after numbers large; that is where the credit story becomes concrete.
