## Slides
### Slide 1: Why Free Cash Flow?
- CFO ignores the investment needed to sustain the business
- Free cash flow = cash after necessary investment
- Basis of DCF valuation and payout sustainability
### Slide 2: FCFF from CFO
- FCFF = CFO + Int(1 - t) - FCInv
- Cash available to lenders and shareholders
- FCInv = capex net of asset-sale proceeds
### Slide 3: FCFF from Net Income
- FCFF = NI + NCC + Int(1 - t) - FCInv - WCInv
- NCC: depreciation, amortisation, other non-cash charges
- WCInv: increase in operating working capital
### Slide 4: The IFRS Interest Adjustment
- Interest paid in financing: do not add it back
- Interest paid in operating: add back after tax
- Read the classification note first
### Slide 5: FCFE
- FCFE = CFO - FCInv + net borrowing
- Cash available to common shareholders
- FCFE = FCFF - Int(1 - t) + net borrowing
### Slide 6: Performance Ratios
- Cash flow to revenue; cash return on assets
- Cash to operating income; cash flow per share
- Persistent cash-to-income below 1 is a quality flag
### Slide 7: Coverage Ratios
- Interest coverage = (CFO + interest paid + taxes paid) / interest paid
- Debt coverage = CFO / total debt
- Reinvestment, debt payment, dividend payment ratios
### Slide 8: Ravel Manufacturing Results
- CFO 340; FCFF 227.5; FCFE 220
- Interest coverage 9.6x; reinvestment 2.27
- Debt coverage 0.34
### Slide 9: Key Takeaways
- Know who each free cash flow belongs to
- After-tax interest, net borrowing, classification
- Coverage ratios add back the items being covered

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Ravel Manufacturing (USD millions).** NI 300; depreciation 80; working capital increase 40; interest paid 50; taxes paid 90; capex 150; net borrowing 30; tax rate 25%; debt 1,000.
- CFO = 300 + 80 - 40 = **340**.
- FCFF = 340 + 50 × 0.75 - 150 = **227.5**.
- FCFE = 340 - 150 + 30 = **220** (check: 227.5 - 37.5 + 30 = 220).
- Interest coverage = 480 / 50 = **9.6x**; reinvestment = **2.27**; debt coverage = **0.34**.

**Example 2 - Coverage pitfalls.** CFO 500, interest paid 60, taxes paid 120. Correct coverage = (500 + 60 + 120) / 60 = **11.33x**. Show 8.33 (CFO / interest only) and 9.33 (omits taxes) as the classic wrong answers.

**Example 3 - IFRS company.** CFO 400 under IFRS with interest paid of 40 classified in financing, capex 180. FCFF = 400 - 180 = **220**, with no interest add-back because interest was never deducted from CFO.

## Questions to ask students
- Who is FCFF available to? (All capital providers: lenders and shareholders.)
- Why add back interest after tax rather than before tax? (The interest tax saving stays with the company regardless of financing.)
- A company repays more debt than it issues. Does FCFE rise or fall relative to CFO - FCInv? (Falls; net borrowing is negative.)
- Why add taxes paid in the interest coverage numerator? (Interest is paid before taxes, so pre-tax cash is available to cover it.)
- What does a reinvestment ratio below 1 tell you? (CFO does not cover capex; external financing or cash reserves are needed.)

## Analogies
- FCFF is the whole pizza before anyone takes a slice; FCFE is what is left for shareholders after lenders take theirs and any new borrowing tops the pizza up.
- Capex is the cost of keeping the delivery van running: you cannot call your earnings "free" until the van is repaired.
- A dividend funded by borrowing is like paying your rent with a credit card: it works for a while.

## Recording tips
- Put FCFF and FCFE formulas side by side and colour-code the interest and borrowing terms.
- Students forget the IFRS classification adjustment; ask "where was interest classified?" before every FCFF calculation.
- Keep ratios brief; emphasise interpretation over memorising every ratio name.
