## Slides
### Slide 1: The Framework at a Glance
- Code of Ethics: broad principles
- Standards of Professional Conduct: specific obligations
- Applies to members and candidates alike
### Slide 2: Who Is Bound
- Every CFA Institute member
- Every CFA Program candidate, from enrolment
- No grace period before earning the charter
### Slide 3: The Code (1-3)
- Act with integrity, competence, diligence and respect
- Profession and clients before self
- Reasonable care and independent judgment
### Slide 4: The Code (4-6)
- Practise ethically and encourage others to do so
- Promote integrity and viability of capital markets
- Maintain and improve competence
### Slide 5: The Seven Standards
- I Professionalism; II Integrity of capital markets
- III Duties to clients; IV Duties to employers
- V Investment analysis; VI Conflicts of interest
- VII Responsibilities as member or candidate
### Slide 6: Memory Hook
- Please Invest Carefully, Everyone Inspects CFA Rules
- P-I-C-E-I-C-R maps to I through VII
### Slide 7: Sub-sections Map
- I: law, independence, misrepresentation, misconduct, competence
- II: MNPI, manipulation
- III: loyalty, fair dealing, suitability, performance, confidentiality
- IV: loyalty, additional compensation, supervisors
- V: diligence, communication, records; VI: disclosure, priority, referral fees; VII: conduct, references
### Slide 8: How Inquiries Start
- Self-disclosure on annual professional conduct statement
- Written complaints; media and public records
- Exam proctor reports; analysis of exam materials
### Slide 9: Investigation and Outcomes
- No sanction, cautionary letter, continue, or propose sanction
- Accept the sanction or reject and go to a hearing panel
### Slide 10: Sanctions
- Members: public censure, suspension, revocation
- Candidates: suspension or ban from exams
- Purpose: self-regulation protects the designation

## Whiteboard examples
1. **Sort the sub-sections.** Write ten sub-section names in random order: priority of transactions, suitability, record retention, market manipulation, additional compensation, misrepresentation, referral fees, fair dealing, competence, communication with clients. Have students assign each to I-VII. Solution: priority of transactions VI; suitability III; record retention V; market manipulation II; additional compensation IV; misrepresentation I; referral fees VI; fair dealing III; competence I; communication with clients V.
2. **Felix's disclosure.** Draw the process as a flowchart: trigger (self-disclosure) to investigation to outcome boxes (no action / cautionary letter / continue / propose sanction) to accept or hearing panel. Place Felix's case: candidate, bound; Standard IV supervisory duty; possible proposed suspension from exams; can reject and go to hearing.

## Questions to ask students
- Are candidates bound by the Standards before passing Level I? (Yes, from enrolment.)
- Which Standard contains record retention? (Standard V, Investment Analysis, Recommendations and Actions.)
- Which Standard contains referral fees? (Standard VI, Conflicts of Interest.)
- Name two ways a professional conduct inquiry can begin. (Self-disclosure, written complaint, media reports, proctor reports, exam analysis.)
- What happens if a member rejects a proposed sanction? (A hearing panel reviews the case and decides.)

## Analogies
- The Code is a national constitution; the Standards are the statutes written under it.
- The Professional Conduct Program is like a medical board: the profession polices its own to keep public trust.
- The seven Standards are rooms in a house; exam questions ask which room the problem is in before asking how to fix it.

## Recording tips
- Put the full sub-section table on screen and leave it up while you talk through each row.
- Repeat the memory hook three times during the lecture; students recall it on exam day.
- Emphasise that many ethics questions first require identifying the right Standard.
