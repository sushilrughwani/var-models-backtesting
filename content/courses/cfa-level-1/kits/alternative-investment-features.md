## Slides
### Slide 1: Alternative Investments - Features and Structures
- Private equity, private debt, real estate, infrastructure, natural resources, hedge funds
- Goal: understand how they differ and how investors get access
- Builds the vocabulary for the rest of the module

### Slide 2: Common Features of Alternatives
- Illiquid assets and restricted redemptions
- Specialist managers; returns depend on skill
- Appraisal or model-based valuations, less transparency
- High minimums, complex fees, lighter regulation

### Slide 3: Why Invest Anyway?
- Illiquidity premium for patient capital
- Access to return sources not available in public markets
- Low measured correlation with stocks and bonds
- Caveat: smoothed appraisals understate volatility and correlation

### Slide 4: Fund Investing
- Commit to a pooled vehicle; manager does everything
- Diversified across deals, little in-house expertise needed
- Highest fees, no control over individual deals

### Slide 5: Co-Investing
- LP invests extra capital directly alongside the fund in a specific deal
- Reduced or zero fees on co-invested capital
- Learning opportunity, but risk of adverse selection and concentration

### Slide 6: Direct Investing
- Investor buys assets itself, no intermediary
- Full control, lowest fees
- Needs a large expert team; least diversified

### Slide 7: The Trade-off at a Glance
- Fund -> co-investment -> direct
- Fees fall, control rises
- Required expertise rises, diversification falls

### Slide 8: Limited Partnership Structure
- GP: manages the fund, makes decisions, unlimited liability
- LPs: provide capital, passive, liability limited to commitment
- Rules set in the limited partnership agreement (LPA)

### Slide 9: Key Fund Terms
- Committed capital vs called capital (capital calls)
- Lockup period and notice period
- Side letters: special terms for particular LPs
- Waterfall and clawback

### Slide 10: Fee Drag Formula
- Net gain = G - M - p(G - M) when incentive is net of management fee
- G = gross gain, M = management fee, p = incentive rate
- Always compute net return step by step

### Slide 11: Recap
- Six features, three access methods, two partner types
- Know the direction of each trade-off
- Next lesson: fee structures and return measurement in detail

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: Fund vs co-investment.** A foundation puts USD 20m into a fund (1.5% management fee on invested capital, 20% incentive fee on gains net of the management fee) and USD 5m into a fee-free co-investment. All assets earn 18% gross.
- Fund gross gain: 20 x 0.18 = 3.60m. Management fee: 20 x 0.015 = 0.30m. Gain after management fee = 3.30m.
- Incentive fee: 0.20 x 3.30 = 0.66m. Net gain 2.64m, net return 13.2%.
- Co-investment: 5 x 0.18 = 0.90m, return 18%.
- Blended: (2.64 + 0.90) / 25 = 14.16%. Co-investing added about 0.96 percentage points versus fund-only.

**Example 2: Committed vs called.** An LP commits USD 10m. In year 1 the GP calls 3m, in year 2 another 4m. Called capital is 7m; unfunded commitment is 3m. Ask: if the management fee is 2% on committed capital, the year 2 fee is 0.2m even though only 7m has been drawn.

## Questions to ask students
- Which access method requires the most in-house expertise? (Direct investing.)
- Why might a GP offer co-investment rights? (To win and keep LPs, fill large deals without breaking fund concentration limits; it costs the GP little.)
- What is the main risk of accepting every co-investment offered? (Adverse selection and concentration.)
- Who bears unlimited liability in a limited partnership? (The general partner.)
- What does a clawback protect against? (The GP keeping incentive fees that later losses show were not earned.)
- Does low reported correlation guarantee diversification? (No, appraisal smoothing understates correlation.)

## Analogies
- Fund investing is taking a guided tour, co-investing is the tour plus a private side trip with the guide, direct investing is planning and leading your own expedition.
- The GP is the captain who steers the ship and is responsible for it; LPs are passengers who paid for tickets and can lose only the fare.
- A capital call is like a builder who bills you in stages as each part of the house is constructed, rather than taking the full price on day one.

## Recording tips
- Draw the fund/co-invest/direct spectrum as a horizontal arrow with "fees" and "control" moving in opposite directions; students remember the picture.
- Pause on "net of management fee" in the whiteboard example; many students apply 20% to the gross gain.
- Flag that the waterfall and hurdle detail is coming next lesson so students do not get stuck here.
