## Slides
### Slide 1: Standard IV at a Glance
- A Loyalty
- B Additional compensation arrangements
- C Responsibilities of supervisors
### Slide 2: Loyalty While Employed
- Act for employer's benefit in employment matters
- Do not divulge confidential information
- Independent practice: notify, describe, get consent
### Slide 3: Preparing to Leave - Allowed
- Register a company, lease an office, interview
- Do it outside working hours
- No harm to employer while still employed
### Slide 4: Preparing to Leave - Not Allowed
- Soliciting clients or prospects before you go
- Taking client lists, models or records
- Misusing confidential information
### Slide 5: After You Leave
- Contact former clients unless a non-compete prevents it
- Use memory, skills and public directories
- Records stay with the employer
### Slide 6: Whistleblowing
- Loyalty never overrides clients or market integrity
- Good-faith reporting of misconduct is not disloyalty
### Slide 7: Additional Compensation
- Anything that may conflict with employer's interest
- Written consent from all parties, before accepting
- Disclose nature, amount, duration
### Slide 8: Supervisors
- Reasonable efforts to prevent and detect violations
- Adequate compliance procedures; decline in writing if inadequate
- Investigate promptly; limit activities; do not rely on promises
### Slide 9: Case - Carmen Leaves Oakhurst
- Evening preparation
- Emails to clients while employed
- USB copy of client list; performance bonus
### Slide 10: Key Takeaways
- Prepare, do not poach
- Records are firm property
- Written consent first
- Supervisors act, not just warn

## Whiteboard examples
1. **Allowed or not timeline.** Draw a timeline with "Still employed" and "Departed." Place: register company (allowed, before), lease office (allowed, before), email clients inviting them to follow (not allowed, before), copy client list (not allowed, any time), contact former clients from memory (allowed, after, absent non-compete), use public directory (allowed, after).
2. **Additional compensation vs gift.** (a) Client offers a bonus of 1% of gains above 10% per year - needs written employer consent before accepting (IV(B)). (b) Client sends a bottle of wine after a good year - disclose to employer (I(B)), no written consent requirement. (c) Board seat at an unrelated company paying fees - written consent required because it may compete for time and create conflicts.
3. **Supervisor response.** Compliance flags that a junior trader front-ran client orders. Solution: investigate promptly, restrict the trader's activities or increase monitoring during the review, and review procedures; a warning alone is insufficient.

## Questions to ask students
- Can you lease an office for your new firm while still employed? (Yes, outside working hours.)
- Can you take a copy of a model you built at your old job? (No, it belongs to the employer.)
- Is verbal consent enough for a client-paid performance bonus? (No, written consent from the employer before accepting.)
- A supervisor finds the firm's compliance procedures inadequate. What should she do? (Decline supervisory responsibility in writing until adequate procedures are adopted.)
- Does reporting your firm's fraud to a regulator breach loyalty? (No, duties to clients and markets come first.)

## Analogies
- Leaving an employer is like moving out of a rented flat: pack your own belongings and memories, but the furniture stays.
- Additional compensation is like a referee being paid by one team's sponsor: the league must approve it in writing first.
- A supervisor is a lifeguard, not a sign that says "no running": you must watch and act, not just post rules.

## Recording tips
- Use the timeline graphic; departure scenarios are very common on the exam.
- Contrast IV(B) written consent with I(B) gift disclosure explicitly, since students confuse them.
- Remind learners that the employer never outranks the client.
