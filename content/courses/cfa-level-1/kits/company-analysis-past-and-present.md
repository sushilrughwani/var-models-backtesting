## Slides
### Slide 1: The Research Report
- Business model and industry position
- Historical performance, forecasts, valuation
- Risks and what would change the view
### Slide 2: Describe the Business Model
- Who are the customers and what do they buy?
- How is it priced and delivered?
- What does it cost to serve them?
### Slide 3: Revenue = Volume x Price
- Volume: units, stores, users, passengers
- Price: ASP, revenue per store, ARPU
- Growth compounds: (1 + gv)(1 + gp) - 1
### Slide 4: Price and Mix
- Pure price changes vs product mix shifts
- Organic growth vs acquisitions
- Each source has different sustainability
### Slide 5: Segment Contributions
- Change in segment revenue / total prior revenue
- Contributions add up to total growth
- Big slow segments can outweigh small fast ones
### Slide 6: Operating Performance
- Gross and EBIT margins
- Incremental margin = change in EBIT / change in revenue
- Strip out one-off items
### Slide 7: Operating Leverage
- % change in EBIT / % change in revenue
- High fixed costs, high leverage
- Works in reverse in downturns
### Slide 8: KPIs
- Retail: same-store sales; airlines: load factor
- Subscriptions: churn, ARPU; industrials: backlog
- Link every KPI to a line in the financials
### Slide 9: Capital Needs
- Working capital and capex required for growth
- Return on invested capital
- Growth that eats capital is worth less

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - coffee chain.** Retail: 400 stores x 1.50m to 440 x 1.56m. Wholesale: 200m to 190m. EBIT 96.0m to 113.9m.
- Retail 600.0 to 686.4 (+14.40% = 1.10 x 1.04 - 1).
- Total 800.0 to 876.4, growth 9.55%. Contributions: retail +10.80 pts, wholesale -1.25 pts.
- EBIT margin 12.00% to 13.00%. Incremental margin = 17.9/76.4 = 23.43%.
- Operating leverage = 18.65%/9.55% = 1.95.

**Example 2 - quick decomposition.** Volume +6%, price +3%.
- Revenue growth = 1.06 x 1.03 - 1 = 9.18% (not 9%).

## Questions to ask students
- Units rose 5% but revenue fell. What happened? (Average price or mix fell by more than 5%.)
- A segment grew 50% but contributed only 2 points of growth. How? (It was small - about 4% of prior revenue.)
- Revenue rises from 1,200 to 1,320 and EBIT from 150 to 186. What is the incremental margin? (36/120 = 30%.)
- Why remove a gain on an asset sale before computing margins? (It is not recurring and distorts the trend.)
- Name a KPI for a streaming service. (Subscribers, churn, ARPU.)

## Analogies
- Revenue analysis is like explaining a restaurant's takings: more tables served (volume) or a bigger bill per table (price and mix).
- Operating leverage is a see-saw with the pivot near the fixed-cost end: a small push on sales makes the profit end swing a lot.
- KPIs are the dashboard lights; the financial statements are the trip summary at the end of the journey.

## Recording tips
- Show the segment contribution bar chart (retail +10.80, wholesale -1.25) - it makes the arithmetic obvious.
- Emphasise compounding of drivers; many students add growth rates by reflex.
- Tell students this lesson feeds directly into forecasting: the same drivers become the forecast assumptions.
