## Slides
### Slide 1: Debt comes in many costumes
- Bonds payable, leases and pension promises are all fixed claims on future cash
- Analysts must find them, measure them and add them up

### Slide 2: Bond issue price
- Price = PV of coupons + PV of face, at the market rate at issue
- Coupon < market rate → discount; coupon > market rate → premium
- Coupon = market rate → par

### Slide 3: Effective interest method
- Interest expense = opening carrying amount × market rate at issue
- Amortisation = interest expense − coupon paid
- Discount bond: carrying amount rises to face value

### Slide 4: Bond example
- 5-year, 5% coupon, face 1,000,000, market rate 6%
- Price 957,876.36; Year 1 expense 57,472.58
- Year-end carrying amount 965,348.94

### Slide 5: Lessee at commencement
- Lease liability = PV of lease payments
- ROU asset = lease liability (+ initial direct costs, prepayments)
- Rate: implicit rate, else incremental borrowing rate

### Slide 6: Two expense patterns
- IFRS and US GAAP finance lease: depreciation + interest (front-loaded)
- US GAAP operating lease: single straight-line lease cost
- Total expense over the lease is identical

### Slide 7: Lease example
- 5 payments of 100,000 in arrears at 7% → liability 410,019.74
- Finance: Year 1 expense 110,705.33
- Operating (US GAAP): Year 1 expense 100,000

### Slide 8: Cash flow classification
- Finance lease / IFRS: principal in financing, interest per policy
- US GAAP operating lease: whole payment in operating
- IFRS reporters show higher CFO and EBITDA than similar US GAAP operating-lease users

### Slide 9: Defined benefit pensions
- Employer bears investment and longevity risk
- Funded status = plan assets − benefit obligation
- Deficit = net pension liability (debt-like)

### Slide 10: Ratio impact
- Capitalised leases raise debt-to-equity and lower asset turnover
- Pension deficits belong in adjusted debt
- Compare companies only after aligning these obligations

- Liability = 410,019.74

## Questions to ask students
- A bond's coupon is 4% and the market rate at issue is 5%. Premium or discount? (Discount.)
- Does a discount bond's interest expense rise or fall over time? (Rise - the carrying amount grows.)
- Under IFRS, where does a lessee report the principal portion of lease payments? (Financing activities.)
- A US GAAP company has an operating lease. Is there a lease liability on the balance sheet? (Yes, together with an ROU asset.)
- Plan assets exceed the benefit obligation. What appears on the balance sheet? (A net pension asset, subject to an asset ceiling under IFRS.)

## Analogies
- A discount bond is like buying a ticket below face value: the "missing" money is paid back gradually as extra interest expense until the bond reaches face value.
- A lease is a loan used to buy the right to use an asset: the ROU asset is what you bought, the lease liability is the loan.
- A pension deficit is a mortgage the company quietly took out on behalf of its employees.

## Recording tips
- Build the amortisation table on screen one column at a time; students learn the method from the rhythm.
- Emphasise that finance and operating leases differ in timing of expense, not total expense.
- Keep the IFRS-versus-US GAAP lessee distinction on a sticky slide; it is the most tested point here.
