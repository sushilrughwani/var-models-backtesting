## Slides
### Slide 1: Can You Explain It in Four Sentences?
- Who buys, what they get, how it is delivered, how the firm gets paid
- If you cannot explain the model, you cannot forecast it
- Business model analysis precedes every valuation

### Slide 2: Customers and Value Proposition
- Segments, geographies, channels
- Problem solved and why customers choose this firm
- Differentiation drives pricing power

### Slide 3: Delivering Value
- The value chain: activities, assets, suppliers, partners
- Make versus outsource decisions
- Where in the chain the firm earns its margin

### Slide 4: Pricing Approaches
- Value-based: price to customer value, needs differentiation
- Cost-plus: markup on cost, ignores willingness to pay
- Competitive: follow the market, typical for price takers

### Slide 5: Pricing Tactics
- Tiered pricing and price discrimination
- Bundling to raise spend and cut churn
- Penetration vs skimming; dynamic pricing and auctions

### Slide 6: Revenue Models
- Razor-and-blades: cheap base, profitable consumables
- Freemium: free base, paid upgrade
- Subscription: recurring, predictable revenue

### Slide 7: Network Effects and Platforms
- Value rises with the number of users
- Two-sided platforms charge one or both sides
- Winner-takes-most dynamics, but expensive to build

### Slide 8: Asset-Light vs Asset-Heavy
- Asset-light: franchising, marketplaces, outsourcing; high ROIC, less control
- Asset-heavy: owned plants and networks; entry barriers, high operating leverage

### Slide 9: Unit Economics
- $\text{LTV} = \frac{\text{ARPU} \times m}{c}$
- Compare with customer acquisition cost
- StreamLeaf: LTV 350, LTV/CAC 1.75, payback 14.3 months

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - StreamLeaf.** ARPU 20 dollars per month, gross margin 70%, monthly churn 4%, CAC 200.
- Monthly gross profit = 20 × 0.70 = 14.
- Lifetime = 1/0.04 = 25 months; LTV = 14 × 25 = 350.
- LTV/CAC = 350/200 = 1.75; payback = 200/14 = 14.3 months.
- Churn cut to 3%: LTV = 14/0.03 = 466.67, ratio 2.33.

**Example 2 - Razor-and-blades.** A printer sells at a loss of 30 dollars; each cartridge earns a margin of 12 dollars; a typical customer buys 6 cartridges a year for 3 years.
- Lifetime profit = −30 + 12 × 6 × 3 = −30 + 216 = 186 dollars.
- Discuss risk: third-party cartridges or lower usage destroy the model.

**Example 3 - Classify the model (no numbers).**
- A ride-hailing app taking a cut of each fare: two-sided platform, network effects, asset-light, dynamic pricing.
- A cloud storage service with free 5 GB and paid tiers: freemium plus tiered subscription.

## Questions to ask students
- A luxury watchmaker sets prices far above cost. Which pricing approach? (Value-based.)
- Game consoles sold near cost with profitable game licences: which revenue model? (Razor-and-blades.)
- Why do platforms often lose money early? (They must subsidise users to build network effects.)
- ARPU 15, gross margin 80%, churn 5%: LTV? (12/0.05 = 240.)
- What is the main risk of an asset-light model? (Dependence on partners and less control over quality and supply.)

## Analogies
- A business model is a recipe: ingredients (resources), method (value chain), the dish (value proposition) and the price on the menu (value capture).
- Network effects are a party: the more people who show up, the more people want to come.
- Churn is a leaky bucket: acquisition pours customers in, churn drains them out, and LTV is how long each drop stays.

## Recording tips
- Use well-known product categories (printers, streaming, ride-hailing) without naming brands; students recognise the patterns instantly.
- Show why gross profit, not revenue, belongs in LTV; it is the most common numeric error.
- Keep the lesson qualitative and practical; emphasise classification skills the exam tests.
