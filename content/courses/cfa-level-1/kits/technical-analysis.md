## Slides
### Slide 1: What technicians assume
- Prices set by supply and demand, rational and irrational
- Prices trend and patterns repeat because behaviour repeats
- Uses only market data: price, volume, sentiment
### Slide 2: Where it works
- Any freely traded asset, including commodities and currencies
- Weak in illiquid or manipulated markets
- In tension with weak-form efficiency
### Slide 3: Charts
- Line, bar (OHLC), candlestick
- Volume beneath price
- Log scale for long histories
### Slide 4: Trend, support, resistance
- Uptrend: higher highs and higher lows
- Support: buyers step in; resistance: sellers step in
- Change in polarity: broken resistance becomes support
### Slide 5: Patterns
- Reversal: head and shoulders, inverse H&S, double/triple tops and bottoms
- Continuation: triangles, rectangles, flags, pennants
### Slide 6: Head-and-shoulders target
- Top: neckline − (head − neckline)
- Inverse: neckline + (neckline − head)
- Example: head 80, neckline 62 → target 44
### Slide 7: Moving averages
- SMA of last n closes
- Golden cross: short above long (bullish); death cross: short below long
- Bollinger bands: MA ± 2σ
### Slide 8: Oscillators
- $RSI = 100 - 100/(1+RS)$, RS = avg gain / avg loss
- Above 70 overbought, below 30 oversold
- ROC crossing zero; divergence warns of weakening trend
### Slide 9: Sentiment and flows
- Contrarian reading: high put/call or VIX can be bullish
- Margin debt rising = bullish sentiment
- TRIN above 1 = selling pressure
### Slide 10: Cycles and intermarket
- Kondratieff (~54 years), decennial pattern
- Elliott: five-wave impulse, three-wave correction, Fibonacci ratios
- Relative-strength ratios across asset classes
### Slide 11: Recap
- Assumptions, trends and polarity, pattern targets, indicators, contrarian sentiment

## Whiteboard examples
1. **Head-and-shoulders target.** Left shoulder 72, head 80, right shoulder 71, neckline 62. Height = 18, target = 62 − 18 = 44. Then an inverse pattern: head 30, neckline 42, target = 42 + 12 = 54.
2. **RSI.** Over 14 days, gains sum to 21 and losses to 9. Average gain 1.50, average loss 0.643, RS = 2.333, RSI = 100 − 100/3.333 = 70.0 (overbought threshold).
3. **Moving average and TRIN.** Closes 50, 51, 53, 52, 55, 56: 5-day SMA 52.2 then 53.4; ROC(5) on day 6 = 12.0. TRIN with 1,800 advancers, 1,200 decliners, advancing volume 600 million, declining volume 900 million: (1,800/1,200)/(600/900) = 1.5/0.667 = 2.25, signalling heavy selling pressure.

## Questions to ask students
- Why can technical analysis be applied to currencies but fundamental DCF valuation struggles there? (Currencies have no cash flows to discount; technical analysis needs only price data.)
- A stock breaks above resistance at 40 and later falls back to 40. What does a technician expect? (Support at 40 - change in polarity.)
- Is a very high put/call ratio bullish or bearish for a contrarian? (Bullish - pessimism may be overdone.)
- The price hits a new high but RSI makes a lower high. What does that suggest? (Bearish divergence - weakening momentum.)
- What does a TRIN below 1 indicate? (Buying pressure - more volume going into advancing stocks.)

## Analogies
- Support and resistance are a floor and a ceiling; smash through the ceiling and it becomes the floor of the next storey.
- RSI is a car's rev counter: high revs (above 70) can be sustained only briefly before the driver eases off.
- Contrarian sentiment is the party rule: when everyone is already dancing, there is no one left to join the dance floor.

## Recording tips
- Draw the head-and-shoulders on screen and physically measure the head height down from the neckline; students retain the visual.
- Do the inverse pattern immediately after the top to prevent the sign mistake.
- Keep a "contrarian or not" column on the sentiment slide; this is where most marks are lost.
