## Slides
### Slide 1: Why Performance Standards?
- Same word "return," wildly different meanings
- Investors need fair comparison across firms
- GIPS: voluntary, global, ethical principles
### Slide 2: The Tricks GIPS Stops
- Cherry-picking accounts or periods
- Survivorship bias from dropping losers
- Simulated results shown as real
### Slide 3: Who Benefits
- Prospective clients: comparable, honest data
- Firms: credibility and a global passport
- Industry: trust
### Slide 4: Who Can Claim Compliance
- Firms (and asset owners) only
- Not individuals, products or software
- Voluntary, but expected by many institutions
### Slide 5: All or Nothing, Firm-Wide
- Firm defines itself and applies GIPS throughout
- No "partial compliance" claims
- Every applicable requirement must be met
### Slide 6: What Is a Composite?
- One or more portfolios with a similar mandate or strategy
- Defined by strategy, not results
- Examples: Large-Cap Growth, Short-Duration IG Bonds
### Slide 7: Composite Inclusion Rules
- Every fee-paying discretionary portfolio in at least one composite
- Closed portfolios stay in history
- Non-discretionary portfolios excluded
- No switching portfolios to flatter results
### Slide 8: Composite Return
- Weight each portfolio by beginning assets
- $R_C = \sum w_i R_i$
- Not a simple average
### Slide 9: Verification
- Independent third party
- Firm-wide, not composite-specific
- Optional but recommended
### Slide 10: Key Takeaways
- Fair, comparable, complete
- Firms only, all or nothing
- Composites capture all eligible portfolios
- Asset-weight the returns

## Whiteboard examples
1. **Composite return.** Portfolios: P1 USD 50m at 6%, P2 USD 30m at 9%, P3 USD 20m at -2%. Total 100. Weights 0.50, 0.30, 0.20. Weighted: 3.0% + 2.7% - 0.4% = **5.3%**. Equal-weighted average: (6 + 9 - 2)/3 = 4.33%. Cherry-picked (drop P3): (50 × 6 + 30 × 9)/80 = 7.125%, about 7.13%. Ask students which number a non-compliant firm would be tempted to advertise (7.13%).
2. **Compliant claim or not?** (a) "Our Large-Cap fund complies with GIPS" - incorrect, only firms comply. (b) "Harborview claims compliance with GIPS except for its real estate composite" - incorrect, no partial compliance. (c) "Harborview claims compliance with GIPS and has been independently verified" - correct form of claim. (d) "Jane Doe, portfolio manager, is GIPS-compliant" - incorrect.

## Questions to ask students
- Can an individual portfolio manager claim GIPS compliance? (No, only firms.)
- Must a firm include a fee-paying discretionary account that performed badly? (Yes, in at least one composite.)
- Is verification required to claim compliance? (No, it is optional but recommended.)
- Why weight by assets rather than averaging returns? (So the composite reflects how the firm's money actually performed, with larger portfolios counting more.)
- What happens to a portfolio's history when the client leaves? (It stays in the composite for the periods it was managed.)

## Analogies
- A composite is a school's exam results: you must report every student who sat the exam, not just the top of the class.
- Survivorship bias is judging a sport by the athletes still competing and forgetting everyone who retired injured.
- GIPS is a common measuring tape: you can compare heights only if everyone uses the same one.

## Recording tips
- Do the composite calculation live; the side-by-side of 5.3%, 4.33% and 7.13% makes the point unforgettable.
- Tie back to Standard III performance presentation so students see GIPS as its practical implementation.
- Keep the scope introductory; avoid detailed calculation methodology beyond asset weighting.
