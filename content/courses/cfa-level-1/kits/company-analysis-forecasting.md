## Slides
### Slide 1: Forecasting Is Driver-Based
- A few explicit assumptions, each tied to the business model
- Avoid mechanically extending past growth
- Every number should have a reason
### Slide 2: Top-Down Revenue
- Market size x (1 + market growth) x market share
- Or growth relative to nominal GDP
- Good sanity check on ambition
### Slide 3: Bottom-Up Revenue
- Stores x sales per store; users x ARPU
- Capacity x utilisation x price
- Hybrid: cross-check bottom-up against implied share
### Slide 4: Forecasting Costs
- Variable costs: % of revenue
- Fixed costs: currency amounts that step up
- The mix drives operating leverage
### Slide 5: Working Capital
- AR = revenue x DSO / 365
- Inventory and AP use COGS x days / 365
- Increase in NWC is a cash outflow
### Slide 6: Capital Expenditure
- Maintenance capex ~ depreciation
- Growth capex = capital intensity x increase in revenue
- Fast growth needs heavy reinvestment
### Slide 7: The Forecast Horizon
- Long enough to reach a steady state
- Longer for young, cyclical or restructuring firms
- Then hand over to a terminal value
### Slide 8: Scenarios and Sensitivities
- Base, bull and bear cases
- Flex the two or three key drivers
- Beware anchoring and overconfidence
### Slide 9: Exam Traps
- DIO and DPO use COGS, not revenue
- Cash flow uses the change in NWC
- Do not forget growth capex

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - full mini forecast.** Market 5,000 growing 6%; share 12% to 13%; COGS 58%; SG&A 90 + 8% of revenue; DSO 45, DIO 60, DPO 40; depreciation 30; growth capex 0.25 per unit of extra revenue.
- Revenue 600 to 689.0. COGS 399.62; SG&A 145.12; EBIT 144.26 (20.94%).
- AR 84.95; inventory 65.69; AP 43.79; NWC 106.84 vs 93.04 this year; increase 13.80.
- Capex = 30 + 0.25 x 89 = 52.25.

**Example 2 - quick checks.** Market 2,000 growing 5%, share 15% to 16%: revenue = 2,000 x 1.05 x 0.16 = 336.0. Revenue 730 with DSO 50: AR = 730 x 50/365 = 100.0.

## Questions to ask students
- Which approach starts from GDP or industry size? (Top-down.)
- If all costs are forecast as a fixed percentage of sales, what is the implied operating leverage? (One - EBIT grows exactly with sales.)
- Why does a fast-growing company often have weak free cash flow? (Growth capex and rising working capital absorb cash.)
- Depreciation 40, capital intensity 0.30, revenue rising from 800 to 900: forecast capex? (40 + 30 = 70.)
- Would you use a longer or shorter explicit horizon for a start-up than for a utility? (Longer.)

## Analogies
- Top-down is estimating your slice by measuring the whole pizza; bottom-up is counting the toppings on your own slice.
- Working capital is the fuel tank: drive faster (grow revenue) and you need a bigger tank, which costs money to fill.
- The forecast horizon is like following a rocket until it reaches orbit; after that a simple formula (terminal value) describes the path.

## Recording tips
- Build Example 1 in a simple spreadsheet on screen, row by row - students see how each assumption flows to cash.
- Say "COGS, not revenue" out loud every time you compute inventory or payables.
