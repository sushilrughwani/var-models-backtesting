## Slides
### Slide 1: Profitable but Broke
- Firms pay suppliers before customers pay them
- Cash is trapped in inventory and receivables
- Working capital management decides how much, how long and how financed

### Slide 2: The Days Measures
- DIO = Inventory / (COGS/365)
- DSO = Receivables / (Revenue/365)
- DPO = Payables / (COGS/365), purchases ideal
- Match each balance to the right flow

### Slide 3: Cash Conversion Cycle
- $\text{CCC} = \text{DIO} + \text{DSO} - \text{DPO}$
- Days cash is tied up in operations
- Shorter usually better, but not at the cost of sales or supplier relations

### Slide 4: Liquidity Ratios
- Current = CA / CL
- Quick = (cash + ST investments + receivables) / CL
- Cash = (cash + ST investments) / CL
- Each ratio removes less liquid assets

### Slide 5: Sources of Liquidity
- Primary: cash, ST investments, operating cash flow, trade credit, bank lines
- Secondary: renegotiate debt, sell assets, bankruptcy protection
- Secondary sources change the firm's financial or operating position

### Slide 6: Drags and Pulls
- Drag: inflows slow down (late customers, obsolete stock)
- Pull: outflows speed up (suppliers tighten terms, lines cut)
- Both shrink the liquidity cushion

### Slide 7: Trade Credit Is Not Free
- "2/10, net 30": 2% off if paid in 10 days, else full amount by day 30
- Cost = $(1 + \frac{d}{1-d})^{365/(N-D)} - 1$
- Compare with bank borrowing rate

### Slide 8: Short-Term Financing Menu
- Bank lines: uncommitted, committed (fee), revolving
- Secured loans and factoring of receivables
- Commercial paper: cheap, for strong credits, needs backup lines

### Slide 9: Orca Supplies Results
- DSO 40, DIO 50, DPO 30, CCC 60 days
- Current 2.375, quick 1.50, cash 0.50
- Skipping the discount costs 44.59% a year

### Slide 10: Working Capital Approaches
- Conservative: high current assets, long-term funding, safe and costly
- Aggressive: lean current assets, short-term funding, cheap and risky
- Moderate: match funding maturity to asset life

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Orca Supplies.** Revenue 7,300,000; COGS 5,110,000; receivables 800,000; inventory 700,000; payables 420,000; cash 250,000; ST investments 150,000; other current liabilities 380,000.
- Revenue/day 20,000; COGS/day 14,000.
- DSO 40, DIO 50, DPO 30, CCC = 50 + 40 - 30 = 60 days.
- CA 1,900,000; CL 800,000. Current 2.375; quick 1,200,000/800,000 = 1.50; cash 400,000/800,000 = 0.50.

**Example 2 - Cost of trade credit.** Terms 2/10, net 30.
- d/(1 - d) = 0.02/0.98 = 0.020408; extra days = 20; periods per year = 18.25.
- Effective cost = 1.020408^18.25 - 1 = 44.59%.
- Simple (non-compounded) figure for comparison: 0.020408 × 18.25 = 37.24%.
- Decision: if the bank line costs 8%, borrow and take the discount.

**Example 3 - What if Orca stretches payables to 45 days?**
- New CCC = 50 + 40 - 45 = 45 days, freeing 15 days of COGS = 15 × 14,000 = 210,000 dollars of cash.
- Discuss the cost: lost discounts and supplier goodwill.

## Questions to ask students
- Why does DIO use COGS rather than revenue? (Inventory is carried at cost, so divide by a cost-based flow.)
- DIO 52, DSO 38, DPO 41: what is the CCC? (49 days.)
- Is selling a factory to meet payroll a primary or secondary source? (Secondary.)
- A key customer starts paying in 70 days instead of 40: drag or pull? (Drag.)
- What is the effective annual cost of 1/15, net 45? (About 13.01%.)
- Which approach relies most on short-term debt? (Aggressive.)

## Analogies
- The CCC is a relay race where cash hands off the baton: inventory runner, receivables runner, and the supplier who lets you start before paying for the shoes.
- Forgoing a trade discount is like paying a 2% fee to borrow money for 20 days, which is a loan shark rate once annualised.
- Primary liquidity is your wallet and salary; secondary liquidity is selling your car to pay rent.

## Recording tips
- Show the timeline diagram (purchase, sale, collection, payment) and label DIO, DSO, DPO on it before any formula.
- Emphasise the 20 days (N - D) in the trade-credit exponent; most errors come from using 30.
- Keep financing instruments to a quick menu; the exam focus is on the CCC, ratios and the trade-credit cost.
