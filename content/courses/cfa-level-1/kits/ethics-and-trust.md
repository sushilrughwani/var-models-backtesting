## Slides
### Slide 1: Ethics and Trust
- Why this topic is first in the curriculum and carries real exam weight
- Ethics questions are scenario-based: judgment, not memorisation
- Goal today: a mindset and a process
### Slide 2: The Information Gap
- Professionals know more than clients about fees, risks and conflicts
- Clients participate only if they trust the gap will not be exploited
- Trust lost means higher cost of capital and smaller markets
### Slide 3: What Makes a Profession
- Specialised knowledge
- Service to others ahead of self-interest
- A shared code of conduct
- Accountability through self-regulation
### Slide 4: Ethics vs Law
- Law is the floor; ethics is often higher
- Legal but unethical: steering clients to costly products
- Illegal but arguably ethical: whistleblowing despite a gag clause
- Laws lag new products and technology
### Slide 5: Why Good People Slip
- Overconfidence in one's own morality
- Situational influences: bonuses, bosses, peer culture
- Short-term focus over long-term reputation
### Slide 6: The Decision Framework
- Identify: facts, stakeholders, duties, conflicts
- Consider: biases, alternatives, guidance
- Decide and act
- Reflect
### Slide 7: Quick Tests
- Front-page test: would you be comfortable in the news?
- Client-watching test: would your client approve?
- Neither replaces the framework, but both are fast filters
### Slide 8: Case - The Inflated Forecast
- Analyst asked to raise forecast to support trading desk position
- Bonus depends on the person asking
- Walk through the four steps
### Slide 9: Key Takeaways
- Trust is the product
- Ethics exceeds law
- Recognise pressures before they act on you
- Use a repeatable process

## Whiteboard examples
1. **The 2x2 grid.** Draw axes "Legal / Illegal" and "Ethical / Unethical." Ask students to place: (a) charging a legally disclosed but excessive fee to an elderly client who did not understand it - legal but unethical; (b) an employee leaking evidence of accounting fraud to a regulator in breach of an NDA - illegal (contractually) but ethical; (c) giving honest, well-researched advice - legal and ethical; (d) insider trading - illegal and unethical.
2. **Priya's case with the framework.** Fill four boxes on the board. Identify: forecast change for non-analytical reasons; stakeholders are clients, firm, desk, Priya, market; duty of independent research; conflict with desk position. Consider: bonus pressure; options to comply, refuse, or refuse and escalate. Decide: refuse, document, escalate to compliance. Reflect: insist that future requests are in writing. Final answer: publish the neutral view and escalate.

## Questions to ask students
- Why would clients demand higher returns if they distrusted professionals? (They price in the risk of being exploited, which raises the cost of capital.)
- Give an example of something legal but unethical in investment management. (E.g., recommending a high-commission product that is permitted but clearly not in the client's interest.)
- Which bias is captured by "I am a good person, so my choice is fine"? (Overconfidence.)
- What is the first step of the framework? (Identify facts, stakeholders, duties and conflicts.)
- Why is reflection included as a step? (To learn from the outcome and improve future decisions.)

## Analogies
- Trust is like a bridge's load rating: invisible on a normal day, catastrophic when it fails, and very slow to rebuild.
- The law is the speed limit; ethics is driving safely in fog even when the posted limit is higher.
- Situational pressure is a current in the sea: you do not feel it pulling you until you look back at the shore.

## Recording tips
- Keep this lesson conversational and story-driven; it is a free preview and sets the tone for the course.
- Show the four-step framework as a persistent on-screen graphic during the case study.
- Stress that the exam rewards recognising pressure and conflicts, not finding loopholes.
