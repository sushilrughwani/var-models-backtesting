## Slides
### Slide 1: Why Governance Fails
- Owners delegate to managers who have different goals and better information
- Governance is the set of controls that narrows that gap
- Today: conflicts, mechanisms, voting maths, risks, ESG

### Slide 2: Principal-Agent Problem
- Principals: shareholders; agents: directors and managers
- Symptoms: shirking, empire building, entrenchment, misaligned pay, information asymmetry
- Also: controlling vs minority holders, shareholders vs creditors

### Slide 3: Board Structure
- One-tier: executives and non-executives on one board
- Two-tier: supervisory board oversees management board
- Best practice: independent majority, separate CEO and chair, annual elections
- Staggered boards slow change and can entrench management

### Slide 4: Board Committees
- Audit: financial reporting, internal controls, external auditor
- Remuneration: executive pay design
- Nomination: director selection and succession
- Risk and ESG committees increasingly common; independents should dominate

### Slide 5: Shareholder Tools
- Vote on directors, auditors, major deals, say on pay
- Proxy voting and shareholder proposals
- Activism, exit (selling) and the takeover market

### Slide 6: Straight vs Cumulative Voting
- Straight: votes per seat, majority wins every seat
- Cumulative: pool shares × seats on chosen candidates
- Shares to guarantee n seats: $\frac{nS}{D+1} + 1$

### Slide 7: Other Mechanisms
- Creditors: covenants, collateral
- Employees: contracts, labour law
- External: auditors, regulators, media, market for corporate control

### Slide 8: Risks of Weak Governance
- Fraud, fines, lawsuits, reputational damage
- Value-destroying acquisitions and excessive risk-taking
- Weak controls and misstated accounts; higher cost of capital

### Slide 9: Benefits of Strong Governance
- Better operational efficiency and control
- Higher-quality financial reporting
- Lower agency risk, so lower cost of debt and equity

### Slide 10: ESG in Investment Analysis
- E: emissions, climate risk, stranded assets
- S: labour, product safety, data privacy
- G: board quality, pay, shareholder rights
- Focus on financially material factors

### Slide 11: ESG Approaches
- Negative screening, ESG integration, thematic, impact investing
- Challenges: inconsistent data and ratings, greenwashing

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Harbor Corp.** 1,000,000 shares, 5 seats, Priya owns 300,000.
- Straight voting: 700,000 vs 300,000 on every seat, Priya wins 0.
- Cumulative: Priya has 1,500,000 votes out of 5,000,000.
- One seat: 1,000,000/6 = 166,666.67, round down to 166,666, add 1 = 166,667 shares.
- Two seats: 2,000,000/6 = 333,333.33, round down to 333,333, add 1 = 333,334 shares.
- Priya guarantees exactly 1 seat.

**Example 2 - How many shares to win a majority of a 7-seat board under cumulative voting?** 2,400,000 shares outstanding; majority = 4 seats.
- 4 × 2,400,000 / 8 = 1,200,000, plus 1 = 1,200,001 shares.
- Compare: under straight voting the same 1,200,001 shares (a bare majority) win all 7 seats.
- Point: cumulative voting changes the minority's position, not the majority threshold for control.

## Questions to ask students
- Why does information asymmetry make agency problems worse? (Owners cannot easily observe managers' effort or the true state of the business.)
- Which committee oversees the external auditor? (The audit committee.)
- Does a staggered board help or hurt shareholders seeking change? (Hurts; only a fraction of seats is up each year.)
- With 600,000 shares, 3 seats, how many shares guarantee 1 seat under cumulative voting? (600,000/4 + 1 = 150,001.)
- Name an ESG factor that could directly hit cash flows. (Carbon taxes, pollution fines, product recalls, stranded reserves.)
- Is excluding tobacco stocks the same as ESG integration? (No; that is negative screening.)

## Analogies
- Principal-agent as hiring a house-sitter: they live in your house, know more than you about what happens there and may not water the plants.
- Cumulative voting as putting all your raffle tickets in one draw: spread thin you win nothing, concentrated you can win one prize.
- ESG materiality as a weather forecast for cash flows: you do not care about every cloud, only the storms that can flood the business.

## Recording tips
- Do the straight-voting outcome first so students feel the minority's powerlessness, then reveal cumulative voting.
- Write "D + 1" in a different colour; dividing by D is the most common error.
- For ESG, keep it anchored in valuation: every factor should be linked to cash flows, risk or discount rates.
