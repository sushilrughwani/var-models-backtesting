## Slides
### Slide 1: Why Standard II Exists
- Prices should reflect information everyone can access
- Volume should reflect genuine supply and demand
- Two parts: MNPI and manipulation
### Slide 2: The Two-Part Test
- Material: likely to move price or matter to a reasonable investor
- Nonpublic: not yet disseminated to the market
- Both must be true
### Slide 3: Reliability and Materiality
- Specific facts from insiders: likely material
- Vague rumours from unreliable sources: less likely
- Examples: earnings, takeovers, dividend changes, approvals
### Slide 4: Selective Disclosure and Tipping
- Private analyst calls do not make information public
- Causing others to act is a violation
- Response: do not act, inform compliance, encourage disclosure
### Slide 5: The Mosaic Theory
- Public info + nonmaterial nonpublic info = legitimate conclusion
- Even if the conclusion itself would be material
- Keep records of your inputs
### Slide 6: Firm Controls
- Information barriers between deals and research or trading
- Watch lists and restricted lists
- Pre-clearance of personal trades
### Slide 7: Information-Based Manipulation
- False rumours to move prices
- Pump and dump schemes
### Slide 8: Transaction-Based Manipulation
- Wash-style trades to fake volume
- Cornering supply to squeeze shorts
- Intent to mislead is the test
### Slide 9: Case - Arcturus Lines
- Port statistics, former employee opinion, dividend tip
- Classify each input
### Slide 10: Key Takeaways
- Material AND nonpublic
- Mosaic is fine; one MNPI tile is not
- Manipulation requires intent

## Whiteboard examples
1. **Classification table.** Columns: Input, Material?, Public?, Usable? Rows: (a) published quarterly sales - yes, yes, usable; (b) a supplier says industry demand is soft - no, no, usable (nonmaterial); (c) CEO tells you privately of a pending merger - yes, no, NOT usable; (d) a rumour on an anonymous forum about a product delay - uncertain reliability, public posting but unverified; treat with caution, verify before relying on it.
2. **Nadia's case.** Port statistics (public), former manager (nonmaterial nonpublic), dividend cut (MNPI). Sell recommendation from items 1 and 2 is legitimate; she must not act on item 3, should inform compliance and encourage disclosure. Final answer: mosaic acceptable, dividend information unusable.
3. **Manipulation or not?** (a) Selling a losing position in December for a tax loss - not manipulation; (b) buying and selling the same thinly traded stock between two of your own accounts to show volume - manipulation; (c) posting that a company is about to be acquired when you know it is false - manipulation.

## Questions to ask students
- Is information shared on an invitation-only analyst call public? (No, it remains nonpublic until broadly disseminated.)
- If you do not trade but tell your brother about an unannounced takeover, have you violated the standard? (Yes, by causing others to act.)
- Can an analyst's conclusion be material and still be legitimate? (Yes, if built only from public and nonmaterial nonpublic information.)
- What is the deciding factor between aggressive trading and manipulation? (Intent to mislead market participants.)
- What should you do on receiving MNPI? (Not act, inform compliance, encourage the issuer to disclose.)

## Analogies
- The mosaic theory is a jigsaw puzzle: you may complete the picture from many small pieces, but not by stealing the finished photo from the box lid.
- Material nonpublic information is a radioactive package: once you touch it, you seal it, report it and do not use it.
- Manipulation is like a shop hiring fake customers to form a queue outside: the queue itself is the lie.

## Recording tips
- Use the classification table on screen; students need to practise labelling inputs quickly.
- Bring in modern alternative-data examples (satellite images, card-spend data) to show the mosaic theory is current.
- Stress intent repeatedly in the manipulation segment, as distractor answers often omit it.
