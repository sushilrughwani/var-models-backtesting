## Slides
### Slide 1: Why the FX Market Exists
- Pay for imported goods and services
- Move capital into foreign assets
- Hedge currency exposure

### Slide 2: Who Trades
- Sell side: dealer banks quoting two-way prices
- Buy side: corporations, real money investors, leveraged funds
- Governments, central banks, sovereign wealth funds, retail

### Slide 3: Products
- Spot: settles in two business days for most pairs
- Forward: fixed rate for a future date
- FX swap: spot plus offsetting forward, the largest product by volume
- Futures and options

### Slide 4: Quote Notation
- S(A/B) = units of A per one unit of B
- USD/EUR = 1.10: one euro costs 1.10 dollars
- Quote rises: base currency B appreciates

### Slide 5: Real Exchange Rate
- R(DC/FC) = S(DC/FC) × P(FC) / P(DC)
- Higher R: foreign goods dearer, domestic exports more competitive
- Inflation differentials can offset nominal moves

### Slide 6: Exchange Rate Regimes (Rigid)
- No separate legal tender: dollarisation, monetary union
- Currency board: legally backed peg
- Fixed peg within a narrow band; target zone with a wider band

### Slide 7: Exchange Rate Regimes (Flexible)
- Crawling peg: small, regular adjustments
- Managed float: intervention without a target
- Independent float: market-determined

### Slide 8: Capital Flows
- FDI: control, long-term, hard to reverse
- Portfolio flows: liquid, can reverse fast (hot money)
- Sudden stops hurt emerging markets

### Slide 9: Capital Controls and the Trinity
- Reasons: keep savings home, stabilise rate, protect policy independence
- Cost: less efficient capital allocation
- Pick two: fixed rate, free capital, independent monetary policy

### Slide 10: Exam Traps
- Read which currency is the base
- Foreign price level goes in the numerator
- Pegs plus open capital mean no independent rates

## Whiteboard examples
1. **Real exchange rate (USD domestic).** USD/EUR moves 1.10 to 1.12; US inflation 3%, euro inflation 1%.
   - Nominal: 1.12/1.10 − 1 = +1.82% (euro up)
   - Real end = 1.12 × 101/103 = 1.0983; real start = 1.1000
   - Real change = −0.16%: the dollar appreciated slightly in real terms
2. **Real exchange rate (INR domestic).** INR/USD moves 83.00 to 85.00; Indian inflation 5%, US inflation 3%.
   - Nominal: 85/83 − 1 = +2.41% (dollar up, rupee down)
   - Real change = (85/83) × (1.03/1.05) − 1 = +0.46%
   - The rupee depreciated only 0.46% in real terms, so the competitiveness gain is small.

## Questions to ask students
- GBP/USD falls from 0.80 to 0.78. Which currency appreciated? (The pound; fewer pounds are needed to buy one dollar, so the dollar depreciated.)
- Which FX product has the largest daily volume? (FX swaps.)
- A country fixes its currency and allows free capital flows. What must it give up? (Independent monetary policy.)
- Which capital flow is harder to reverse in a crisis? (FDI.)
- Under which regime is the domestic currency fully backed by reserves by law? (A currency board.)

## Analogies
- The real exchange rate is the "Big Mac test": what matters is how many of your burgers it takes to buy one of theirs, not the sticker price in each currency.
- The impossible trinity is a three-legged stool where you can only ever stand on two legs.
- FDI is buying a house in a city; portfolio flows are booking a hotel room you can cancel tonight.
