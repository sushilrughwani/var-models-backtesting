## Slides
### Slide 1: Private Capital
- Financing companies outside public markets
- Private equity buys ownership; private debt lends
- Usually accessed through long-lived limited partnerships

### Slide 2: Leveraged Buyouts
- Mature, cash-generative targets financed with heavy debt
- Debt repaid from the target's own cash flows
- MBO: existing managers buy; MBI: outside managers come in
- Value from operations, strategy and capital structure

### Slide 3: Venture Capital Stages
- Formative: angel, seed, early stage
- Later stage: expansion of a company with sales
- Mezzanine stage: pre-IPO financing
- Most deals fail; a few big winners drive returns

### Slide 4: Growth Equity
- Minority stakes in established, growing firms
- Little or no leverage
- Owners keep control

### Slide 5: Venture Valuation
- Post-money = investment / ownership fraction
- Pre-money = post-money - investment
- Example: 5m for 20% -> post 25m, pre 20m

### Slide 6: Exit Routes
- Trade sale: fast, private, synergy premium
- IPO: highest potential price, slow and costly
- Secondary sale to another sponsor; recapitalisation (partial)
- Write-off when the deal fails

### Slide 7: Private Debt Types
- Direct lending: senior, secured, often floating rate
- Mezzanine: subordinated, warrants or conversion features
- Venture debt, distressed debt, unitranche

### Slide 8: LBO Return Formulas
- Equity = EV - debt
- MOIC = exit equity / entry equity
- IRR = MOIC^(1/T) - 1 with no interim cash flows

### Slide 9: Where LBO Returns Come From
- EBITDA growth x multiple
- Multiple expansion x exit EBITDA
- Debt paydown

### Slide 10: Recap
- Know the three PE strategies and the VC stage names
- Post-money first, then pre-money
- Always move from EV to equity by subtracting debt

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: LBO.** EBITDA 50m bought at 10x with 300m debt. Exit after 5 years: EBITDA 70m, 10x, debt 180m.
- Entry EV 500m, entry equity 200m. Exit EV 700m, exit equity 520m.
- MOIC = 520/200 = 2.6x. IRR = 2.6^(0.2) - 1 = 21.06%.
- Attribution: EBITDA growth 20 x 10 = 200m; multiple change 0; debt paydown 120m. Total 320m = 520 - 200.

**Example 2: VC round.** A fund invests 5m for 20%. Post-money = 25m; pre-money = 20m. Follow-up: if a later investor pays 10m for 20% of the enlarged company, post-money is 50m and pre-money is 40m, so the first investor's stake (now 16%) is worth 8m.

## Questions to ask students
- Why can an LBO generate equity returns even if the company is sold at the same multiple? (EBITDA growth and debt paydown both increase equity value.)
- What is the difference between the mezzanine stage and mezzanine debt? (The stage is pre-IPO venture financing; mezzanine debt is subordinated debt with equity features.)
- Which exit typically achieves the highest valuation but takes longest? (IPO.)
- If a VC pays 6m for 30%, what is the post-money value? (20m; pre-money 14m.)
- Why do private debt investors earn a premium over public bonds? (Illiquidity, complexity and limited ability to exit.)

## Analogies
- An LBO is like buying a rental house with a big mortgage: the tenant's rent pays down the loan, and your equity grows even if house prices do not move.
- VC is a talent scout signing many young players knowing only one or two will become stars who pay for the rest.
- The capital structure is a queue at a lifeboat: senior lenders board first, mezzanine next, equity last.

## Recording tips
- Draw the LBO as two stacked bars (debt and equity) at entry and exit; the shrinking debt bar makes deleveraging visible.
- Slow down on the post-money calculation; students often subtract before dividing.
- Explicitly contrast "mezzanine stage" and "mezzanine debt" on screen; it is a favourite exam trap.
