## Slides
### Slide 1: Pricing Power Drives Margins
- Margins depend on how much a firm can set its own price
- Market structure is the starting point for any industry forecast
- Four structures from no power to full power

### Slide 2: The Four Structures at a Glance
- Perfect competition: many sellers, identical product, no barriers
- Monopolistic competition: many sellers, differentiated product, low barriers
- Oligopoly: few sellers, high barriers, interdependence
- Monopoly: one seller, no close substitutes, very high barriers

### Slide 3: One Rule for All - MR = MC
- Produce where the last unit adds as much revenue as cost
- Price taker: P = MR, so produce where P = MC
- Price searcher: MR below P, read price from the demand curve

### Slide 4: Perfect Competition
- Horizontal firm demand at market price
- Short-run profits or losses possible
- Long run: entry and exit drive P to minimum ATC, zero economic profit

### Slide 5: Monopolistic Competition
- Downward-sloping demand from product differentiation
- Advertising and branding matter
- Long run: zero economic profit, but P > MC and excess capacity

### Slide 6: Oligopoly Models
- Kinked demand: rivals match cuts, ignore rises, prices sticky
- Cournot: compete on quantity; Stackelberg: leader moves first
- Nash equilibrium: no one gains by deviating alone
- Cartels raise profit but invite cheating

### Slide 7: Monopoly
- Faces market demand; MR = a - 2bQ for P = a - bQ
- Restricts output, raises price
- Deadweight loss versus the competitive outcome

### Slide 8: Breakeven and Shutdown
- Breakeven: P = ATC
- Short run: operate if P is at least AVC
- Short run: shut down if P < AVC
- Long run: exit if P < ATC

### Slide 9: Measuring Concentration
- N-firm concentration ratio: sum of top N shares
- HHI: sum of squared shares (percent), monopoly = 10,000
- HHI reacts to mergers among large firms; CR may not
- Neither measures barriers to entry

### Slide 10: Exam Traps
- Monopolist does not set P = MC
- Short-run shutdown uses AVC, not ATC
- Monopolistic competition: zero long-run economic profit
- Keep HHI units consistent

## Whiteboard examples
1. **Monopoly.** Demand $P = 100 - 2Q$, MC = 20 constant, no fixed cost.
   - MR = 100 - 4Q; set equal to 20, Q = 20
   - P = 100 - 40 = 60; profit = 40 × 20 = 800
   - Competitive output: 100 - 2Q = 20 gives Q = 40
   - Deadweight loss = 0.5 × 20 × 40 = 400
2. **Shutdown.** Price 12, ATC 15, AVC 10, output 1,000.
   - Operate: loss = (12 - 15) × 1,000 = -3,000
   - Shut down: lose fixed cost = (15 - 10) × 1,000 = 5,000
   - Operate in the short run; exit in the long run unless P reaches 15
3. **HHI.** Shares 40/30/20/10: HHI = 1,600 + 900 + 400 + 100 = 3,000. Merge the 20% and 10% firms: 1,600 + 900 + 900 = 3,400. Three-firm CR goes from 90% to 100%.

## Questions to ask students
- A firm's price is 8, AVC is 9 and ATC is 11. What should it do in the short run? (Shut down; price does not cover AVC.)
- Why does a monopolist's MR lie below its demand curve? (To sell an extra unit it must lower the price on all units.)
- Which structure has many firms, differentiated products and zero long-run economic profit? (Monopolistic competition.)
- Why are cartels unstable? (Each member gains by secretly producing more than its quota.)
- What is the HHI of a pure monopoly? (10,000.)

## Analogies
- Perfect competition is a farmers' market where every stall sells identical apples: charge a penny more and nobody buys.
- The kinked demand curve is a petrol station at a crossroads: cut your price and the station opposite cuts too; raise it and they happily keep theirs.
- A cartel is a group diet pact: everyone benefits if all stick to it, but each person is tempted to sneak a slice of cake.

## Recording tips
- Draw the monopoly diagram step by step (demand, MR, MC, then price) rather than showing it finished; the order of reasoning is what gets tested.
- Emphasise that the shutdown comparison is loss when operating versus fixed cost when closed; that framing removes most confusion.
- Show the comparison table on screen for at least a minute and invite students to pause and copy it.
