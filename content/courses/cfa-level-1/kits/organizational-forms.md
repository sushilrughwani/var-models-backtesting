## Slides
### Slide 1: Same Business, Different Wrapper
- A business's legal form decides who can lose what, who decides and who pays tax
- Today: four forms, two ownership states (public and private)
- Sets up the stakeholder and governance lessons that follow

### Slide 2: Five Questions That Classify Any Form
- Separate legal person?
- Owner liability: limited or unlimited?
- Who controls: owners or hired managers?
- Taxed once (pass-through) or twice?
- How easy to raise capital and transfer ownership?

### Slide 3: Sole Proprietorship
- One owner, no separate legal identity
- Owner keeps all profit and all control
- Unlimited personal liability; growth limited by owner's wealth
- Profits taxed as personal income

### Slide 4: General Partnership
- Two or more owners under a partnership agreement
- More capital and skills than a sole proprietor
- Every general partner has unlimited liability, including for partners' actions
- Pass-through taxation

### Slide 5: Limited Partnership
- General partner: manages, unlimited liability
- Limited partners: provide capital, no management role, loss capped at investment
- Standard structure for private equity, venture capital and many hedge funds

### Slide 6: The Corporation
- Separate legal entity owned by shareholders
- Limited liability, perpetual life, freely transferable shares
- Shareholders elect a board; board hires managers
- Separation of ownership and control creates agency problems

### Slide 7: Double Taxation
- Corporate tax first, then dividend tax on what is distributed
- $t_{\text{eff}} = 1-(1-t_c)(1-t_d)$
- Multiply the "keep" fractions; never add the rates

### Slide 8: Comparison Table
- Sole prop / GP: unlimited liability, owner control, taxed once
- LP: mixed liability, GP control, usually taxed once
- Corporation: limited liability, board and managers, often taxed twice

### Slide 9: Public versus Private
- Public: listed shares, liquidity, capital access, heavy disclosure, market pressure
- Private: concentrated owners, illiquid shares, light disclosure, long horizon
- Private does not mean small

### Slide 10: Moving Between the Two
- IPO: underwritten sale of new and/or existing shares
- Direct listing: existing shares start trading, traditionally no new capital
- SPAC merger: listed shell with cash merges with a private firm
- Take-private / LBO: buyer acquires all shares and delists

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Lena's structure choice.** Pretax profit USD 200,000. Pass-through personal rate 35%; corporate rate 25%, dividend rate 20%, full payout.
- Pass-through: 200,000 × 0.65 = 130,000 kept.
- Corporation: 200,000 × 0.75 = 150,000 after corporate tax; 150,000 × 0.80 = 120,000 kept.
- Effective corporate-route rate: 1 - 0.75 × 0.80 = 40%. Difference: 10,000 dollars in favour of pass-through.
- Discussion: what does Lena buy with that 10,000? (Limited liability, transferable shares, ability to retain earnings and defer dividend tax.)

**Example 2 - Who is liable?** A limited partnership has GP Arun and LPs Bea (invested 50,000) and Caz (invested 80,000). The fund collapses owing creditors 400,000 beyond its assets.
- Bea loses at most her 50,000, Caz at most his 80,000.
- Arun, as GP, is personally liable for the 400,000 shortfall.
- Twist: if Bea had been actively managing the fund, she could lose her limited liability protection.

## Questions to ask students
- Which form gives the owner the most control but the most personal risk? (Sole proprietorship.)
- Why would investors accept double taxation? (Limited liability, liquidity of shares, diversification across many firms, professional management.)
- Corporate tax 30%, dividend tax 10%: what is the effective rate? (1 - 0.7 × 0.9 = 37%, not 40%.)
- What distinguishes a limited partner from a general partner? (LPs supply capital with limited liability and no management role; GPs manage with unlimited liability.)
- A large family-owned firm with no listed shares: public or private? (Private; size is irrelevant.)
- Does a traditional direct listing raise new money for the company? (No, it gives existing holders a market to sell into.)

## Analogies
- Legal form as a wrapper: the business inside is the same, but the wrapper decides who gets burned if it catches fire.
- Limited partnership as a car with a driver and passengers: the GP drives and is responsible for crashes; LPs paid for fuel but are not allowed to touch the wheel.
- Double taxation as two toll booths on the same road: the second toll is charged only on the money that made it past the first.

## Recording tips
- Put the five classification questions on screen and keep returning to them for each form; students remember the grid, not the prose.
- Students most often add tax rates. Do the 25% + 20% wrong answer on screen first, then show why multiplying the keep fractions is right.
- Keep public versus private brief and concrete: one sentence per route (IPO, direct listing, SPAC, take-private).
