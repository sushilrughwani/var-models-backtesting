## Slides
### Slide 1: Why model?
- Turn a view of the business into numbers
- Inputs to valuation and credit analysis
- Last lesson's ratios become this lesson's assumptions

### Slide 2: Revenue approaches
- Growth relative to GDP or industry
- Market size × market share
- Drivers: volume × price, units × revenue per unit

### Slide 3: Top-down vs bottom-up
- Top-down: economy → industry → company
- Bottom-up: stores, products, customers
- Reconcile the two for a credible forecast

### Slide 4: Cost forecasting
- COGS via gross margin; adjust for input prices and pricing power
- SG&A = fixed + variable × revenue
- Fixed costs → operating leverage

### Slide 5: Working capital from days
- AR = revenue × DSO / 365
- Inventory = COGS × DOH / 365; AP = COGS × DPO / 365
- ΔNWC is a cash use when the business grows

### Slide 6: Capital expenditures
- Maintenance capex ≈ depreciation (adjust for inflation)
- Growth capex linked to revenue growth
- Growing firms: capex > depreciation

### Slide 7: Forecast to free cash flow
- FCFF = EBIT(1 − t) + D&A − capex − ΔNWC
- Each line comes from one assumption
- Keep assumptions visible and separate from calculations

### Slide 8: Worked example results
- Revenue 2,160; EBIT 441.2 (20.4% margin)
- ΔNWC 24.99; capex 116
- FCFF 289.91

### Slide 9: Scenarios and sensitivity
- Base, bull and bear cases
- Flex growth, margin and days ratios
- Focus on the drivers that move value the most

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Year 1 forecast.** Year 0 revenue 2,000, COGS 1,200; growth 8%; COGS 60% of revenue; SG&A 150 + 8% of revenue; depreciation 100; tax 25%; DSO 45, DOH 60, DPO 40; capex = depreciation + 10% of revenue increase.
- Revenue = 2,160; COGS = 1,296; gross profit = 864
- SG&A = 322.8; EBIT = 441.2; NOPAT = 330.9
- NWC Year 0 = 246.58 + 197.26 − 131.51 = 312.33
- NWC Year 1 = 266.30 + 213.04 − 142.03 = 337.32; ΔNWC = 24.99
- Capex = 116
- FCFF = 330.9 + 100 − 116 − 24.99 = 289.91

**Example 2 - Operating leverage check.** Year 0 EBIT using the same cost structure: SG&A = 150 + 0.08 × 2,000 = 310; EBIT = 800 − 310 − 100 = 390. Year 1 EBIT 441.2 is up 13.1% while revenue rose 8%, because 150 of SG&A and the 100 of depreciation did not grow.

## Questions to ask students
- Revenue next year is forecast at 3,650 and DSO at 40 days. What is forecast AR? (3,650 × 40 / 365 = 400.)
- Why forecast inventory from COGS rather than revenue? (Inventory is carried at cost, so it scales with COGS.)
- A company has large fixed costs and expects revenue to fall 10%. What happens to its operating margin? (It falls, because fixed costs do not shrink with revenue.)
- Should a fast-growing company's capex equal its depreciation? (No - it needs growth capex on top of maintenance.)
- Is an increase in NWC a source or use of cash? (A use of cash.)

## Analogies
- A model is a flight plan: revenue is the destination, costs are fuel burn, working capital and capex are the luggage you must carry to get there.
- Operating leverage is like a gym membership: the fixed fee hurts when you rarely go, but the more you use it, the cheaper each visit becomes.
- Working capital is the float a growing shop needs: more sales means more stock on the shelves and more customers on credit, all funded before the cash comes back.

## Recording tips
- Show the model as a spreadsheet layout with an assumptions block on top; label every input.
- Stress that the change in NWC, not its level, enters free cash flow - a very common slip.
- End with a quick sensitivity: at 5% growth Year 1 FCFF is 290.88 versus 289.91 at 8%, because slower growth also needs less capex and working capital. Use this to show that growth pays off in later years, and that a one-year snapshot can mislead.
