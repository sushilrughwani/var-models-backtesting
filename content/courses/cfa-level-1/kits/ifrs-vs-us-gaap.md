## Slides
### Slide 1: Why differences matter
- Same economics, different reported numbers
- Cross-border comparisons need adjustments
- Focus on items that move the ratios you use

### Slide 2: Inventory
- LIFO: US GAAP only
- Write-down reversals: IFRS yes, US GAAP no
- FIFO inventory = LIFO inventory + LIFO reserve

### Slide 3: LIFO to FIFO adjustments
- FIFO COGS = LIFO COGS − change in LIFO reserve
- Equity + LIFO reserve × (1 − t)
- Tax liability + LIFO reserve × t

### Slide 4: Long-lived assets
- IFRS: revaluation model allowed; US GAAP: cost only
- IFRS capitalises development costs when criteria met
- US GAAP generally expenses R&D

### Slide 5: Impairment
- IFRS: carrying amount vs recoverable amount (one step)
- US GAAP: undiscounted cash flow test, then fair value
- Reversals: IFRS yes (not goodwill), US GAAP no

### Slide 6: Leases and provisions
- IFRS lessee: single model; US GAAP: finance vs operating
- IFRS provision threshold: more likely than not
- Range of outcomes: IFRS midpoint, US GAAP low end

### Slide 7: Cash flow classification
- Interest paid: IFRS operating or financing; US GAAP operating
- Interest and dividends received: IFRS operating or investing; US GAAP operating
- Dividends paid: IFRS operating or financing; US GAAP financing

### Slide 8: Alder vs Birch results
- Alder CFO restated: 900 → 840
- Birch FIFO inventory 550; FIFO COGS 1,970
- Birch NI +22.5; equity +112.5

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Alder vs Birch** (tax 25%).
- Alder (IFRS): CFO 900 with interest paid 60 in financing → US GAAP-basis CFO = 840
- Birch (US GAAP, LIFO): inventory 400, LIFO reserve 120 → 150, LIFO COGS 2,000
- FIFO inventory = 400 + 150 = 550
- FIFO COGS = 2,000 − (150 − 120) = 1,970
- Net income +30 × 0.75 = +22.5
- Equity +150 × 0.75 = +112.5; tax liability +37.5

**Example 2 - Development costs.** An IFRS company spends 50 on development that meets the capitalisation criteria, amortised over 5 years; a US GAAP peer expenses the 50.
- Year 1 pre-tax profit: IFRS company is higher by 50 − 10 = 40
- CFO: IFRS company is higher by 50 (outflow in investing instead of operating)
- Total assets: IFRS company higher by 40 at year-end

## Questions to ask students
- A US GAAP firm's LIFO reserve fell during the year. Is FIFO COGS higher or lower than LIFO COGS? (Higher - subtracting a negative change adds to COGS.)
- Can a US GAAP company reverse an inventory write-down? (No.)
- Which framework allows PP&E to be carried at revalued amounts? (IFRS.)
- An IFRS company classifies dividends paid as operating. How does its CFO compare with a US GAAP peer? (Lower, since US GAAP puts dividends paid in financing.)
- Why might IFRS impairments happen earlier than US GAAP impairments? (IFRS compares carrying amount with a discounted recoverable amount directly; US GAAP first screens with undiscounted cash flows.)

## Analogies
- Comparing IFRS and US GAAP numbers without adjustments is like comparing temperatures in Celsius and Fahrenheit - the weather is the same, but the readings are not.
- The LIFO reserve is the price tag history hidden in the notes: add it back and you see what the inventory would be worth at recent costs.
- IFRS cash flow classification choices are like a restaurant choosing whether to list the service charge in the menu price or on the bill - the meal costs the same, but the headline number changes.
