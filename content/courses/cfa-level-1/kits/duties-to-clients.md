### Slide 2: Who Is the Client?
- Pension plan: the beneficiaries
- Fund: the fund and its mandate
- Client interests before employer and self
### Slide 3: Proxies and Soft Dollars
- Proxy voting is part of the fiduciary job
- Cost-benefit analysis can justify not voting
- Soft dollars belong to the client: research that benefits them, best execution
### Slide 4: Fair, Not Equal
- Premium service tiers allowed if disclosed and available to all
- Simultaneous dissemination of recommendation changes
- No disadvantage from timing
### Slide 5: Trade Allocation
- Pro rata or another fair, disclosed method
- Partial fills shared proportionally
- Oversubscribed IPOs: clients before own and family accounts
### Slide 6: Suitability and the IPS
- Experience, objectives, constraints
- Written IPS, reviewed at least annually
- Judge at whole-portfolio level
### Slide 7: Unsolicited Trades
- Explain the mismatch before executing
- Small impact: proceed after discussion
- Material impact: update IPS or reconsider relationship
### Slide 8: Fund Managers
- Suitability means sticking to the stated mandate
- Style drift is a suitability problem
### Slide 9: Performance Presentation
- Fair, accurate, complete

## Whiteboard examples
1. **Pro rata allocation.** Orders: Account A 20,000, B 15,000, C 15,000 (total 50,000). Fill: 30,000. Fill ratio = 30,000 / 50,000 = 0.6. A gets 12,000, B gets 9,000, C gets 9,000. Check: 12,000 + 9,000 + 9,000 = 30,000.
2. **Unsolicited trade decision tree.** Client requests unsuitable trade. Branch 1: explain unsuitability. Branch 2a: small relative to portfolio - execute and document. Branch 2b: material change - propose IPS update; if client refuses, decide whether to continue the relationship. Apply to the retiree asking for a 2% crypto allocation: explain, document, execute.
3. **Confidentiality checklist.** Journalist asks about a client: illegal activity? No. Required by law? No. Client consent? No. Result: decline to disclose.

## Questions to ask students
- Who is the client for a corporate pension plan manager? (The plan's beneficiaries.)
- Can a firm offer a faster research service to clients who pay more? (Yes, if disclosed and available to all who choose to pay, without disadvantaging others.)
- Must a manager vote every proxy? (No; a cost-benefit analysis can justify not voting.)
- A client requests a small unsuitable trade. Must the adviser refuse? (No; discuss the unsuitability first, then may execute.)
- Name the three exceptions to confidentiality. (Illegal activities, required by law, client permission.)

## Analogies
- Loyalty to clients is like a lifeguard's duty: you watch the swimmers, not the beach owner's profits.
- Fair dealing is like airline boarding: first class boards first because it is disclosed and purchasable, but nobody's seat is given away.
- The IPS is a GPS route: every trade is checked against the destination, and you reroute only with the driver's agreement.

## Recording tips
- This is the most-tested Standard; slow down and use many short examples.
- Put the pro rata calculation on screen step by step; students like the one numeric moment in ethics.
- Emphasise the "fair is not equal" distinction - it is a common distractor.
