## Slides
### Slide 1: Fixed-Income Instrument Features
- A bond is a loan cut into tradable pieces
- Everything you need is written in the contract
- Goal today: read a bond's terms like an analyst

### Slide 2: The Indenture and the Trustee
- Indenture (trust deed) = the legal contract between issuer and holders
- Trustee monitors compliance and acts for all bondholders
- Five questions: who owes, when, how much, how often, what protects me

### Slide 3: Who Issues Bonds
- Sovereign and local governments, agencies
- Supranational organisations (development banks)
- Financial and non-financial corporations
- Special purpose vehicles in securitisations

### Slide 4: Maturity, Tenor and Par
- Maturity date = principal repayment date; tenor = time remaining
- One year or less: money market; longer: capital market
- Perpetual bonds have no maturity
- Prices quoted as % of par (98.50 = 98.5% of face)

### Slide 5: Coupon Rate and Frequency
- Coupon payment = coupon rate × par ÷ payments per year
- Frequency: annual, semiannual, quarterly, monthly
- Coupons are based on par, never on price

### Slide 6: Seniority and Collateral
- Secured (specific assets) vs unsecured (debentures)
- Senior unsecured ranks above subordinated
- Higher ranking = higher recovery = lower yield

### Slide 7: Credit Enhancements
- Internal: subordination, overcollateralisation, reserve accounts
- External: guarantees, letters of credit, surety bonds
- Covered bonds: asset pool plus recourse to the issuer
- External support is only as strong as the guarantor

### Slide 8: Covenants
- Affirmative = must do (pay, insure, report, maintain)
- Negative = must not do (extra debt, negative pledge, big dividends, asset sales)
- Tighter covenants, lower yield

### Slide 9: Contingency Provisions
- Call: issuer redeems early (investor demands more yield)
- Put: investor sells back early (investor accepts less yield)
- Conversion: investor swaps for shares (lower coupon)
- Issuer's option raises yield; investor's option lowers it

### Slide 10: Legal and Tax Considerations
- Domestic, foreign and Eurobonds
- Dual-currency bonds: coupons and principal in different currencies
- Tax-exempt coupons and taxable accretion on discount bonds

### Slide 11: Recap
- Read the indenture: issuer, maturity, par, coupon, currency
- Protection: seniority, collateral, enhancements, covenants
- Options change cash flows and the yield investors require

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - coupon payment.** A bond has par USD 1,000,000, a 4.5% coupon paid semiannually. Coupon = 0.045 × 1,000,000 ÷ 2 = **USD 22,500** every six months (USD 45,000 per year). Then ask: what if it paid quarterly? 0.045 × 1,000,000 ÷ 4 = USD 11,250.

**Example 2 - covenant sorting.** Write six clauses and have students classify:
1. Must deliver audited accounts within 120 days (affirmative)
2. May not pledge the same assets to another lender (negative - negative pledge)
3. Must maintain insurance on plant (affirmative)
4. Debt may not exceed 3.0× EBITDA (negative)
5. May not pay dividends above 50% of net income (negative)
6. Must pay coupons on time (affirmative)

**Example 3 - yield ranking.** Three otherwise identical 10-year bonds: straight, callable, putable. Rank yields highest to lowest: **callable > straight > putable**, because the call is the issuer's option (investor needs compensation) and the put is the investor's option (investor pays for it with lower yield).

## Questions to ask students
- If a bond quotes at 101.25 and par is USD 500,000, what is the price in dollars? (USD 506,250)
- Who benefits from a call provision, and when is it likely to be exercised? (The issuer; after rates fall, to refinance more cheaply.)
- Is "the issuer may not sell more than 20% of its assets without bondholder consent" affirmative or negative? (Negative - it restricts an action the issuer could otherwise take.)
- Why might a bank-guaranteed bond still default? (If the guaranteeing bank itself fails - external enhancement carries the guarantor's credit risk.)
- Would a convertible bond have a higher or lower coupon than a straight bond from the same issuer? (Lower - investors receive equity upside.)

## Analogies
- The indenture is a rental lease: it lists the rent (coupon), the end date (maturity), the deposit (collateral) and the house rules (covenants). The trustee is the building manager.
- Seniority is the queue at a closing-down sale: secured creditors get first pick of specific items, senior unsecured next, subordinated holders get what is left.
- Embedded options as "free exits": whoever holds the exit door key gets value, and the other side charges for it through the yield.

## Recording tips
- Keep a sample term sheet on screen for the whole lecture and highlight each feature as you cover it.
- Students most often reverse the call/put yield effect; say the "issuer's option raises yield" rule at least three times.
- Pause the recording after the covenant list and invite viewers to classify before revealing answers.
