## Slides
### Slide 1: Roles of a Central Bank
- Sole issuer of currency, banker to government and banks
- Lender of last resort; supervisor of banks and payments
- Manages foreign reserves and conducts monetary policy

### Slide 2: Objectives and Qualities
- Main objective: price stability (inflation target)
- Independence from political pressure
- Credibility anchors expectations
- Transparency explains decisions

### Slide 3: How Banks Create Money
- Fractional reserves: keep r, lend the rest
- Money multiplier = 1 / r
- New deposit D supports up to D / r of deposits

### Slide 4: Money and Prices
- MV = PY
- Stable V: money growth ≈ inflation + real growth
- Fisher effect: nominal rate = real rate + expected inflation

### Slide 5: The Tools
- Open market operations: buy bonds adds reserves, sell drains
- Policy rate: price of central bank funds
- Reserve requirements: higher r, smaller multiplier

### Slide 6: Transmission Channels
- Market and bank lending rates
- Asset prices and wealth effect
- Expectations and confidence
- Exchange rate and net exports

### Slide 7: The Neutral Rate
- Neutral = trend real growth + inflation target
- Policy rate above neutral: contractionary
- Policy rate below neutral: expansionary

### Slide 8: Limits of Monetary Policy
- Long yields set by markets and inflation fears
- Liquidity trap: money hoarded at near-zero rates
- Deflation keeps real rates high
- Quantitative easing as the response

### Slide 9: Fiscal and Monetary Mix
- Easy + easy: strongly expansionary
- Tight + tight: strongly contractionary
- Easy monetary + tight fiscal: private sector grows relative to public
- Tight monetary + easy fiscal: public sector grows relative to private

### Slide 10: Exam Traps
- Compare policy rate with neutral rate, not with inflation target
- Selling bonds drains reserves
- Higher reserve ratio shrinks the multiplier

## Whiteboard examples
1. **Policy stance.** Inflation target 2.0%, trend real growth 2.0%, policy rate 5.5%.
   - Neutral = 4.0%; policy rate is 1.5 points above
   - Stance: contractionary
2. **Quantity theory.** Money growth 6%, stable V, real growth 2.5%.
   - Approximate inflation = 3.5%; exact = 1.06/1.025 − 1 = 3.41%
3. **Money multiplier.** Reserve ratio 8%, new deposit 50 million.
   - Multiplier = 1 / 0.08 = 12.5
   - Maximum deposits = 50 × 12.5 = 625 million; new money from lending = 575 million

## Questions to ask students
- The central bank buys government bonds from banks. What happens to reserves and short-term rates? (Reserves rise, short-term rates fall.)
- Trend growth is 1.5%, inflation target 2.5%, policy rate 3%. What is the stance? (Expansionary; neutral is 4%.)
- What does raising the reserve requirement from 10% to 20% do to the multiplier? (It halves it, from 10 to 5.)
- Why might long-term yields rise after a rate cut? (Markets may expect higher future inflation.)
- What is a liquidity trap? (Rates near zero, extra money is hoarded, so conventional policy loses power.)

## Analogies
- The neutral rate is the speed at which a car neither accelerates nor brakes on a flat road; policy above it is a foot on the brake.
- Credibility is like a parent's warning: if it is always carried out, the threat alone changes behaviour.
- The money multiplier is a relay race: each bank passes most of the baton on, keeping only a small slice.

## Recording tips

- Use a flow diagram for transmission channels, adding one arrow at a time.
- Show the policy mix matrix as a 2x2 grid and quiz students by covering one cell at a time.
