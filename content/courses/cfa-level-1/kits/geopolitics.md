## Slides
### Slide 1: Why Geopolitics Belongs in Analysis
- Policy decisions abroad change cash flows and risk at home
- Shocks can be sudden and large
- Analysts need a framework, not predictions

### Slide 2: The Actors
- State actors: governments and agencies
- Non-state actors: multinationals, international organisations, NGOs, individuals
- Motives: security, prosperity, protection of political system and culture

### Slide 3: Two Dimensions
- Cooperative versus non-cooperative behaviour
- Globalisation versus nationalism
- The dimensions are independent

### Slide 4: Four Regimes
- Autarky: non-cooperative and nationalist
- Hegemony: non-cooperative and globalised
- Bilateralism: cooperative and nationalist
- Multilateralism: cooperative and globalised

### Slide 5: Tools of Geopolitics
- National security: conflict, espionage, alliances
- Economic: trade agreements, tariffs, quotas, nationalisation
- Financial: reserve currency, sanctions, investment restrictions, capital controls

### Slide 6: Types of Geopolitical Risk
- Event risk: known dates (elections, deadlines)
- Exogenous risk: sudden, unanticipated shocks
- Thematic risk: long-running trends (climate, cyber, trade fragmentation)

### Slide 7: Assessing Risk
- Likelihood
- Velocity: how fast markets feel it
- Size and nature of impact: broad or contained

### Slide 8: How Risk Reaches Portfolios
- Cash flows: lost sales, costs, seized assets
- Discount rates: higher risk premiums
- Volatility and flight to safe havens

### Slide 9: Managing Geopolitical Risk
- Scenario analysis with probabilities
- Signposts that update probabilities
- Match the response to velocity

### Slide 10: Exam Traps
- Scheduled events are event risk, not exogenous
- Non-state actors matter
- Cooperation and globalisation are different axes

## Whiteboard examples
1. **Scenario-weighted EPS.** Manufacturer with 30% of components from a country facing possible sanctions; status-quo EPS 5.00, P/E 12.
   - Scenarios: status quo 70% (EPS 5.00), targeted sanctions 20% (4.20), escalation 10% (3.00)
   - Expected EPS = 3.50 + 0.84 + 0.30 = 4.64, which is 7.2% below 5.00
   - Value at constant P/E: 12 × 4.64 = 55.68 versus 60.00
   - Classification: event risk with an exogenous-style tail; velocity high, so act early
2. **Classify the risks.** (a) A presidential election in six months: event risk. (b) A surprise military coup: exogenous risk. (c) Gradual reshoring of supply chains over a decade: thematic risk.

## Questions to ask students
- A multinational lobbying for new trade rules is what kind of actor? (A non-state actor.)
- Which regime combines cooperation with globalisation? (Multilateralism.)
- Freezing a country's central bank reserves is which type of tool? (A financial tool.)
- Why does velocity matter if likelihood and impact are the same? (A fast risk needs an immediate tactical response; a slow one can be handled strategically.)
- Name two channels through which geopolitical risk lowers equity values. (Lower expected cash flows and higher discount rates.)

## Analogies
- Event risk is a scheduled exam date, exogenous risk is a fire alarm in the middle of the exam, thematic risk is the gradual change in the syllabus over the years.
- Signposts are the dashboard warning lights on a long drive: they tell you to update your plan before the engine fails.
- A reserve currency is like owning the toll road: whoever controls it can decide who gets to drive.

## Recording tips
- Draw the 2x2 regime grid live and fill each quadrant with a one-line example; it is the visual students will recall.
- Keep this lecture concrete: one scenario table on screen beats a list of abstract definitions.
- Remind students the exam tests classification (which risk type, which tool, which regime) more than prediction.
