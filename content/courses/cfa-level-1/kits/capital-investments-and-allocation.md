## Slides
### Slide 1: Capital Allocation Is Strategy in Numbers
- Long-term investments shape what the firm becomes
- Goal: invest only where returns exceed the cost of capital
- Tools today: NPV, IRR, real options

### Slide 2: Types of Projects
- Going concern: maintenance, replacement, cost cutting
- Regulatory/compliance: required by law, may be negative NPV alone
- Expansion: bigger existing business, less certain forecasts
- Other: new products, R&D, acquisitions, highest scrutiny

### Slide 3: Cash Flow Principles
- Incremental after-tax cash flows only
- Include opportunity costs and externalities (cannibalisation)
- Ignore sunk costs
- Financing costs live in the discount rate, not the cash flows

### Slide 4: Net Present Value
- $\text{NPV} = \sum \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t} - CF_0$
- Accept if NPV > 0
- NPV = expected change in shareholder wealth

### Slide 5: Internal Rate of Return
- Rate that makes NPV = 0
- Accept if IRR > required return
- Same decision as NPV for one independent conventional project

### Slide 6: Delta Robotics
- Cost 1,000; inflows 400, 450, 500; r = 10%
- PVs 363.64 + 371.90 + 375.66 = 1,111.19
- NPV 111.19; IRR 15.98%: accept

### Slide 7: When the Rules Disagree
- Mutually exclusive projects of different scale or timing
- A: −100, +120 (IRR 20%, NPV 9.09); B: −300, +345 (IRR 15%, NPV 13.64)
- Follow NPV: it measures dollars of value created

### Slide 8: Why NPV Wins
- NPV assumes reinvestment at the cost of capital, IRR at the IRR
- IRR ignores scale
- Non-conventional cash flows can give multiple or no IRRs

### Slide 9: Allocation Pitfalls
- Inertia and last year's budget
- Treating internal cash as free
- Pet projects and optimistic forecasts
- Chasing EPS or ROE instead of NPV
- Ignoring alternatives, overhead and competitor reactions

### Slide 10: Real Options
- Timing, abandonment, expansion, flexibility
- NPV with options = static NPV + option value − option cost
- A negative static NPV can become positive with valuable options

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Delta Robotics.** CF0 = −1,000; CF1 = 400; CF2 = 450; CF3 = 500; r = 10%.
- 400/1.1 = 363.64; 450/1.21 = 371.90; 500/1.331 = 375.66.
- Sum 1,111.19; NPV = 111.19.
- NPV at 15% = 16.85; at 16% = −0.42; IRR = 15.98%.
- Accept on both rules.

**Example 2 - Scale conflict.** r = 10%. A: −100 then +120. B: −300 then +345.
- A: NPV = 120/1.1 − 100 = 9.09; IRR = 20%.
- B: NPV = 345/1.1 − 300 = 13.64; IRR = 15%.
- Choose B. Show the incremental project B − A: −200 then +225, NPV = 225/1.1 − 200 = 4.55 > 0, IRR = 12.5% > 10%, confirming the extra investment is worthwhile.

**Example 3 - Annuity project.** Cost 2,500; 900 a year for 4 years; r = 12%.
- PV of annuity = 900 × [1 − 1.12^−4]/0.12 = 2,733.61; NPV = 233.61; IRR = 16.37%.

## Questions to ask students
- A test-market study cost 50,000 last year. Include it in the new product's cash flows? (No, it is sunk.)
- A new product will reduce sales of an existing one by 20,000 a year. What do you do? (Subtract the lost after-tax cash flow as a negative externality.)
- Project NPV is positive. What must be true about IRR versus required return? (IRR exceeds it, for conventional cash flows.)
- Two mutually exclusive projects: higher IRR or higher NPV? (Higher NPV.)
- Why might a firm accept a negative-NPV pollution scrubber? (Regulatory requirement; the alternative is fines or closure.)
- Name a real option in a mining project. (Delay development, expand, or abandon if prices fall.)

## Analogies
- NPV is the price tag difference: what the project is worth today minus what it costs; positive means you are buying a dollar for less than a dollar.
- IRR is the project's "speed"; NPV is the "distance travelled". A fast scooter on a short trip loses to a slower truck on a long haul.
- Sunk costs are spilt milk: you cannot unspill it, so it should not decide whether you buy more milk.

## Recording tips
- Show the calculator keystrokes for IRR on screen; many students have never used the cash flow worksheet.
- Spend real time on the scale conflict and the incremental-project check; it is the most testable concept here.
- Use the pitfalls slide to tell short stories; candidates remember examples, not lists.
