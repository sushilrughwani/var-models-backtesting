## Slides
### Slide 1: Same ROE, different stories
- Company A: 25% ROE from strong margins
- Company B: 25% ROE from heavy borrowing
- DuPont tells them apart

### Slide 2: Ratio analysis ground rules
- Compare over time and against peers
- Use average balances when mixing flows and stocks
- Ratios point to questions, not answers

### Slide 3: Activity ratios
- Receivables turnover → DSO
- Inventory turnover → DOH
- Payables turnover → DPO; total asset turnover

### Slide 4: Cash conversion cycle
- CCC = DSO + DOH − DPO
- Days of operations funded by the company's own cash
- Very high DPO may signal payment problems

### Slide 5: Liquidity ratios
- Current = CA / CL
- Quick = (cash + securities + receivables) / CL
- Cash = (cash + securities) / CL

### Slide 6: Solvency ratios
- Debt-to-equity, debt-to-assets
- Financial leverage = assets / equity
- Interest coverage = EBIT / interest

### Slide 7: Profitability ratios
- Gross, operating, net margin
- ROA = NI / average assets
- ROE = NI / average equity

### Slide 8: Three-part DuPont
- ROE = net margin × asset turnover × leverage
- Margin = pricing and cost control
- Turnover = asset efficiency; leverage = financing risk

### Slide 9: Five-part DuPont
- Tax burden (NI/EBT) × interest burden (EBT/EBIT)
- × EBIT margin × asset turnover × leverage
- Higher burden ratios are better

### Slide 10: Orion results
- CCC 60.8 days; current 1.50; quick 0.77
- D/E 0.83; coverage 9.0×
- ROE 25% = 0.75 × 0.889 × 18% × 0.833 × 2.5

### Slide 11: Limits of ratios
- Different accounting choices across firms
- Diversified firms lack clean peer groups
- One-off items and seasonality distort single-period ratios

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Orion Manufacturing** (millions, averages): revenue 5,000; COGS 3,000; SG&A 1,100; interest 100; tax 25%; assets 6,000; equity 2,400; debt 2,000; receivables 625; inventory 500; payables 375; current assets 1,800 incl. cash 300; current liabilities 1,200.
- EBIT 900; EBT 800; tax 200; NI 600
- DSO = 365 / 8.0 = 45.6; DOH = 365 / 6.0 = 60.8; DPO = 365 / 8.0 = 45.6; CCC = 60.8
- Current 1.50; quick 925 / 1,200 = 0.77
- D/E 0.83; D/A 0.33; leverage 2.5; coverage 9.0×
- Net margin 12%; turnover 0.833; ROA 10%; ROE 25%
- Five-part: 0.75 × 0.889 × 0.18 × 0.833 × 2.5 = 25%

**Example 2 - What-if.** Orion borrows 600 more and buys back 600 of equity. Average assets stay 6,000, equity falls to 1,800; assume extra interest of 40.
- EBT = 900 − 140 = 760; NI = 760 × 0.75 = 570
- Leverage = 6,000 / 1,800 = 3.33; net margin = 570 / 5,000 = 11.4%
- ROE = 570 / 1,800 = 31.7% - higher, but coverage falls to 900 / 140 = 6.4×

## Questions to ask students
- DSO 40, DOH 55, DPO 30. What is the CCC? (65 days.)
- Why does the quick ratio exclude inventory? (It may take a long time to sell or sell below book value.)
- ROE rose while net margin and asset turnover were flat. What drove it? (Higher financial leverage.)
- The interest burden ratio fell from 0.95 to 0.85. Good or bad? (Bad - interest is consuming more of EBIT.)
- Why use average balances? (Income statement items accumulate over the whole period, so they should be matched with the period's average balance.)

## Analogies
- DuPont is a medical check-up for ROE: margin is the heart (profit per sale), turnover the lungs (efficiency), leverage the adrenaline - it boosts performance but strains the system.
- The cash conversion cycle is a relay race: cash leaves when you pay suppliers and comes back only when customers pay you. The fewer days between handoffs, the less cash you need.
- Liquidity vs solvency: can you pay this month's rent (liquidity) versus can you pay off the mortgage (solvency)?

## Recording tips
- Keep one master data table for Orion on screen and point to the inputs as each ratio is computed.
- Pause after the five-part product reproduces 25%; students love seeing the decomposition reconcile.
- Run the what-if buyback example to make the risk side of leverage concrete.
