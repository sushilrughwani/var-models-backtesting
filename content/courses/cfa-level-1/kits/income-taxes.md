## Slides
### Slide 1: Two rulebooks, one company
- Financial reporting (IFRS / US GAAP) gives pre-tax accounting profit
- The tax code gives taxable income
- Different timing rules create gaps that must be accounted for

### Slide 2: Key terms
- Taxes payable = taxable income × tax rate (cash owed now)
- Income tax expense = what the income statement shows
- Income tax expense = taxes payable + ΔDTL − ΔDTA

### Slide 3: Carrying amount vs tax base
- Carrying amount: value on the balance sheet
- Tax base: value the tax authority recognises
- Deferred tax = (carrying amount − tax base) × expected tax rate

### Slide 4: The direction grid
- Asset: carrying > tax base → DTL
- Asset: carrying < tax base → DTA
- Liability: carrying > tax base → DTA
- Liability: carrying < tax base → DTL

### Slide 5: Classic DTL - accelerated tax depreciation
- Bigger tax deductions now, smaller later
- Book value of the asset exceeds its tax base
- Company owes more tax in later years

### Slide 6: Classic DTA - warranty and bad-debt provisions
- Expense recognised in the books before it is deductible
- Liability on the books, zero tax base
- Company has effectively prepaid tax

### Slide 7: Worked example set-up
- Equipment 900,000; book 300,000/yr; tax 450,000 in Year 1
- Warranty 40,000 (not yet deductible); fine 20,000 (never deductible)
- Pre-tax profit 1,000,000; tax rate 25%

### Slide 8: Worked example answers
- Taxable income 910,000 → taxes payable 227,500
- DTL 37,500; DTA 10,000
- Tax expense 255,000; effective rate 25.5%

### Slide 9: Permanent differences
- Never reverse: fines, tax-exempt interest
- No deferred tax created
- Explain the gap between effective and statutory rates

### Slide 10: Valuation allowance and rate changes
- DTA reduced if future taxable income is doubtful
- Allowance changes go straight through net income
- Enacted rate change remeasures all deferred balances

### Slide 11: Analyst treatment of a DTL
- Expected to reverse → liability
- Not expected to reverse → equity
- Uncertain → exclude from both

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 (the lesson example).** Equipment cost 900,000, book depreciation 300,000, tax depreciation 450,000, warranty expense 40,000 (unpaid), fine 20,000, pre-tax profit 1,000,000, tax rate 25%.
- Taxable income = 1,000,000 + 300,000 − 450,000 + 40,000 + 20,000 = 910,000
- Taxes payable = 910,000 × 0.25 = 227,500
- DTL = (600,000 − 450,000) × 0.25 = 37,500
- DTA = (40,000 − 0) × 0.25 = 10,000
- Tax expense = 227,500 + 37,500 − 10,000 = 255,000
- Effective rate = 255,000 / 1,000,000 = 25.5% (statutory 25% + 0.5% from the fine)

**Example 2 (rate change).** Carry the Year 1 balances forward. The government enacts a cut from 25% to 20% before year-end.
- DTL remeasured: 150,000 × 0.20 = 30,000 (falls by 7,500, reducing tax expense)
- DTA remeasured: 40,000 × 0.20 = 8,000 (falls by 2,000, increasing tax expense)
- Net effect on tax expense: −7,500 + 2,000 = −5,500 (a 5,500 benefit)

## Questions to ask students
- A company accrues bad-debt expense on the books but deducts it only when written off. DTA or DTL? (DTA - liability-like provision with zero tax base, or receivable carried below its tax base.)
- Taxes payable are 400; the DTL rose by 50 and the DTA rose by 20. What is tax expense? (400 + 50 − 20 = 430.)
- Does a non-deductible fine create a deferred tax asset? (No - it is permanent; it only raises the effective rate.)
- If a company with a large net DTL benefits from a tax-rate cut, what happens to net income? (It rises, because the DTL is remeasured downward through tax expense.)
- When might an analyst treat a DTL as equity? (When it is not expected to reverse, e.g. a growing firm keeps replacing assets.)

## Analogies
- A DTL is an IOU to the tax authority: you borrowed deductions from the future, and you will repay them by paying more tax later.
- A DTA is a prepaid gift card: you paid tax early and can spend the deduction later, but only if the store (future taxable profit) is still open - hence the valuation allowance.
- Permanent differences are like a fee that is simply not refundable: no future adjustment, just a higher effective cost now.

## Recording tips
- Put the direction grid on screen and leave it visible during the worked example; most errors are sign errors.
- Build taxable income line by line from accounting profit rather than presenting it as a finished number.
- Close with the reconciliation of the 25.5% effective rate back to 25% - it convinces students the method works.
