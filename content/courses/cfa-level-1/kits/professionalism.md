## Slides
### Slide 1: Standard I Overview
- A Knowledge of the law
- B Independence and objectivity
- C Misrepresentation
- D Misconduct
- E Competence
### Slide 2: Stricter Rule Wins
- Comply with law AND the Code and Standards
- Conflict: follow the stricter
- Law permits but Standards forbid: follow Standards
### Slide 3: Dissociation
- Do not knowingly participate in violations
- Raise with supervisor or compliance first
- If it continues: step away, possibly resign
- External reporting encouraged, not required by the Standards
### Slide 4: Gifts and Entertainment
- Client gifts (past service): lower risk, disclose to employer
- Issuer or broker gifts (future influence): higher risk
- Modest and customary is fine; lavish is not
### Slide 5: Issuer-Paid Research and Travel
- Flat fee, not tied to conclusions, fully disclosed
- Pay your own travel to company sites
- Use commercial transport when available
### Slide 6: Internal Pressure
- Investment banking, sales or big clients pushing ratings
- Firewalls and research independence policies
- Your rating reflects your analysis only
### Slide 7: Misrepresentation
- No false statements about performance, services or credentials
- Omissions count
- Plagiarism: credit identifiable authors
- No guarantees on risky returns
### Slide 8: Misconduct
- Dishonesty, fraud, deceit in professional conduct
- Acts reflecting adversely on professional integrity
- Purely private matters usually outside scope
### Slide 9: Competence
- Have the skills for the role
- Keep them current
- Decline or get help beyond your capability
### Slide 10: Case - Leila and the Gold Mine
- Lax local law on gifts
- Luxury issuer-paid trip
- Sales pressure to upgrade
### Slide 11: Key Takeaways
- Stricter rule wins
- Protect independence
- Tell the truth, credit sources
- Stay competent

## Whiteboard examples
1. **Three-country law grid.** Columns: "Local law," "Standards," "Follow." Row 1: local law allows insider trading on non-public info; Standards prohibit - follow Standards. Row 2: local law requires disclosing all personal holdings to clients; Standards require disclosure of conflicts - follow local law (stricter). Row 3: no local law on gifts; Standards restrict compromising gifts - follow Standards.
2. **Leila's case.** Step through: stricter rule (Standards); the mine visit is legitimate but the resort is lavish - decline it and have Ridgeback pay travel; sales pressure - keep independent rating; publishing an unbelieved rating would also be misrepresentation. Final answer: visit at firm's expense, decline hospitality, keep rating.
3. **Gift sort.** Classify: (a) a client sends a holiday hamper after a good year - acceptable, disclose to employer; (b) a broker offers concert tickets worth a modest amount once a year - likely acceptable if modest and customary; (c) a company you cover offers a week at a ski chalet - decline.

## Questions to ask students
- Local law is less strict than the Standards. Which do you follow? (The Standards, the stricter rule.)
- Must a member report a colleague's violation to the regulator? (Not required by the Standards; must dissociate and should report internally.)
- Can an analyst accept a fee from a company to write research on it? (Yes, if it is a flat fee unrelated to the conclusions and fully disclosed.)
- Is copying a chart from another analyst's report without credit a violation? (Yes, misrepresentation through plagiarism.)
- Is getting arrested at a peaceful protest a violation of the misconduct standard? (Generally not, as it does not involve professional dishonesty.)

## Analogies
- The stricter-rule principle is like driving across borders: obey whichever speed limit is lower, the road sign or your company's policy.
- Gifts from issuers are like a referee accepting dinner from one team before the match: even if the call is fair, nobody believes it.
- Plagiarism is wearing someone else's medal: the achievement is real, but it is not yours.

## Recording tips
- Spend extra time on independence and objectivity; it generates many exam questions.
- Use a table on screen for "client gift vs issuer gift" so students see the contrast.
- Remind students that dissociation, not whistleblowing, is the requirement - this is a frequent wrong answer.
