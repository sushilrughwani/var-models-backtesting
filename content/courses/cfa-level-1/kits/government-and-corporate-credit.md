## Slides
### Slide 1: Credit Analysis for Government and Corporate Issuers
- Last lesson: what credit risk is
- Today: how analysts judge a borrower
- Governments first, then companies

### Slide 2: Sovereigns Are Special
- They can tax and, in their own currency, print money
- Local-currency default is rare, but inflation can still hurt
- Foreign-currency debt needs hard currency, so it is riskier
- The local-currency rating is usually at least as high as the foreign-currency rating

### Slide 3: Sovereign Analysis Checklist
- Institutional: stability, rule of law, payment record (willingness)
- Economic: income level, growth, diversification
- External: reserve currency, current account, FX reserves
- Fiscal: debt/GDP, interest/revenue; Monetary: central bank independence, inflation

### Slide 4: Regional and Local Governments
- They cannot print money, so they behave more like companies
- GO bonds: backed by taxing power
- Revenue bonds: backed by one project; use DSCR = net revenue / debt service

### Slide 5: Corporate Credit Framework
- Capacity: cash flow to service debt
- Capital: the equity cushion and funding access
- Collateral: assets available to creditors
- Covenants: legal protection; Character: management and governance

### Slide 6: Leverage Ratios
- Debt/EBITDA and net debt/EBITDA: lower is better
- Debt/capital: lower is better
- FFO/debt: higher is better

### Slide 7: Coverage Ratios
- EBITDA/interest and EBIT/interest: higher is better
- These measure the cushion before interest payments are threatened
- Always compare with industry peers

### Slide 8: Corvane Industries
- Debt/EBITDA 3.00×, net 2.50×, FFO/debt 21.7%
- Debt/capital 40%, EBITDA/interest 6.67×, EBIT/interest 5.00×

### Slide 9: Buyback Funded with Debt
- +300 debt at 5%: Debt/EBITDA 3.75×, EBITDA/interest 5.33×, debt/capital 50%
- Good news for shareholders can be bad news for bondholders
- This is why covenants restricting payouts matter

### Slide 10: Exam Traps
- Know which direction is "good" for each ratio
- Revenue bonds are not backed by taxes
- Adjust debt for leases and pension deficits

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Corvane ratios (in millions).** EBITDA 400, EBIT 300, interest 60, FFO 260, debt 1,200, cash 200, equity 1,800.
- Debt/EBITDA = 3.00×; net debt/EBITDA = 1,000/400 = 2.50×.
- FFO/debt = 21.7%; debt/capital = 1,200/3,000 = 40.0%.
- EBITDA/interest = 6.67×; EBIT/interest = 5.00×.

**Example 2 - debt-financed buyback.** Borrow 300 at 5% and buy back stock.
- Debt 1,500; interest 75; equity 1,500.
- Debt/EBITDA = 3.75×; EBITDA/interest = 5.33×; debt/capital = 50.0%.
- Conclusion: credit quality weakens on every measure.

**Example 3 - revenue bond.** A toll road generates net revenue of 45m against annual debt service of 30m.
- DSCR = 45/30 = 1.50×. If traffic falls 20%, net revenue is 36m and DSCR = 1.20×, which leaves a thinner cushion.

## Questions to ask students
- Why might a sovereign's local-currency rating be higher than its foreign-currency rating? (It can raise taxes or create its own currency to pay local-currency debt.)
- Which is riskier from the same city: a GO bond or a revenue bond for a new stadium? (Usually the revenue bond, because it relies on a single project.)
- Debt/EBITDA goes from 2× to 4×. Better or worse for credit? (Worse, because leverage has doubled.)
- Which C does a restriction on additional borrowing relate to? (Covenants.)
- Why add operating leases and pension deficits to debt? (They are fixed obligations that compete with bondholders for cash.)

## Analogies
- Mortgage application: a bank asks a household about income (capacity), savings (capital), the house (collateral), loan terms (covenants) and credit history (character).
- Country as household with a printing press: it can always pay bills written in its own currency, but not bills in someone else's.
- Seesaw between shareholders and bondholders: a buyback lifts one end and lowers the other.

## Recording tips
- Put a green "higher is better" or red "higher is worse" label next to each ratio on screen.
- Do the buyback as a before/after table. The visual comparison makes the point instantly.
- Keep sovereign analysis brisk and qualitative; spend the time on ratio interpretation.
