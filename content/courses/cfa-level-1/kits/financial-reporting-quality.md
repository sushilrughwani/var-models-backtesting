## Slides
### Slide 1: Two questions about any report
- Is it faithful? (reporting quality)
- Are the results sustainable and adequate? (earnings quality)
- Honest bad news beats flattering fiction

### Slide 2: The quality spectrum
- Decision-useful, sustainable → decision-useful, unsustainable
- Biased (aggressive or conservative) → managed earnings
- Non-compliant → fictitious

### Slide 3: Conservative is not the same as good
- Reserves built in good years can be released later
- Smoothing hides real volatility
- Bias in either direction reduces usefulness

### Slide 4: The fraud triangle
- Motivation: targets, covenants, bonuses
- Opportunity: weak controls, complex estimates, dominant management
- Rationalisation: "it's only temporary"

### Slide 5: Revenue red flags
- Early recognition, bill-and-hold, channel stuffing
- Gross vs net revenue as an agent
- Receivables growing faster than sales

### Slide 6: Expense and balance sheet red flags
- Capitalising operating costs; stretching useful lives
- Thin reserves for bad debts, warranties, obsolescence
- Goodwill never impaired despite weak performance

### Slide 7: Cash flow red flags
- Stretching payables, factoring receivables
- Operating outflows classified as investing
- CFO persistently below net income

### Slide 8: Screening metrics
- DSO = AR / revenue × 365
- CFO / NI below 1 for several years
- Accruals ratio = (NI − CFO − CFI) / average NOA

### Slide 9: Kestrel Ltd results
- Revenue +10%, receivables +66.7%
- DSO 43.8 → 66.4 days
- CFO/NI 1.13 → 0.42

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Kestrel Ltd** (millions): revenue 1,000 → 1,100; AR 120 → 200; NI 80 → 95; CFO 90 → 40.
- Revenue growth = 10%; AR growth = 80/120 = 66.7%
- DSO Y1 = 120/1,000 × 365 = 43.8; DSO Y2 = 200/1,100 × 365 = 66.4
- CFO/NI Y1 = 1.125 (≈1.13); Y2 = 0.42
- NI − CFO: Y1 = −10; Y2 = 55 → about 5.5% of 1,000 average assets
- Verdict: earnings growth not backed by cash; investigate revenue recognition

**Example 2 - Cash-flow accruals ratio.** NI 500, CFO 300, CFI −150, average net operating assets 2,500.
- Accruals = 500 − 300 − (−150) = 350
- Ratio = 350 / 2,500 = 14%
- Compare with peers and with the company's own history; a rising ratio is a warning sign

## Questions to ask students
- A company reports a loss with complete, transparent disclosure. High or low reporting quality? (High reporting quality, low earnings quality.)
- Name a way management can boost CFO without improving operations. (Stretch payables, sell receivables, or reclassify operating outflows as investing.)
- Why might a large reserve release be a concern? (It may reflect earlier over-reserving used to smooth earnings.)
- DSO jumps from 44 to 66 days while revenue grows 10%. What two explanations come to mind? (Looser credit terms or premature/fictitious revenue recognition.)
- Is GAAP compliance enough to guarantee quality? (No - biased and managed earnings can be compliant.)

## Analogies
- Earnings are the restaurant's menu photo; cash flow is the plate that actually arrives. When the two diverge, trust the plate.
- The fraud triangle is like fire: it needs fuel (motivation), oxygen (opportunity) and a spark (rationalisation). Remove one and it is much harder to ignite.
- Cookie-jar reserves are a savings jar management fills in good years and raids in bad years, making performance look smoother than it is.

## Recording tips
- Open with the Kestrel numbers on screen before explaining anything; let students react first.
- Use the spectrum as a visual ladder and refer back to it whenever a technique is discussed.
- Stress trend analysis and peer comparison; one data point is rarely a red flag on its own.
