## Slides
### Slide 1: Hedge Funds and Digital Assets
- Two of the newest and least regulated corners of alternatives
- Hedge funds: flexible strategies, high fees
- Digital assets: new technology, extreme volatility

### Slide 2: What Makes a Hedge Fund
- Private, lightly regulated pooled vehicle
- Can short, use leverage and derivatives
- Management + incentive fees, lockups, limited transparency

### Slide 3: Equity Hedge
- Long/short: some net market exposure
- Market neutral: beta near zero
- Short bias and sector specialists

### Slide 4: Event-Driven
- Merger arbitrage: long target, short acquirer in stock deals
- Distressed securities and activist investing
- Key risk: deal failure or adverse outcome

### Slide 5: Relative Value and Opportunistic
- Relative value: convertible arbitrage, fixed income arbitrage; often levered
- Opportunistic: global macro, managed futures (trend-following)

### Slide 6: Multi-Manager
- Multi-strategy: internal teams, one fee layer
- Fund of funds: external managers, diversification and due diligence
- Costs: second fee layer and netting risk

### Slide 7: Merger Arbitrage Math
- Spread = k x P_acq - P_target
- Earn spread if deal closes; large loss if it breaks
- Expected payoff = p x spread + (1 - p) x loss

### Slide 8: Distributed Ledgers
- Shared database copied across participants
- Blockchain: blocks linked by cryptographic hashes
- Permissionless (open) vs permissioned (restricted)

### Slide 9: Consensus Mechanisms
- Proof of work: computing race, energy intensive
- Proof of stake: validators lock up holdings, low energy

### Slide 10: Types of Digital Assets
- Cryptocurrencies, stablecoins
- Utility and security tokens, NFTs
- DeFi applications built on smart contracts

### Slide 11: Access and Risks
- Direct (exchanges, wallets) or indirect (futures, ETPs, trusts, funds, related equities)
- Volatility, no cash flows, fraud and hacking, custody, regulation

### Slide 12: Recap
- Classify strategies into the five groups
- Merger arb = small gain, big loss tail
- Know PoW vs PoS and the main token types

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: Stock-for-stock merger arbitrage.** Offer 0.8 Delta shares per Target share. Delta 60.00, Target 45.00. Buy 1 Target, short 0.8 Delta.
- Offer value = 48.00; spread = 3.00; return if deal closes = 6.67%.
- If the deal fails: Target to 38.00 (-7.00), Delta to 62.00 (short loses 0.8 x 2 = 1.60). Total -8.60 (about -19.1% of the 45.00 invested).
- With 85% completion probability: 0.85 x 3.00 + 0.15 x (-8.60) = 1.26 expected profit per Target share.

**Example 2: Fee layering.** USD 100 in a fund of funds; underlying fund earns 20% gross with 2% (year-end) and 20% incentive; FoF charges 1% and 10%.
- Underlying: 120 - 2.40 = 117.60; incentive 3.52; NAV 114.08.
- FoF: 114.08 - 1.14 = 112.94; incentive 1.29; NAV 111.65.
- Net 11.65% vs 20% gross: fees absorb over 40% of the gross gain.

## Questions to ask students
- What distinguishes market neutral from long/short equity? (Market neutral targets near-zero beta; long/short keeps some net exposure.)
- Why does a cash merger arbitrage trade not need a short leg? (The payoff is a fixed cash amount, so there is no acquirer price risk to hedge.)
- What is netting risk in a fund of funds? (Paying incentive fees to winning managers even when losers make the total portfolio flat.)
- Which consensus mechanism is more energy intensive? (Proof of work.)
- What gives a stablecoin its stability, and what is its weakness? (Reserves backing it; its value depends on the quality and existence of those reserves.)

## Analogies
- Merger arbitrage is picking up coins in front of a steamroller: steady small gains with an occasional crushing loss.
- A fund of funds is a restaurant critic who charges you for picking restaurants, on top of each restaurant's bill.
- A blockchain is a shared notebook where every page is stamped with a fingerprint of the previous page, so tearing out a page is instantly noticed.

## Recording tips
- Spend the most time on strategy classification; exam questions often describe a trade and ask for its group.
- Show the merger arbitrage payoff as two branches (deal closes vs fails) to make the asymmetry visual.
- Keep the digital assets section factual and neutral; avoid price predictions or endorsing specific coins.
