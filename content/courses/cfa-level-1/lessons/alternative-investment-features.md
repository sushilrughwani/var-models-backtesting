## Learning goals
- Describe the features that set alternative investments apart from traditional stocks and bonds.
- Compare direct investing, co-investing and fund investing in terms of cost, control, diversification and expertise required.
- Explain the roles of the general partner and limited partners in a limited partnership.
- Interpret common fund terms such as commitments, capital calls, lockups, side letters and the distribution waterfall.
- Calculate the net return an investor earns through a fund versus a fee-free co-investment.

## Explanation

### What makes an investment "alternative"?
Traditional investing means long-only positions in listed shares, bonds and cash. Alternatives cover private equity and debt, real estate, infrastructure, natural resources and hedge funds. They tend to share these features:

- **Illiquidity**: assets may take years to sell, and fund units often cannot be redeemed freely.
- **Specialist management**: returns depend heavily on the manager's skill in sourcing, structuring and improving assets.
- **Low transparency**: valuations are often based on appraisals or models rather than market trades, and reporting is less frequent.
- **High minimum investments and complex fees**: a management fee plus a share of profits is typical.
- **Low measured correlation with traditional assets**, partly an artefact of smoothed valuations.
- **Lighter regulation** and few reliable benchmarks.

Investors accept these drawbacks hoping for an illiquidity premium, unique return sources and diversification.

### Three ways to invest
**Fund investing** is the most common route. The investor commits capital to a pooled vehicle and the manager selects, manages and exits the assets. It needs the least expertise and diversifies across many deals, but carries full fees and gives no say over individual deals.

**Co-investing** means investing in a fund and also being offered the chance to put extra money directly alongside the fund into a specific deal. Co-investment capital usually carries reduced or zero fees. The risks are adverse selection (the manager may offer the less attractive deals) and concentration.

**Direct investing** means the investor buys assets itself, as some large pension and sovereign funds do. It gives maximum control and the lowest fees but needs a large skilled team and offers the least diversification.

A useful rule: as you move from fund to co-investment to direct, **cost falls and control rises, but required expertise rises and diversification falls**.

### The limited partnership structure
Most private funds are organised as **limited partnerships**. The **general partner (GP)** is the manager: it makes all investment decisions, runs the fund and, in principle, has unlimited liability. **Limited partners (LPs)** are outside investors such as pensions and endowments. They provide most of the capital, take no part in management and their liability is limited to their commitment.

Key terms are set out in the **limited partnership agreement (LPA)**:

- **Committed capital**: the total the LP promises; the GP draws it down through **capital calls** as deals are found.
- **Lockup period**: the time during which investors cannot redeem.
- **Side letters**: side agreements giving particular LPs special terms, for example lower fees or extra reporting.
- **Distribution waterfall**: the order in which cash goes to LPs and the GP (covered next lesson).
- **Clawback**: a provision requiring the GP to return incentive fees previously paid if later losses mean it was overpaid.

### Measuring the fee drag
The investor's net return through a fund is the gross asset return minus the management fee and the incentive fee. If the incentive fee is charged on the gain after the management fee:

$$
\text{Net gain} = G - M - p\,(G - M)
$$

where $G$ is the gross gain in currency units, $M$ is the management fee and $p$ is the incentive fee rate.

## Worked example
A foundation invests USD 20 million in a buyout fund and, alongside it, USD 5 million as a fee-free co-investment in one of the fund's deals. Assume every asset earns a gross return of 18% this year. The fund charges a 1.5% management fee on invested capital and a 20% incentive fee on gains net of the management fee.

**Fund investment**
1. Gross gain: $20 \times 0.18 = 3.60$ million.
2. Management fee: $20 \times 0.015 = 0.30$ million.
3. Gain after management fee: $3.60 - 0.30 = 3.30$ million.
4. Incentive fee: $0.20 \times 3.30 = 0.66$ million.
5. Net gain: $3.30 - 0.66 = 2.64$ million, a net return of $2.64 / 20 = 13.2\%$.

**Co-investment**
6. No fees, so the net gain is $5 \times 0.18 = 0.90$ million, a return of 18.0%.

**Total programme**
7. Total net gain: $2.64 + 0.90 = 3.54$ million on 25 million invested, so the blended net return is **14.16%**.

Co-investing lifted the blended return by almost one percentage point versus the 13.2% fund-only result.

## Common exam traps
- Mixing up the roles: the GP manages and has unlimited liability; LPs are passive and their liability is capped at their commitment.
- Thinking co-investing means investing without a fund. Co-investment rights normally come from being an LP in the manager's fund.
- Assuming low reported correlation proves true diversification; appraisal-based smoothing understates both volatility and correlation.
- Charging the incentive fee on the gross gain when the question says it is calculated net of the management fee.
- Confusing committed capital (the promise) with invested or called capital (the money actually drawn).

## Key takeaways
- Alternatives are typically illiquid, opaque, specialist-managed, lightly regulated and expensive, but can offer diversification and an illiquidity premium.
- Fund investing is cheapest in expertise but most expensive in fees; direct investing is the reverse; co-investing sits in between.
- Limited partnerships split roles between the managing GP and passive LPs with limited liability.
- The LPA sets commitments, capital calls, lockups, side letters, the waterfall and clawback terms.
- Fees can absorb a large share of gross returns, so always compute net returns step by step.
