## Learning goals
- Describe the purpose of the Code of Ethics and how it differs from the Standards of Professional Conduct.
- List the six broad principles of the Code in your own words.
- Name the seven Standards and the sub-sections each contains.
- Explain how CFA Institute's professional conduct process investigates and sanctions violations.

## Explanation

### Two layers: principles and rules
CFA Institute's ethical framework has two layers. The **Code of Ethics** is short and aspirational: a handful of principles that describe the kind of professional a member or candidate should be. The **Standards of Professional Conduct** translate those principles into specific, testable obligations. Think of the Code as a constitution and the Standards as the laws written under it.

Both apply to every **member** of CFA Institute and every **candidate** in the CFA Program. Candidates are bound from the moment they enrol, not only after they earn the charter.

### The Code in plain language
Members and candidates are expected to:
1. Behave with integrity, competence, diligence and respect, and act ethically toward clients, prospects, employers, colleagues and other market participants.
2. Put the integrity of the profession and the interests of clients ahead of their own interests.
3. Use reasonable care and independent professional judgment in analysis, recommendations and actions.
4. Practise professionally and ethically themselves, and encourage others to do the same, in a way that reflects well on them and the profession.
5. Promote the integrity and viability of global capital markets.
6. Maintain and improve their professional competence and strive to do the same for other professionals.

Notice the ordering embedded in principle 2: **profession and clients first, self last.** You will see this priority again and again in the Standards.

### The seven Standards
| Standard | Theme | Sub-sections |
|---|---|---|
| I | Professionalism | Knowledge of the law; Independence and objectivity; Misrepresentation; Misconduct; Competence |
| II | Integrity of capital markets | Material nonpublic information; Market manipulation |
| III | Duties to clients | Loyalty, prudence and care; Fair dealing; Suitability; Performance presentation; Preservation of confidentiality |
| IV | Duties to employers | Loyalty; Additional compensation arrangements; Responsibilities of supervisors |
| V | Investment analysis, recommendations and actions | Diligence and reasonable basis; Communication with clients and prospects; Record retention |
| VI | Conflicts of interest | Disclosure of conflicts; Priority of transactions; Referral fees |
| VII | Responsibilities as a CFA Institute member or candidate | Conduct in CFA Institute programs; Reference to CFA Institute, the designation and the program |

A memory hook for the order: **P**lease **I**nvest **C**arefully, **E**veryone **I**nspects **C**FA **R**ules - Professionalism, Integrity, Clients, Employers, Investment analysis, Conflicts, Responsibilities.

### How violations are handled
CFA Institute runs a **Professional Conduct Program** under formal rules of procedure. The process works roughly like this:

- **Triggers.** An inquiry can start from a member's own disclosure on the annual professional conduct statement, a written complaint, reports in the media or public records, reports from exam proctors, or analysis of exam materials and scores.
- **Investigation.** Professional conduct staff gather documents and may interview the member, candidate or witnesses.
- **Outcomes after investigation.** Staff may conclude that no sanction is warranted, issue a cautionary letter, continue investigating, or propose a disciplinary sanction.
- **Acceptance or hearing.** If a sanction is proposed, the individual may accept it. If they reject it, the matter goes to a hearing panel drawn from a volunteer disciplinary review committee, which decides whether a violation occurred and what sanction applies.
- **Sanctions.** For members these range from public censure through suspension of membership and the right to use the designation, up to revocation. For candidates, sanctions can include suspension or a ban from taking exams.

The point of the program is **self-regulation**: the profession policing itself to protect its credibility.

## Worked example
**Scenario.** Felix Amadi, a CFA Level II candidate at the fictional Saltmarsh Securities, discloses on his annual professional conduct statement that a regulator recently fined him for failing to supervise a junior broker.

**Step 1 - Does the framework apply to Felix?** Yes. He is a candidate, and candidates are bound by the Code and Standards from enrolment.

**Step 2 - What triggered review?** His own self-disclosure on the professional conduct statement, one of the standard sources of inquiry.

**Step 3 - Which Standard is most relevant?** Failure to supervise falls under Standard IV, Duties to Employers, specifically responsibilities of supervisors.

**Step 4 - Possible outcomes.** After investigating, staff could close the matter, send a cautionary letter, or propose a sanction such as suspension from the exam program. If Felix rejects a proposed sanction, a hearing panel decides.

**Conclusion: Felix is subject to the Professional Conduct Program as a candidate, and the matter is assessed under the supervisory responsibilities part of Standard IV.**

## Common exam traps
- Thinking candidates are not bound until they become charterholders. They are bound from enrolment.
- Confusing the Code (broad principles) with the Standards (specific obligations).
- Misplacing sub-sections, e.g. putting priority of transactions under Duties to Clients rather than Conflicts of Interest, or record retention under Duties to Employers rather than Standard V.
- Assuming a proposed sanction is final. The individual may reject it and request a hearing panel.

## Key takeaways
- The Code sets six aspirational principles; the Standards give seven areas of specific obligations.
- Clients and the profession come before personal interest.
- Memorise the seven Standards and their sub-sections; exam questions often hinge on which Standard applies.
- Members and candidates are equally bound.
- Self-regulation through the Professional Conduct Program protects the value of the designation.
