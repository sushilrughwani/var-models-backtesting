---
title: "Standard I: Professionalism"
---

## Learning goals
- Apply the "stricter rule wins" principle when local law and the Standards differ.
- Identify threats to independence and objectivity, including gifts, issuer-paid research and sales pressure.
- Recognise misrepresentation, including plagiarism, omissions and return guarantees.
- Distinguish professional misconduct from purely private behaviour.
- Explain what the competence requirement asks of members and candidates.

## Explanation
Standard I is about being a professional in the fullest sense: obeying the law, thinking independently, telling the truth, behaving honestly and staying capable. It has five parts.

### A. Knowledge of the law
You must understand and comply with all applicable laws, rules and regulations, **and** with the Code and Standards. When they conflict, follow **whichever is stricter**. If local law permits something the Standards forbid, follow the Standards. If local law forbids something the Standards allow, follow the law.

You must not knowingly participate in or assist others' violations. If you discover wrongdoing, first raise it with a supervisor or compliance. If it continues, **dissociate**: refuse to work on the matter, and in serious cases resign. Reporting to regulators is often wise and sometimes legally required, but the Standards themselves do not demand it. Simply staying silent while remaining involved is not enough.

### B. Independence and objectivity
Use reasonable care to keep your judgment independent, and never offer or accept gifts or benefits that could reasonably be expected to compromise your own or someone else's objectivity. Key distinctions:

- **Gifts from clients** (thanks for past service) are less of a threat than gifts from parties trying to influence future decisions, such as issuers or brokers. Client gifts should still be disclosed to your employer.
- **Modest, customary** business entertainment is acceptable; lavish trips are not.
- **Issuer-paid research** is permitted if you are paid a flat fee unrelated to your conclusions and the arrangement is clearly disclosed.
- When visiting a company, pay for your own travel and use commercial transport where possible.
- Pressure from investment banking, sales colleagues or major clients to change a rating must be resisted.

### C. Misrepresentation
Do not knowingly misrepresent facts about investment analysis, recommendations, actions or professional activities. This covers:
- False statements about your qualifications, services, or past performance.
- **Omissions** of material information that make a statement misleading.
- **Plagiarism** - using others' research or ideas without credit. You may cite recognised public sources such as published economic statistics without naming each one, but you must credit identifiable authors.
- **Guarantees** of investment returns on risky assets. You may describe an instrument that is genuinely guaranteed by its structure, such as an insured deposit, as guaranteed.

### D. Misconduct
Do not engage in professional conduct involving dishonesty, fraud or deceit, or commit any act that reflects adversely on your professional reputation, integrity or competence. The standard targets behaviour that undermines your trustworthiness as a professional. Purely personal matters - such as civil disobedience at a peaceful protest - generally do not breach it, whereas lying, cheating, theft or substance abuse that impairs work do.

### E. Competence
Act with and maintain the competence needed to fulfil your professional responsibilities. That means having the knowledge and skills for the role you take on, keeping them current as products and technology change, and declining or seeking help with work beyond your capability.

## Worked example
**Scenario.** Leila Haddad, CFA, covers mining stocks for the fictional Ridgeback Securities in a country where securities law does not restrict analysts from accepting gifts. A gold producer she covers invites her on an all-expenses-paid week at a luxury resort "to see the mine." Meanwhile, Ridgeback's sales head asks her to raise her rating to "buy" because a large client holds the stock.

**Step 1 - Which rule applies?** Local law is silent on gifts; the Standards restrict gifts that could compromise objectivity. The Standards are stricter, so Leila follows them.

**Step 2 - Evaluate the trip.** Visiting the mine has a legitimate purpose, but a luxury week paid by the issuer is a lavish benefit likely to compromise objectivity. Leila should decline the resort portion, pay her own travel, and use commercial transport where possible.

**Step 3 - Evaluate the rating pressure.** Changing a rating to please a client violates the independence and objectivity requirement. Leila keeps the rating her analysis supports.

**Step 4 - Misrepresentation check.** If she published "buy" without believing it, she would also misrepresent her analysis.

**Conclusion: Leila should visit the mine at her own firm's expense, decline the luxury hospitality, and maintain her independent rating.**

## Common exam traps
- Following local law when the Standards are stricter, or vice versa. Always apply the stricter rule.
- Believing you must report violations to authorities. The Standards require dissociation, not external reporting.
- Treating all gifts the same. Client gifts for past service carry less risk than issuer or broker gifts seeking future influence, though they should still be disclosed to the employer.
- Assuming any guarantee language is banned. Describing a genuinely guaranteed product as such is acceptable.
- Classifying personal, non-professional behaviour as misconduct when it does not involve dishonesty or affect professional integrity.

## Key takeaways
- When law and the Standards differ, the stricter one governs.
- Dissociate from violations; report internally first.
- Protect objectivity: modest gifts only, flat-fee and disclosed issuer-paid research, pay your own way.
- Never misrepresent, plagiarise or guarantee uncertain returns.
- Misconduct focuses on dishonesty and conduct reflecting on professional integrity.
- Stay competent for the work you take on.
