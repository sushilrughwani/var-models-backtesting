---
title: "Standards V-VII: Analysis, Conflicts and Member Responsibilities"
---

## Learning goals
- Explain what diligence and a reasonable basis require, including use of third-party research and quantitative models.
- Describe what must be communicated to clients about process, risks and changes, and how to separate fact from opinion.
- State record-retention expectations.
- Apply the rules on disclosing conflicts, priority of transactions and referral fees.
- Identify correct and incorrect ways to refer to CFA Institute, the designation and the CFA Program.

## Explanation
### V(A) Diligence and reasonable basis
Be diligent, independent and thorough, and have a **reasonable and adequate basis**, supported by research, for any analysis, recommendation or action.

- **Third-party research**: you may rely on it after making reasonable checks of its soundness - its assumptions, rigour, timeliness and the provider's independence.
- **Quantitative models**: understand the assumptions and limitations of models you use, and test them under a range of scenarios, including stressed ones.
- **Group research**: if you disagree with a committee's recommendation but the process was sound and the basis reasonable, you may stay associated with it. You may document your dissent.

### V(B) Communication with clients and prospective clients
- Disclose the **basic format and general principles** of your investment process, and promptly inform clients of **significant changes** to it.
- Explain the **significant limitations and risks** of the strategy.
- **Distinguish fact from opinion.** Forecasts, price targets and projections must be presented as opinions.

### V(C) Record retention
Keep adequate records supporting your analysis, recommendations and actions, and client communications. Records belong to the **employer**. Follow local regulation; where none exists, a commonly recommended minimum is **seven years**.

### VI(A) Disclosure of conflicts
Make **full and fair disclosure** of all matters that could reasonably be expected to impair independence and objectivity or interfere with duties to clients, prospects and the employer. Disclosures must be **prominent** and in **plain language**. Typical conflicts: owning shares you recommend, board seats, or pay tied to banking deals.

### VI(B) Priority of transactions
**Clients first, employer second, you last.** Client and employer transactions take priority over personal transactions where you are a beneficial owner. You must not trade ahead of client orders (front-running).

- Family accounts that are genuine **fee-paying client accounts** should be treated like any other client - neither favoured nor disadvantaged.
- Firm controls include pre-clearance, blackout periods and limits on IPO participation.

### VI(C) Referral fees
Disclose to your employer, clients and prospects, as appropriate, any compensation, consideration or benefit received from or paid to others for recommending products or services. Disclose the **nature and estimated value** before the client enters into the arrangement, so they can judge any bias.

### VII(A) Conduct in CFA Institute programs
Do not engage in conduct that compromises the reputation or integrity of CFA Institute or the designation, or the integrity, validity or security of the exams. That includes cheating, disclosing **specific** exam content (questions, topics or formulas tested) and lying on the professional conduct statement. Criticising CFA Institute's policies is allowed.

### VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute, the designation and the program
- Do not misrepresent or exaggerate what membership, the charter or candidacy means.
- Use **"CFA"** as an adjective: "Ana Silva, CFA" or "a CFA charterholder" - never "a CFA" or "the CFA designation guarantees superior returns."
- There is **no partial designation**. "Passed Level II" is fine; "CFA Level II" as a title is not.
- To use the designation you must keep membership current (pay dues and file the annual professional conduct statement).
- You may call yourself a **candidate** only while enrolled for the next exam.

## Worked example
**Scenario.** Tobias Ekström, CFA, is an equity analyst at the fictional Marlow Securities.

1. He owns 2,000 shares of Halden Tech and plans to issue a buy rating on it.
2. His sister, a fee-paying client, holds a discretionary account he manages.
3. He receives a fee from a law firm for each client he refers.
4. His email signature reads "Tobias Ekström, a CFA - Level III passed."

**Step 1 - Ownership.** He must prominently disclose his holding in the report (VI(A)) and must not trade his own shares ahead of clients when the rating is released (VI(B)).

**Step 2 - Sister's account.** Treat it like any other client account: include it in block trades and allocations normally.

**Step 3 - Referral fee.** Disclose its nature and estimated value to his employer and to clients before they engage the law firm (VI(C)).

**Step 4 - Signature.** "A CFA" uses the mark as a noun, and "Level III passed" alongside it is confusing. Correct form: "Tobias Ekström, CFA" (VII(B)).

**Conclusion: Tobias must disclose his holding and the referral fee, trade only after clients, treat his sister's account normally and fix his signature to "Tobias Ekström, CFA."**

## Common exam traps
- Thinking disclosure cures everything. Some conflicts must be avoided, and personal trades still come after clients.
- Excluding family client accounts from block trades. They are clients and should be treated equally.
- Believing you cannot stay associated with group research you disagree with. You can, if the process was sound.
- Writing "CFA" as a noun or claiming partial designations such as "CFA Level I."

## Key takeaways
- Have a reasonable, well-researched basis; understand models and vet third-party work.
- Communicate process, risks and changes; separate fact from opinion.
- Retain records (seven years absent other rules); they belong to the employer.
- Disclose conflicts prominently and plainly; clients and employer trade before you.
- Disclose referral fees, including estimated value, before the client engages.
- Use the designation correctly and protect exam integrity.
