## Slides
### Slide 1: Why Build a Model?
- Turn assumptions into forecast statements
- Answer a decision: invest, lend, buy, budget
- Make cause and effect visible and testable
### Slide 2: The Model Family
- Three-statement: the engine
- DCF: intrinsic value from forecast free cash flow
- LBO: debt capacity and sponsor returns
- Merger: EPS accretion or dilution; budget: internal planning
### Slide 3: Meet Northwind Components Ltd (USD m)
- FY2025 revenue 500.0, COGS 290.0, SG&A 110.0
- One-off restructuring 6.0, D&A 25.0, interest 9.0
- Tax rate 25%, dividend payout 30%
### Slide 4: Anatomy of a Three-Statement Model
- Inputs and assumptions
- Historical statements
- Supporting schedules: revenue, costs, working capital, capex, debt
- Forecast statements, then checks and outputs
### Slide 5: The Critical Links
- Net income to retained earnings and to cash flow
- Non-cash items added back
- Balance sheet changes become cash flows
- Closing cash returns to the balance sheet
### Slide 6: Retained Earnings Roll-Forward
- Closing RE = opening RE + net income - dividends
- Northwind FY2025: 105.9 + 45.0 - 13.5 = 137.4
### Slide 7: Flex Test: D&A up 10
- Net income -7.5 (after 25% tax)
- Operating cash flow +2.5
- PP&E -10, cash +2.5, equity -7.5: still balances
### Slide 8: What a Model Is Not
- Not a forecast of certainty
- Balancing does not mean right
- Assumptions need evidence
### Slide 9: Key Takeaways
- Start from the decision
- Three statements underpin every valuation model
- Learn to trace one change through all statements

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: Northwind FY2025 net income.** EBITDA = 500.0 - 290.0 - 110.0 = 100.0. EBIT = 100.0 - 6.0 - 25.0 = 69.0. EBT = 69.0 - 9.0 = 60.0. Tax 25% = 15.0. Net income = 45.0. Dividends 30% = 13.5. Retained earnings: 105.9 + 45.0 - 13.5 = 137.4.

**Example 2: D&A flex.** D&A +10.0. EBIT -10.0, tax -2.5, net income -7.5. Cash flow: -7.5 + 10.0 = +2.5. Balance sheet: PP&E -10.0 and cash +2.5 gives assets -7.5; retained earnings -7.5. Balanced.

## Questions to ask students
- If depreciation goes up, does the company have more or less cash? (More, by the tax saving: D&A times the tax rate.)
- Which model would a private equity fund use to size acquisition debt? (An LBO model.)
- What is the one line that connects the income statement to the balance sheet? (Net income into retained earnings.)
- Where does balance sheet cash come from in a forecast? (The closing cash from the cash flow statement.)
- Can a model that balances still be wrong? (Yes; balancing only proves internal consistency, not good assumptions.)

## Analogies
- A model is a flight simulator: you change the weather (assumptions) and see whether the plane (company) lands safely.
- The three statements are three camera angles on the same event; if one angle shows something, the others must agree.
- Retained earnings is a savings jar: profits go in, dividends come out.

## Recording tips
- Keep the Northwind numbers on screen; students will see this company in every lesson.
- Slow down on the D&A flex. It is the moment most students realise depreciation is not a cash cost.
- Draw arrows between the three statements live rather than showing a finished diagram.
