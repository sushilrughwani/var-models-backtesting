## Slides
### Slide 1: Buying the Whole Company
- Trading comps price a minority stake
- Acquirers get control and synergies
- They pay a premium for it

### Slide 2: Control Premium
- Offer price / undisturbed price − 1
- Undisturbed: before rumours and leaks
- Typical range 20-40%, deal dependent

### Slide 3: Deal EV
- Offer price × diluted shares + net debt + NCI
- Treasury stock method at the offer price
- Multiple on LTM EBITDA at announcement

### Slide 4: Tamsin Buys Orrin
- 26.00 per share; 40.0 m basic + 1.0 m option shares
- Equity 1,066 + net debt 182 = EV 1,248
- 1,248 / 120 = 10.4x; premium 30.0%

### Slide 5: Relevance Filters
- Recent, same sector, similar size
- Strategic versus financial buyer
- Flag cycle peaks, distressed sales, bidding wars

### Slide 6: Northwind Precedents
- 9.5x, 10.4x, 11.0x, 9.8x, 10.9x
- Quartiles 9.8x and 10.9x; median 10.4x
- Median premium 30%

### Slide 7: Implied Value
- LTM EBITDA 200; bridge −313
- Median: (1,767 + 80) / 54.6 = 33.83
- Range USD 31.63 to 35.66

### Slide 8: Premium Cross-Check
- 27.50 × 1.30 = 35.75
- Above the trading comps range
- Consistent with a control value

### Slide 9: Traps
- Leak-inflated reference price
- LTM multiple on forward EBITDA
- Basic shares; stale deals

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Orrin deal multiple.**
- Option shares = 2.6 × (1 − 16/26) = 1.0 million; diluted 41.0 million
- Equity = 26 × 41 = 1,066; EV = 1,066 + 182 = 1,248; multiple = 1,248 / 120 = 10.4x
- With basic shares only: (1,040 + 182) / 120 = 10.18x (understated)

**Example 2 - Premium measurement.**
- Undisturbed USD 20.00, offer USD 26.00: premium 30.0%
- If rumours lifted the price to USD 23.00 the day before: 26/23 − 1 = 13.0%, a misleading figure

**Example 3 - Northwind range.**
- 9.8x: 1,960 − 313 = 1,647; (1,647 + 80) / 54.6 = 31.63
- 10.9x: 2,180 − 313 = 1,867; (1,867 + 80) / 54.6 = 35.66
- Wrong: 9.8x on forward EBITDA 219 gives 35.04

## Questions to ask students
- Why are precedent multiples usually higher than trading multiples? (They include a control premium and often synergies.)
- Which share price is the right base for a premium? (The undisturbed price, before rumours.)
- A deal from 2021, when rates were near zero, shows 14x. How do you treat it? (Show it, but give it less weight or exclude it; conditions differ.)
- Why use the treasury stock method at the offer price? (Options are cashed out at the deal price.)
- Would you use the precedent range to decide whether to buy Northwind shares on the market? (Not on its own: it reflects control value, not minority value.)

## Analogies
- Buying a whole apartment block versus a single flat: control lets you renovate and cut costs, so you pay more per flat.
- The undisturbed price as a "before" photo: compare the offer with the photo taken before anyone started primping.
- Precedents as past auction results: useful, but a sale during a boom says little about today's market.

## Recording tips
- Rebuild the Orrin deal step by step; students often have never computed a deal EV from offer terms.
- Put trading comps and precedent ranges side by side to make the control premium visible.
- Stress LTM-to-LTM consistency; it is the most common practical mistake.
