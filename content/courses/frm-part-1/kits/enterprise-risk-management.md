## Slides
### Slide 1: Silos versus the enterprise
- Separate teams for rates, credit, insurance
- Over-hedging of offsetting risks
- Missed concentrations when risks move together
### Slide 2: What ERM is
- Firm-wide, integrated view of all material risks
- Consistent measurement and aggregation
- Managed against board-approved risk appetite
### Slide 3: Why ERM pays
- Capital efficiency through diversification
- Lower distress costs, smoother earnings
- Risk-informed strategy and clear disclosure
### Slide 4: Framework components
- Governance: board, risk committee, independent CRO
- Risk appetite cascaded into limits
- Identification, measurement, aggregation, reporting
### Slide 5: Aggregation formula
- $EC_{total} = \sqrt{\sum_i\sum_j \rho_{ij} EC_i EC_j}$
- Benefit = sum of stand-alone minus total
- Correlation 1 means no benefit
### Slide 6: Worked example
- Market 300, credit 400, operational 200
- Correlations 0.5, 0.2, 0.3
- Total 694.26, benefit 205.74 (22.86%)
### Slide 7: Caveats
- Assumes normal-like distributions
- Correlations rise in crises
- Fat tails can make the formula understate risk
### Slide 8: Scenario analysis
- Severe, plausible, consistent narratives
- Covers risks without historical data
- Linked to pre-agreed management actions
### Slide 9: Risk culture
- Bad news travels up quickly
- Risk managers can challenge revenue producers
- Pay tied to risk-adjusted performance
### Slide 10: Emerging risks
- Cyber, climate (physical and transition), AI and model risk
- Geopolitical, supply chain, pandemics
- Third-party concentration such as cloud providers

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Three-risk aggregation.** Stand-alone: 300, 400, 200; correlations 0.5 (M-C), 0.2 (M-O), 0.3 (C-O). Squares: 90,000 + 160,000 + 40,000 = 290,000. Cross terms: 2(0.5)(300)(400) = 120,000; 2(0.2)(300)(200) = 24,000; 2(0.3)(400)(200) = 48,000; sum 192,000. Total $= \sqrt{482{,}000} = 694.26$. Benefit = 900 - 694.26 = 205.74 (22.86%).

**Example 2 - Two-risk sensitivity.** Stand-alone 600 and 800. At $\rho = 1$: 1,400. At $\rho = 0.25$: $\sqrt{360{,}000 + 640{,}000 + 240{,}000} = \sqrt{1{,}240{,}000} = 1{,}113.55$. At $\rho = 0$: 1,000. Point out how a crisis that pushes correlation from 0.25 towards 1 adds almost 290 of required capital.

## Questions to ask students
- Name two failures of silo risk management. (Over-hedging offsetting risks; missing concentrations.)
- What happens to the diversification benefit when all correlations equal 1? (It disappears.)
- To whom should the CRO have direct access? (The board or its risk committee, and the CEO.)
- Why is scenario analysis especially useful for climate risk? (Little relevant history; outcomes depend on policy paths.)
- Give an example of a sign of weak risk culture. (Staff afraid to escalate losses; pay based on raw revenue.)

## Analogies
- Silo risk management is like five doctors each treating one symptom without talking to each other - the patient may get conflicting prescriptions.
- Diversification benefit is like carpooling: the total fuel bill is lower than everyone driving alone, unless everyone needs to go in different directions at the same moment (correlation shock).
- Risk culture is the immune system of the firm: rules are the medicine, but culture decides whether the body fights infection on its own.

## Recording tips
- Build the aggregation formula on screen term by term; students often forget the factor of 2 on cross terms.
- Show the two-risk sensitivity table to make the correlation caveat concrete.
- Link back to the governance lesson (three lines of defence) and forward to the disasters lesson when discussing culture.
