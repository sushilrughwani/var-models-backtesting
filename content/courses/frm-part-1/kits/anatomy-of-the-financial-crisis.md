## Slides
### Slide 1: The puzzle
- Subprime losses large but containable alone
- Yet the whole system froze
- Answer: leverage, maturity mismatch, opacity
### Slide 2: Housing and credit boom
- Low rates, rising prices, global savings
- Low down payments, teaser rates, low documentation
- Originate-to-distribute weakened screening
### Slide 3: Securitization chain
- Mortgages pooled into MBS, tranches into CDOs
- AAA ratings relied on low assumed default correlation
- National price decline broke that assumption
### Slide 4: Fragile funding
- Dealers 30x levered, funded overnight in repo
- SIVs and conduits funded with ABCP
- Long assets, short liabilities
### Slide 5: Haircut arithmetic
- Max assets = equity / haircut
- 5bn equity: 2% haircut supports 250bn
- 8% haircut supports 62.5bn: sell 187.5bn
### Slide 6: 2007 cracks
- August: BNP Paribas freezes three funds; ABCP seizes
- September: Northern Rock depositor run
- Repo haircuts on securitized collateral jump
### Slide 7: 2008 climax
- March: Bear Stearns rescued by JPMorgan with Fed support
- September: Fannie and Freddie, Lehman, AIG, Reserve Primary Fund
- October: TARP and global bank recapitalizations
### Slide 8: LIBOR-OIS spread
- Unsecured bank rate minus expected policy rate
- A few bp before August 2007
- Several hundred bp after Lehman
### Slide 9: Contagion channels
- Fire sales and mark-to-market losses
- Counterparty exposures (AIG's CDS)
- Liquidity hoarding and loss of confidence
### Slide 10: Exam traps
- Not just subprime: funding runs mattered most
- Runs without queues: repo and ABCP
- Bear before Lehman

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Run on repo.** Equity USD 5bn. Haircut 2%: max assets $5/0.02 = 250$. Haircut 8%: $5/0.08 = 62.5$. Forced sales: 187.5. Variation for class: equity 3, haircut from 3% to 12%: $100 \to 25$, sell 75.

**Example 2 - Under-water mortgage.** Price USD 300,000, 5% down, loan 285,000. Prices fall 12%: value 264,000; equity $-21{,}000$. Ask what price fall wipes out the down payment exactly (5%).

**Example 3 - Leverage of a dealer.** At 30x leverage, equity is $1/30 = 3.33\%$ of assets, so a 3.33% fall in asset values eliminates equity.

## Questions to ask students
- Why did the originate-to-distribute model weaken lending standards? (Originators did not bear the default risk of loans they sold.)
- What is a haircut in a repo? (The percentage by which collateral value exceeds the cash lent; the borrower's own equity stake.)
- What does a spike in LIBOR-OIS tell you? (Banks fear lending unsecured to each other: credit and liquidity stress.)
- Which came first, the Bear Stearns rescue or the Lehman bankruptcy? (Bear Stearns, March 2008.)
- Why was opacity so damaging? (Investors could not tell who held losses, so they withdrew from everyone.)

## Analogies
- Repo haircuts are the deposit a lender demands on a rented car: if the deposit jumps from 2% to 8%, you can rent far fewer cars with the same savings.
- The crisis was a food-poisoning scare: a few contaminated batches (subprime), but since nobody knew which shelves held them, shoppers stopped buying everything.
- Maturity mismatch is building a house on a 30-year mortgage that the bank can recall every night.

- Draw a simple balance sheet for a dealer to show how haircuts cap assets.
- Emphasize the shift from "credit losses" to "funding run"; it is the conceptual heart of exam questions.
