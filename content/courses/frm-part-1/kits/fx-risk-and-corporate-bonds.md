## Slides
### Slide 1: FX Risk and Corporate Bonds
- How firms measure and manage currency exposure
- How corporate bonds are structured and priced for credit risk
- Bridges market products and credit risk topics

### Slide 2: Three FX Exposures
- Transaction: contracted foreign currency cash flows
- Translation: converting subsidiaries' accounts
- Economic: long-run competitiveness and cash flows

### Slide 3: Managing FX Risk
- Net offsetting receivables and payables first
- Hedge the net with forwards, futures, options or swaps
- Operational hedges for economic exposure

### Slide 4: Bond Indenture and Covenants
- Indenture = the contract; trustee enforces it
- Affirmative covenants: must do
- Negative covenants: must not do

### Slide 5: Security and Seniority
- Mortgage bonds: real property
- Collateral trust: financial assets; equipment trust: specific equipment
- Debentures unsecured; subordinated debentures rank lower

### Slide 6: Embedded Provisions
- Call: issuer's option, higher yield
- Put and conversion: investor's options, lower yield
- Sinking fund: gradual retirement, lower credit risk

### Slide 7: Credit Ratings
- Investment grade: BBB-/Baa3 and above
- High yield below; fallen angels
- Event risk can trigger sudden downgrades

### Slide 8: Default and Recovery
- Default rates rise steeply down the rating scale
- Recovery depends on seniority and security
- Loss given default = 1 - R

### Slide 9: Spreads and Default Probability
- $s \approx \lambda(1-R)$
- Implied $\lambda \approx s/(1-R)$
- Real spreads also include liquidity and risk premiums

### Slide 10: Recap
- Net, then hedge
- Know which provisions raise or lower yields
- Next: mortgages and mortgage-backed securities

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: Netting and hedging.** US firm: receive EUR 5m, pay EUR 2m in six months. Forward 1.0959 USD/EUR.
- Net long EUR 3m. Forward sale locks in 3.2877m USD.
- Spot at 1.04: unhedged 3.12m; hedge gains about 0.168m.
- Spot at 1.15: unhedged 3.45m; hedge gives up 0.162m. Emphasise: hedging removes variance, not cost.

**Example 2: Implied default probability.** Spread 2.4%, recovery 40%. Lambda = 0.024 / 0.60 = 4.0% per year.

**Example 3: Required spread.** Default probability 2% per year, recovery 35%. Spread = 0.02 x 0.65 = 1.30%.

## Questions to ask students
- A UK parent converts its US subsidiary's balance sheet into GBP. Which exposure is this? (Translation.)
- Why net before hedging? (Offsetting flows hedge each other for free; hedging the gross wastes transaction costs.)
- Who benefits from a call provision? (The issuer; investors demand a higher yield.)
- Which ranks higher in liquidation, a mortgage bond or a subordinated debenture? (The mortgage bond.)
- Why might a spread imply a default probability above the historical default rate? (Spreads include liquidity and risk premiums.)

## Analogies
- Transaction exposure is a specific bill in a foreign currency; translation exposure is the scoreboard changing because the referee changed units; economic exposure is the whole game becoming harder.
- A covenant is a house rule in a rental agreement: no subletting, keep it insured.
- A call provision is a refinancing option on your mortgage; the lender charges you for it through a higher rate.

## Recording tips
- Use a table of provisions with columns "whose option" and "effect on yield"; it answers most exam questions.
- Show both outcomes of the FX hedge so students see the hedge can lose relative to no hedge.
- Keep ratings detail light; the deeper treatment comes in the credit ratings lesson.
