## Slides
### Slide 1: The Governance of Risk Management
- Structures, roles and incentives
- Who decides, who challenges, who checks
- Culture as the glue

### Slide 2: The Board's Risk Duties
- Approve the risk appetite statement
- Ensure independent, competent risk functions
- Challenge management on major risks
- Oversee compensation design

### Slide 3: Board Composition
- Majority of independent directors
- Real risk and financial expertise
- Separate chair and CEO roles

### Slide 4: Board Committees
- Risk committee: appetite, limits, exposures, CRO
- Audit committee: financial reporting, controls, auditors
- Compensation committee: risk-adjusted, long-term pay

### Slide 5: The Chief Risk Officer
- Leads the independent risk function
- Direct access to the board risk committee
- Seniority to veto or escalate; protection from dismissal

### Slide 6: Three Lines of Defence
- First: business units own and manage risk
- Second: risk management and compliance oversee and challenge
- Third: internal audit gives independent assurance

### Slide 7: Pay and Risk-Taking
- Revenue bonuses reward hidden tail risk
- Deferral and equity-linked pay
- Malus (unvested) and clawback (paid)

### Slide 8: Risk Culture
- Leaders model prudent behaviour
- Safe to escalate concerns
- Breaches punished regardless of profit

### Slide 9: Red Flags
- Desks approving their own limits
- CRO reporting only to a business head
- Internal audit reporting to the business it audits
- Star performers exempt from scrutiny

### Slide 10: Recap
- Board sets appetite; committees specialise
- Three lines: own, oversee, assure
- Incentives and culture decide whether rules work

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: Harbourline Bank diagnosis.** Write the facts in a left column: desk head approves own limit increases; CRO reports to CFO, attends risk committee by invitation; cash bonuses on revenue; internal audit reports to head of trading; analyst told not to slow the business.
- First line: risk-taker setting own limits. Fix: second line sets limits within appetite.
- Second line: CRO not independent. Fix: direct standing line to the board risk committee.
- Third line: audit reports to trading. Fix: report to the audit committee.
- Incentives: introduce deferral, equity, risk adjustment, malus and clawback.
- Culture: protect escalation; treat record profits as a trigger for scrutiny.

**Example 2: Sort the roles.** Ask students to place each in a line: equity trader (1st), market risk analyst (2nd), compliance officer (2nd), internal auditor (3rd), branch loan officer (1st).

## Questions to ask students
- Who owns credit risk on a loan the branch just booked? (The first line, the business that booked it.)
- To which committee should internal audit report? (The audit committee.)
- What is the difference between malus and clawback? (Malus reduces unvested awards; clawback recovers pay already received.)
- Why should the chair and CEO roles be separate? (So the person running the firm is not also the one supervising it.)
- A desk has tripled profits in a year. What should the risk function do? (Increase scrutiny; outsized profits often signal hidden risk.)

## Analogies
- Three lines of defence are a football team: players (first line) defend, the coach (second line) sets tactics and shouts warnings, the referee and video review (third line) check whether the rules were followed.
- A CRO without board access is a smoke detector with the battery removed.
- Cash bonuses on revenue are like paying a driver per kilometre and ignoring accidents.

## Recording tips
- Use a single diagram of the three lines with arrows to the board committees and keep it on screen.
- Students mix up the committees; contrast them side by side with one example question each.
- Walk through the Harbourline case slowly and ask viewers to pause and list the problems before revealing them.
