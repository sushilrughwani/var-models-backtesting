## Slides
### Slide 1: Borrow Short, Lend Long
- Deposits are short and liquid; loans are long and illiquid
- The bank earns a spread for transforming maturity and liquidity
- The same transformation creates run risk
### Slide 2: Business Lines
- Retail and wholesale commercial banking
- Investment banking: underwriting, M&A advice, restructuring
- Securities and derivatives trading
### Slide 3: The Profit Engine
- NII = interest income - interest expense
- NIM = NII / assets (or earning assets)
- Add fees, subtract operating costs and loan-loss provisions
### Slide 4: Leverage and ROE
- ROE = net income / equity
- ROE ≈ ROA × (assets / equity)
- Thin equity magnifies both profits and losses
### Slide 5: Four Core Risks
- Credit, market, liquidity, operational
- Expected loss: priced in and provisioned
- Unexpected loss: absorbed by capital
### Slide 6: Banking Book vs Trading Book
- Banking book: loans held to maturity, amortised cost less provisions
- Trading book: positions held for trading, marked to market daily
- Different capital treatment for each
### Slide 7: Deposit Insurance
- Stops runs by guaranteeing small deposits
- Creates moral hazard: depositors stop monitoring
- Countered by risk-based premiums and capital rules
### Slide 8: Originate-to-Distribute
- Make loans, sell them into securitisations
- Frees capital and spreads risk across investors
- Weakens incentives to screen borrowers
### Slide 9: Underwriting Choices
- Firm commitment: bank buys the issue, bears price risk
- Best efforts: bank sells what it can for a fee
- Dutch auction: price set at the highest level that clears all shares
### Slide 10: Conflicts of Interest
- Lending, advising, underwriting, research and asset management under one roof
- Risks: dumping unsold issues on clients, misuse of confidential information
- Information barriers and compliance oversight
### Slide 11: Harbour Bank Results
- NII 3.075bn, NIM 3.075%
- ROE 11.95% on ROA of 0.96%
- 5% loan loss wipes out 44% of equity
### Slide 12: Takeaways
- Spread business plus leverage
- Capital is the shock absorber for unexpected loss
- Incentives matter: insurance, securitisation, conflicts

## Whiteboard examples
**1. Harbour Bank.** Assets: loans 70bn at 6%, securities 20bn at 3%, cash 10bn at 0%. Funding: deposits 75bn at 1.5%, wholesale 15bn at 4%, subordinated debt 2bn, equity 8bn. Fees 1.0bn, costs 2.2bn, provisions 0.6bn, tax 25%.
- Interest income = 4.2 + 0.6 = 4.8bn; interest expense = 1.125 + 0.6 = 1.725bn.
- NII = 3.075bn; NIM = 3.075%.
- Pre-tax = 3.075 + 1.0 - 2.2 - 0.6 = 1.275bn; net income = 0.956bn.
- ROE = 0.956 / 8 = 11.95%; ROA = 0.96%.
- Stress: loan loss of 5% × 70 = 3.5bn; equity falls to 4.5bn (-44%).

**2. Firm commitment IPO.** A bank agrees to buy 10m shares from the issuer at USD 24 and plans to resell.
- If the market takes the shares at USD 26: profit = 10m × 2 = USD 20m.
- If the bank can only sell at USD 22: loss = 10m × (-2) = -USD 20m.
- Under best efforts with a 7% fee on shares sold at USD 25 the bank earns 10m × 25 × 0.07 = USD 17.5m with no price risk.

## Questions to ask students
- Why does a bank with high ROE not necessarily have high profitability? (High ROE can come from thin equity, i.e. leverage, rather than a high ROA.)
- Which is covered by capital: expected or unexpected loss? (Unexpected; expected loss is priced and provisioned.)
- Who bears price risk in a best-efforts deal? (The issuer; the bank only earns a fee on what it sells.)
- How does deposit insurance increase risk-taking? (Insured depositors no longer demand higher rates from riskier banks, so the bank keeps the upside of risk.)
- Why might originate-to-distribute worsen credit quality? (The originator does not keep the risk, so it has less reason to screen carefully.)

## Analogies
- A bank is a water tower: it collects many small, constantly flowing deposits and releases them as long, steady pipes of credit. If everyone opens their taps at once, the tower runs dry.
- Equity is the crumple zone of a car: small in size, but it absorbs the crash so that depositors (the passengers) walk away.
- Originate-to-distribute is like a chef who never eats the food: the incentive to check ingredients is weaker.

## Recording tips
- Build the Harbour Bank balance sheet on screen line by line before computing anything; students need to see where 8bn of equity sits relative to 100bn of assets.
- Emphasise the expected-versus-unexpected loss split; it reappears in credit risk and capital lessons.
- Keep regulatory capital detail light here and signpost the later lessons.
