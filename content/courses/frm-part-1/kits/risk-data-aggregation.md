## Slides
### Slide 1: The question nobody could answer
- Group-wide exposure to a failing counterparty
- Data spread across systems, entities and currencies
- Days of manual work produced stale numbers
### Slide 2: Two definitions
- Aggregation: define, gather and process risk data
- Reporting: turn data into decision-ready information
- Both measured against risk appetite
### Slide 3: The four groups
- Governance and infrastructure
- Risk data aggregation capabilities
- Risk reporting practices
- Supervisory review, tools and cooperation
### Slide 4: Governance and infrastructure
- Board and senior management own data quality
- Independent validation of the framework
- Common data dictionary, clear data ownership
### Slide 5: Aggregation capabilities
- Accuracy and integrity: reconciled, largely automated
- Completeness: all entities, business lines, risk types
- Timeliness and adaptability: fast and ad hoc on demand
### Slide 6: Reporting practices
- Accurate and comprehensive
- Clear and useful for the specific audience
- Right frequency, right distribution
### Slide 7: Supervisory review
- Periodic assessment of compliance
- Remedial actions: add-ons, business restrictions
- Home-host cooperation
### Slide 8: Trade-offs
- Accuracy vs timeliness, especially in stress
- Know and document tolerances
- Manual workarounds allowed but controlled
### Slide 9: Worked example
- Loans 250 + derivatives 120 + EUR 70 at 1.10
- Remove 30 double count: total 417 vs limit 400
- Siloed views hid the breach
### Slide 10: Exam traps
- Not just IT: board accountability
- Accuracy appears twice (data and reports)
- Final group belongs to supervisors

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1 - Consolidated exposure.** Loans USD 250m, derivatives USD 120m, subsidiary EUR 70m at 1.10 = USD 77m. Sum = 447. Less 30 double-booked = **USD 417m**, breaching the USD 400m limit by 17.

**Example 2 - Classify the failure.** Ask students to name the principle breached in each case and give answers:
1. A risk report to the board runs to 140 pages of raw tables with no commentary (clarity and usefulness).
2. Exposures in the Asian subsidiary are left out because its system uses different counterparty codes (completeness; also data architecture).
3. During a market shock, the liquidity report still arrives only monthly (frequency / timeliness).
4. The bank cannot produce exposure by industry when the supervisor asks for it within a day (adaptability).

## Questions to ask students
- Who is ultimately responsible for risk data quality? (The board and senior management.)
- Name the four aggregation capabilities. (Accuracy and integrity, completeness, timeliness, adaptability.)
- Is a manual spreadsheet step a breach in itself? (No, if documented, controlled and minimized.)
- Why might a less accurate number be preferred in a crisis? (Timeliness matters more for urgent decisions, within known tolerances.)
- Which group of principles applies to supervisors? (Supervisory review, tools and cooperation.)

## Analogies
- Risk data aggregation is like a family trying to know its total debt: credit cards, car loan and mortgage in different apps and currencies - unless someone consolidates them, nobody sees the full picture.
- A risk report is a dashboard in a car: the driver needs speed and fuel at a glance, not the raw engine telemetry.
- Double counting is like counting the same guest twice because they appear on both the bride's and groom's lists.

## Recording tips
- Keep a four-box diagram of the groups on screen while walking through the principles.
- Emphasize that accuracy appears in both groups 2 and 3; students lose marks here.
- Animate the worked example so each desk's number appears alone first, then the consolidated total.
