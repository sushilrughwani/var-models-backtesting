- 08:00 - Costs of default and why restructuring is preferred

- 13:00 - Whiteboard: implied PD, country risk premium and cost of equity

## Slides
### Slide 1: Country Risk
- Extra risk from a country's economy, politics and laws
- Hits companies even when they are strong
- Determinants, sovereign default, measures, equity implications

### Slide 2: Economic Determinants
- Income level and growth, inflation
- Commodity or single-industry dependence
- External debt, current account, FX reserves

### Slide 3: Political and Legal Determinants
- Stability, corruption, violence, expropriation
- Policy continuity versus abrupt change
- Property rights and contract enforcement

### Slide 4: Why Sovereigns Default
- Foreign-currency debt: cannot print dollars
- Local-currency debt: printing is costly; bank holdings; indexation
- Local-currency rating usually ≥ foreign-currency rating

### Slide 5: Costs of Default
- Lost market access and higher future borrowing costs
- Banking system damage, weaker currency, trade disruption
- Political consequences; restructuring preferred to repudiation

### Slide 6: Measuring Country Risk
- Risk scores from commercial services
- Sovereign ratings (TTC, can lag)
- Default spreads and sovereign CDS (market-based, timely)

### Slide 7: Spreads and Default Probability
- Spread ≈ PD × (1 − R)
- Implied PD = spread / (1 − R)
- Both yields must be in the same currency

### Slide 8: Country Risk Premium for Equity
- CRP = default spread × σ_equity / σ_bond
- Cost of equity = r_f + β × ERP + CRP
- Equity is riskier than sovereign bonds, so scale up

### Slide 9: Recap
- Three determinant families
- Local versus foreign currency default
- Spreads give implied PD and feed equity premiums

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: Country Z.** USD bond yields 7.4%, Treasury 4.2%, recovery 40%, σ_equity = 27%, σ_bond = 18%, β = 1.1, ERP = 5%.
- Default spread = 3.2%.
- Implied PD = 3.2% / 0.60 = 5.33% per year.
- CRP = 3.2% × (27 / 18) = 3.2% × 1.5 = 4.8%.
- Cost of equity = 4.2% + 1.1 × 5% + 4.8% = 14.5% (versus 9.7% with no CRP, 12.9% with an unscaled spread).

**Example 2: CDS-implied PD.** A sovereign's 5-year CDS trades at 300 basis points; recovery 25%.
- Implied PD ≈ 3.00% / 0.75 = 4.0% per year.
- Note that a higher assumed recovery would imply a higher PD for the same spread.

## Questions to ask students
- Why can a government default on its own currency debt if it can print money? (High inflation, banking-sector damage and political costs can make default the lesser evil.)
- Which is usually rated higher, local- or foreign-currency sovereign debt? (Local-currency.)
- A dollar bond yields 6% and a euro bond from the same issuer yields 4%. Is the spread 2%? (No; currencies differ, so compare each with a default-free bond in the same currency.)
- Why scale the default spread for equity? (Equity is more volatile than sovereign bonds, so its risk premium is larger.)
- Which reacts faster to a political crisis, a sovereign rating or a CDS spread? (The CDS spread.)

## Analogies
- Country risk is the weather around a house: even a well-built house suffers in a hurricane.
- Local-currency debt is like owing someone IOUs you can print yourself; you can always pay, but printing too many makes each IOU worthless.
- The CRP volatility scaling is like the difference between insuring a car and insuring the driver of a motorbike on the same road: same road, bigger swings.

## Recording tips
- Use a simple map graphic with three overlapping circles (economic, political, legal) to show interaction.
- Write spread = PD × LGD on screen and rearrange it live; students often forget the LGD.
- Keep the equity CRP section practical; mention that other approaches exist but the scaled spread is the standard exam method.
