## Lecture outline

- 02:00 - Structure: three principles and two professional standards
- 04:00 - Professional integrity: independence, honest representation, no deception
- 07:00 - Conflicts of interest: fair dealing and full disclosure
- 09:00 - Confidentiality and personal benefit

## Slides
### Slide 1: Why ethics for risk managers
- Risk managers must often say no
- Their value depends on trust
- Disasters show the cost of massaged numbers
### Slide 2: Structure of the Code
- Principles: integrity, conflicts, confidentiality
- Standards: fundamental responsibilities, accepted practices
- Specific rules of conduct support each
### Slide 3: Professional integrity
- Act honestly and with independence of thought
- Do not misrepresent analysis or qualifications
- Guard against services being used for improper purposes
### Slide 4: Conflicts of interest
- Deal fairly with all parties
- Disclose actual and potential conflicts fully
- A conflict is not a breach; hiding it is
### Slide 5: Confidentiality
- Keep information confidential without consent
- Never use it for personal benefit
- Legal requirements can override
### Slide 6: Fundamental responsibilities
- Comply with laws and regulations
- Understand the rules governing your work
- Ethical duties cannot be delegated
### Slide 7: Generally accepted practices
- Diligent, independent work
- Stay current; flag departures clearly
- Separate fact from opinion
### Slide 8: Who and what happens
- Members, FRM holders and candidates
- Suspension or removal from membership
- Loss of right to use the FRM designation
### Slide 9: Scenario recap
- Model change to hide breach: disclose and flag
- Bonus conflict: disclose
- Client order: confidential, no personal trading

## Whiteboard examples
**Scenario 1 - Model window change.** Analyst asked to lengthen VaR look-back just before quarter-end to bring VaR under the limit. Solution: integrity and accepted-practice issues. Document the change, report both results, flag the departure from established methodology, escalate if pressured to conceal.

**Scenario 2 - Validator conflict.** Marco, at consultancy Aldren Partners, is asked to validate a credit model that his own firm built for the client. Solution: conflict of interest. Disclose fully to the client before accepting; ensure independence (for example, a separate team) or decline.

**Scenario 3 - Résumé claim.** A candidate who passed Part I lists themselves as "FRM" on a networking profile. Solution: misrepresentation of qualifications; only those who have completed the requirements may use the designation, so the candidate should state that they passed Part I.

## Questions to ask students
- Is having a conflict of interest a violation? (No; failing to disclose it fairly, or letting it bias work, is.)
- Can a member rely on the compliance department to carry their ethical responsibility? (No; it is personal and cannot be delegated.)
- What should you do if you depart from generally accepted risk practice? (Disclose the departure clearly.)
- Does the Code apply to FRM candidates? (Yes.)
- What are possible sanctions for a breach? (Suspension or removal from membership, loss of the right to use the FRM designation.)

## Analogies
- The Code is like a referee's rulebook: a referee who favours the home team is useless, however skilled.
- Disclosing a conflict is like a judge stepping back from a case involving a relative: the relationship is not wrong, hiding it would be.
- Confidential information is a borrowed key: you may use it to do the job you were given, never to enter for yourself.

## Recording tips
- Put the 3 + 2 structure on screen first and refer back to it for each scenario.
- Keep scenarios short and ask viewers to name the principle before revealing the answer.
- Stress the two most tested ideas: disclose conflicts, and ethical responsibility cannot be delegated.
