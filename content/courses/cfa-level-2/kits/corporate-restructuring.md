## Lecture outline
- 00:00 - Hook: a merger is "40% accretive to EPS" and the acquirer's stock falls 10% on the news. Why?
- 02:00 - Motives: synergies, focus, scale, activism, distress; and bad motives.
- 05:00 - Taxonomy: investment, divestment and restructuring actions.
- 09:00 - Divestiture structures: sale, spin-off, split-off, equity carve-out.
- 12:00 - Valuation methods: comparable companies, comparable transactions, DCF, sum-of-the-parts.
- 16:00 - Takeover premiums and the split of merger gains.
- 19:00 - Whiteboard: stock deal with EPS accretion and value split.
- 25:00 - Recap and exam traps.

## Slides
### Slide 1: Why Restructure?
- Synergies, scale, capabilities, focus
- Activist pressure, unlocking value, distress
- Watch for empire-building
### Slide 2: Investment Actions
- Minority equity investment
- Joint venture
- Acquisition (full control)
### Slide 3: Divestment Actions
- Sale: cash or securities received
- Spin-off: pro rata shares, no cash
- Split-off: exchange offer, parent shares retired
- Carve-out: IPO of minority stake, cash raised
### Slide 4: Restructuring Actions
- Cost restructuring
- Leveraged recap, special dividends, buybacks
- Sale-leaseback, bankruptcy reorganisation
### Slide 5: Comparable Company Analysis
- Trading multiples of peers
- Add a takeover premium
- Sensitive to peer selection
### Slide 6: Comparable Transactions and DCF
- Deal multiples already include premium
- Pre-transaction DCF plus synergies
- Triangulate across methods
### Slide 7: Sum-of-the-Parts
- Value each segment separately
- Compare to market value
- Conglomerate discount signals spin-off potential
### Slide 8: Takeover Premium
- (Offer − unaffected price) / unaffected price
- Use the price before rumours
### Slide 9: Who Gains?
- Target gains the premium
- Acquirer gains synergies minus premium
- Stock deals share synergy risk
### Slide 10: EPS Accretion
- Accretive if acquirer's P/E > P/E paid
- Accretion ≠ value creation
### Slide 11: Key Takeaways
- Know the divestiture structures cold
- Unaffected price for premiums
- Synergies minus premium = acquirer's gain

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: stock deal.** Acquirer: NI 200m, 100m shares, price 40. Target: NI 30m, 20m shares, unaffected price 15. Offer 18 per share in stock. Synergies PV 80m.
- Premium = 20%; total premium = 60m.
- Shares issued = 360 / 40 = 9m.
- Combined EPS = 230 / 109 = 2.11 (vs 2.00): accretive; P/E paid 12 < 20.
- Target gain 60m; acquirer gain 80 − 60 = 20m.
- If synergies are 50m: acquirer loses 10m, yet EPS still accretive.

**Example 2: sum-of-the-parts.** A conglomerate has three segments valued at 12x, 8x and 15x EBITDA of 100m, 50m and 40m, and net debt of 600m.
- EV = 1,200 + 400 + 600 = 2,200m. Equity = 1,600m.
- If market cap is 1,300m, the conglomerate discount is (1,600 − 1,300) / 1,600 = 18.75%, a possible case for a spin-off.

## Questions to ask students
- Which divestiture raises cash for the parent while keeping control? (Equity carve-out.)
- Which reduces the parent's share count? (Split-off.)
- Why use the unaffected price for the premium? (Pre-announcement run-ups embed part of the premium.)
- Can an accretive deal destroy value? (Yes, if the premium exceeds synergies.)
- Should you add a takeover premium to comparable transaction multiples? (No; they already include it.)

## Analogies
- A spin-off is a parent giving each child a slice of a new family business; a carve-out is selling a minority slice to outsiders for cash.
- Paying a premium above synergies is buying a house for more than the renovations will ever add.
- EPS accretion from a low-P/E target is a sugar rush: it feels good on the income statement but says nothing about health.

## Recording tips
- Draw a table comparing spin-off, split-off and carve-out (who gets shares, cash raised, control retained).
- Run the "synergies disappoint" sensitivity live; it lands the point that accretion is not value.
- Remind students vignettes often show both the unaffected price and a rumour-inflated price.
