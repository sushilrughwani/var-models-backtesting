## Slides
### Slide 1: Inputs Are Estimates
- Beta, ERP and risk-free rate all uncertain
- Hardest cases: emerging markets, small and private firms
- Method choice can move r_e by percentage points
### Slide 2: Historical ERP
- Average excess return over long periods
- Arithmetic > geometric
- Survivorship bias inflates estimates
### Slide 3: Forward-Looking ERP
- ERP = D1/P0 + g − long-term government yield
- Uses current prices; reacts to market levels
- Sensitive to the growth assumption
### Slide 4: Supply-Side ERP
- Inflation + real EPS growth + change in P/E + income yield
- Minus risk-free rate
- Ties ERP to macro fundamentals
### Slide 5: Country Risk Premium
- CRP = sovereign spread × σ_equity / σ_bond
- Spread on USD sovereign debt over developed benchmark
- Added to ERP inside or outside the beta term
### Slide 6: Unlevering a Comparable Beta
- β_A = β_E / [1 + (1 − t)D/E]
- Use the comparable's D/E and tax rate
- Market values preferred
### Slide 7: Relevering for the Target
- β_E = β_A [1 + (1 − t)D/E] with target's inputs
- More leverage, higher equity beta
- Adjusted beta: 2/3 raw + 1/3 × 1
### Slide 8: Build-Up Approach
- r_e = r_f + ERP + size + industry + specific
- Implicit beta of 1
- Specific premium is subjective
### Slide 9: Size Premium Caveats
- Small-cap returns partly reflect illiquidity
- Data and survivorship issues
- Premium unstable over time
### Slide 10: Key Takeaways
- Unlever with theirs, relever with yours
- Equity volatility on top in the CRP
- Always state your method

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: emerging-market private firm.** Comparable β_E = 1.20, D/E = 0.50, t = 25%; target D/E = 0.80, t = 30%; r_f = 4.0%; ERP = 5.5%; sovereign spread 3.0%, σ_equity 30%, σ_bond 20%.
- β_A = 1.20 / 1.375 = 0.8727.
- β_E = 0.8727 × 1.56 = 1.3615.
- CRP = 3.0% × 1.5 = 4.5%.
- r_e = 4.0% + 1.3615 × 10.0% = 17.61% (or 15.99% with CRP outside beta).

**Example 2: Gordon ERP.** Forward dividend yield 2.2%, long-term growth 6.0%, 20-year government yield 4.2%.
- ERP = 2.2% + 6.0% − 4.2% = 4.0%.

**Example 3: build-up.** r_f 4.0%, ERP 5.5%, size premium 3.0%, company-specific premium 1.5%.
- r_e = 4.0% + 5.5% + 3.0% + 1.5% = 14.0%.

## Questions to ask students
- When relevering, whose tax rate do you use? (The target company's.)
- Why might a thinly traded stock show a low regression beta? (Stale prices understate co-movement with the market.)
- Which is higher, arithmetic or geometric historical ERP? (Arithmetic.)
- What beta does the build-up approach implicitly assume? (1.0.)
- If sovereign bond volatility rises while equity volatility is unchanged, what happens to the CRP? (It falls.)

## Analogies
- Unlevering and relevering is like removing a car's roof rack to compare engines, then fitting your own rack.
- The CRP volatility ratio is a currency converter: it translates bond-market default risk into equity-market units.
- The build-up method is stacking blocks: each layer of risk adds a block on a base of the market.

## Recording tips
- Put "unlever with theirs, relever with yours" on screen as a banner during the beta section.
- Show both CRP placements side by side so students see the sensitivity.
- Warn that vignettes often give both the comparable's and the target's tax rates to test which one is used.
