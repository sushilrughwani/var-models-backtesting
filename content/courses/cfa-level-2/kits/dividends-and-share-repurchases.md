## Slides
### Slide 1: MM Irrelevance
- No taxes, costs or information gaps
- Homemade dividends replicate any policy
- Value comes from investments, not payout
### Slide 2: Bird-in-the-Hand and Taxes
- Dividends seen as safer (critics: fallacy)
- Heavier dividend taxes favour low payout
- Clienteles sort by tax and income needs
### Slide 3: Signalling
- Increases and initiations signal confidence
- Cuts signal trouble; markets punish them
- Repurchases can signal undervaluation
### Slide 4: Agency Costs
- Payouts discipline managers with free cash flow
- Large payouts can hurt bondholders
- Covenants limit distributions
### Slide 5: Effective Tax Rates
- Double taxation: 1 − (1 − t_c)(1 − t_d)
- Split-rate: use the lower distributed-earnings corporate rate
- Imputation: equals shareholder's marginal rate
### Slide 6: Repurchase Methods
- Open market (most flexible)
- Fixed-price tender and Dutch auction
- Direct negotiation (watch for premiums)
### Slide 7: The Earnings-Yield Rule
- E/P > after-tax cost of funds: EPS rises
- E/P < after-tax cost of funds: EPS falls
- EPS accretion ≠ value creation
### Slide 8: BVPS Effect
- Price > BVPS: BVPS falls
- Price < BVPS: BVPS rises
### Slide 9: Target Payout Adjustment
- D1 = D0 + a(τE1 − D0)
- Smoothing: partial adjustment toward target
- Adjustment speed a between 0 and 1
### Slide 10: Dividend Safety
- FCFE coverage = FCFE / (dividends + buybacks)
- Below 1: funded by reserves or debt
- Combine with payout ratio and leverage trend

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: debt-financed buyback.** NI USD 50m, 20m shares, price 40, equity 600m, borrow 100m at 6%, tax 25%.
- EPS before = 2.50; earnings yield 6.25% vs after-tax cost 4.5%: accretive.
- Shares bought = 2.5m; remaining 17.5m.
- New NI = 50 − 4.5 = 45.5; EPS = 2.60.
- BVPS: 30.00 → 500 / 17.5 = 28.57.

**Example 2: target payout.** D0 = 1.00, expected EPS 3.00, target payout 40%, adjustment speed 0.3.
- Target dividend = 1.20. D1 = 1.00 + 0.3 × (1.20 − 1.00) = 1.06.

**Example 3: tax systems.** Corporate rate on distributed earnings 30% (split-rate; retained 40%), individual dividend tax 20%.
- Effective = 1 − 0.70 × 0.80 = 44%. Using the 40% retained rate by mistake gives 1 − 0.60 × 0.80 = 52%.

**Example 4: coverage.** FCFE 80m, dividends 50m, buybacks 40m. Coverage = 80 / 90 = 0.89: payout exceeds FCFE.

## Questions to ask students
- Under MM, how does an investor create a dividend from a non-payer? (Sell a portion of shares.)
- When does a cash-funded buyback dilute EPS? (When the after-tax yield on the cash exceeds the earnings yield.)
- Price is below BVPS and the firm buys back shares. What happens to BVPS? (It rises.)
- Under an imputation system, what is the effective tax rate on dividends? (The shareholder's marginal tax rate.)
- Why might bondholders dislike a large special dividend? (Assets leave the firm, reducing their protection.)

## Analogies
- Homemade dividends: cutting a slice from your own pizza instead of having the waiter cut it. Same pizza either way.
- Dividend signalling is a pay rise: a boss who raises your salary expects to keep paying it; a cut tells you something is wrong.
- The earnings-yield rule is a swap: you give up cash earning 2% to buy shares earning 5%; EPS goes up.

## Recording tips
- Show the earnings-yield comparison before doing any arithmetic; students should predict the answer first.
- Highlight BVPS direction with a simple rule on screen: price above book, BVPS down.
- Spend time on after-tax versus pre-tax cost; it is the most common numerical slip.
