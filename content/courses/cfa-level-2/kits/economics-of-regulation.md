## Lecture outline

## Slides
### Slide 1: Why Regulate?
- Markets fail: informational frictions and externalities
- Goals: stability, integrity, consumer protection, competition
- No failure, no case for regulation
### Slide 2: Informational Frictions
- Adverse selection and moral hazard
- Remedies: disclosure, licensing, capital rules
- Trust keeps markets liquid
### Slide 3: Externalities
- Costs or benefits fall on outsiders
- Pollution, systemic bank failures
- Overproduction of harmful activities
### Slide 4: Who Makes the Rules
- Legislatures: statutes
- Agencies: detailed rules and enforcement
- Courts: interpretation and judicial enforcement
### Slide 5: Self-Regulatory Organisations
- Expertise and low taxpayer cost
- Conflict: regulate their own funders
- Standard setters lack enforcement power
### Slide 6: Regulatory Tools
- Taxes and subsidies (price mechanisms)
- Bans, licences, capital and disclosure requirements
- Government provision; enforcement makes it credible
### Slide 7: Cost-Benefit Analysis
- NPV = −upfront cost + PV(benefits − compliance costs)
- Include indirect costs and unintended consequences
- Benefits are hard to measure; costs easier
### Slide 8: Capture, Competition, Arbitrage
- Capture: regulator serves industry
- Competition: jurisdictions race to the bottom
- Arbitrage: firms exploit rule gaps
### Slide 9: Regulation in Valuation
- Barriers to entry protect margins
- Price caps limit upside
- Capital rules shape ROE and dividends

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: disclosure rule.** Upfront cost USD 40m; annual benefits USD 12m; annual compliance costs USD 5m; 10 years; 6% discount rate.
- Net annual benefit = 7m.
- Annuity factor = (1 − 1.06⁻¹⁰) / 0.06 = 7.3601.
- PV = 7 × 7.3601 = 51.52m. NPV = 51.52 − 40 = USD 11.52m.
- Common error: using gross benefits gives 12 × 7.3601 − 40 = 48.32m, overstating the case.

**Example 2: classify the tool.** (a) Carbon tax: price mechanism. (b) Minimum bank capital ratio: requiring an activity. (c) Ban on short selling during a crisis: restricting an activity. (d) Deposit insurance: government provision.

## Questions to ask students
- What two market failures most often justify financial regulation? (Informational frictions and externalities.)
- What is the main weakness of an SRO? (Conflict of interest from regulating its members and funders.)
- Can an accounting standard setter fine a company? (No; it relies on regulators adopting its standards.)
- Give an example of regulatory arbitrage. (Booking a trade in a lightly regulated subsidiary to avoid capital charges.)
- Why might cost-benefit analysis be biased? (Costs are concentrated and measurable; benefits are diffuse and hard to quantify.)

## Analogies
- Regulation is a referee: needed when players cannot police themselves, harmful when the referee starts playing for one team (capture).
- Regulatory arbitrage is water finding cracks: tighten one place and activity flows to the weakest point.
- Externalities are a neighbour's loud party: you bear the cost without being at the party.
