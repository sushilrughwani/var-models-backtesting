## Slides
### Slide 1: What Makes ESG Material?
- Affects revenue, costs, assets, liabilities or cost of capital
- Varies by industry and time horizon
- Use materiality maps to focus
### Slide 2: The Three Pillars
- E: emissions, water, waste, physical climate risk
- S: labour, safety, data security, supply chain
- G: board, ownership, pay, related parties, audit
### Slide 3: Governance as Gatekeeper
- Weak governance lets other risks go unmanaged
- Dual-class shares and controlling holders
- Pay linked to the right metrics
### Slide 4: Investment Approaches
- Exclusionary vs best-in-class screening
- Thematic and impact investing
- Engagement and full integration
### Slide 5: Valuation Lever 1: Cash Flows
- Revenue, operating costs, capex, provisions
- Terminal value and asset lives (stranded assets)
- Remember the tax effect
### Slide 6: Valuation Lever 2: Discount Rate
- Add a premium for hard-to-quantify risks
- Never for a risk already in the cash flows
- Small rate changes, large value changes
### Slide 7: Carbon Cost Formula
- ΔNOPAT = −Emissions × Price × (1 − pass-through) × (1 − t)
- Perpetuity value effect = ΔNOPAT / WACC
### Slide 8: ESG in Credit
- Bondholders: limited upside, full downside
- Litigation, fines, liabilities, governance failures
- Wider spreads and downgrade risk
### Slide 9: Green Bonds
- Proceeds fund designated green projects
- Credit risk usually equals issuer's senior debt
- Check reporting to avoid greenwashing

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: carbon tax.** Emissions 200,000 t, carbon price USD 50, 30% pass-through, tax 25%, WACC 8%, no growth.
- Gross cost = 10.0m; absorbed = 7.0m; after tax = 5.25m.
- Value impact = 5.25 / 0.08 = USD 65.6m lower.
- No pass-through: 7.5 / 0.08 = 93.75m.

**Example 2: ESG risk premium.** Next year's FCFE USD 10m, growth 3%, cost of equity 9%. Weak governance leads the analyst to add 0.5%.
- Before: 10 / (0.09 − 0.03) = 166.67m.
- After: 10 / (0.095 − 0.03) = 153.85m.
- Value falls by 12.82m (7.7%) from just 50 basis points.

## Questions to ask students
- Is water use equally material for a bank and a brewer? (No; materiality is industry-specific.)
- Why is double counting a risk in ESG valuation? (The same risk can be hit through both cash flows and the discount rate.)
- Which screening approach keeps exposure to every sector? (Best-in-class.)
- Why do credit analysts focus on ESG downside? (Bondholders do not share in upside.)
- Does a green bond usually have lower default risk than the issuer's ordinary senior bonds? (No; same issuer recourse.)

## Analogies
- Materiality is a doctor's checklist by patient type: you do not run a pregnancy test on every patient.
- Double counting is paying for insurance twice: once in the cash flows, once in the discount rate.
- Governance is the immune system: when it is weak, every other infection spreads.

## Recording tips
- Show an industry materiality grid on screen to make the industry-specific point vivid.
- Do Example 2 slowly: students underestimate how sensitive value is to the discount rate.
- Keep the tone analytical, not ideological; the exam frames ESG as a risk and value issue.
