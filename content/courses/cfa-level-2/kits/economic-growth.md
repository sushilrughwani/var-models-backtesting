## Slides
### Slide 1: Growth and Asset Prices
- Earnings cannot outgrow GDP forever
- Potential growth anchors terminal growth rates
- Faster trend growth supports credit quality
### Slide 2: Preconditions for Growth
- Saving, investment and functioning financial markets
- Rule of law, property rights, political stability
- Education, health, openness to trade
### Slide 3: The Production Function
- $Y = A K^{\alpha} L^{1-\alpha}$
- α ≈ capital's share of income (0.3 to 0.4)
- A = total factor productivity
### Slide 4: Growth Accounting
- Output growth = TFP growth + α × capital growth + (1 − α) × labour growth
- TFP is the Solow residual
- Use historical data to see what drove growth
### Slide 5: Labour Productivity
- Productivity growth = TFP growth + α × (capital growth − labour growth)
- Second term = capital deepening
- Potential GDP growth = labour growth + productivity growth
### Slide 6: Diminishing Returns
- Each extra unit of capital per worker adds less output
- Capital deepening alone cannot sustain per-capita growth
- Saving rate changes the level, not the long-run rate
### Slide 7: Neoclassical Steady State
- Productivity grows at θ / (1 − α)
- Output grows at θ / (1 − α) + n
- Transitional growth fades toward steady state
### Slide 8: Endogenous Growth
- Knowledge and R&D spill over across firms
- No diminishing returns at the economy level
- Saving and innovation policy can raise growth permanently
### Slide 9: Convergence Hypotheses
- Absolute: everyone converges (weak evidence)
- Conditional: converge if characteristics match
- Club: must meet institutional thresholds to join
### Slide 10: Key Takeaways
- Weights: α to capital, 1 − α to labour
- Technology is the only engine of sustained per-capita growth (neoclassical)
- Openness speeds convergence

## Whiteboard examples
**Example 1: growth accounting.** α = 0.35, capital growth 4.0%, labour growth 1.0%, TFP growth 1.2%.
- Output growth = 1.2% + 0.35 × 4.0% + 0.65 × 1.0% = 1.2% + 1.40% + 0.65% = 3.25%.
- Labour productivity growth = 1.2% + 0.35 × 3.0% = 2.25%.

**Example 2: steady state.** Same economy, TFP growth θ = 1.2%, labour force growth n = 1.0%.
- Steady-state productivity growth = 1.2% / 0.65 = 1.85%.
- Steady-state output growth = 1.85% + 1.0% = 2.85%.
- Interpretation: current growth of 3.25% includes capital deepening that will fade.

**Example 3: Solow residual.** Output grew 3.2%, capital 3.5%, labour 0.8%, α = 0.4.
- TFP = 3.2% − 0.4 × 3.5% − 0.6 × 0.8% = 3.2% − 1.40% − 0.48% = 1.32%.

## Questions to ask students
- What happens to long-run per-capita growth in the Solow model if the saving rate doubles? (Nothing; the level of income rises, the growth rate returns to θ / (1 − α).)
- How is TFP measured? (As the residual after subtracting weighted capital and labour contributions.)
- Why do endogenous growth models allow constant returns to capital? (Knowledge spillovers offset diminishing returns at the economy level.)
- What separates conditional from absolute convergence? (Conditional requires similar saving, population growth and technology.)
- What two components make up potential GDP growth? (Labour force growth and labour productivity growth.)

## Analogies
- Capital deepening is giving a cook more pans: the first extra pan helps a lot, the tenth barely at all. A better recipe (technology) helps forever.
- Club convergence is a members-only lift: countries that meet the entry requirements (institutions) ride up together; others take the stairs.
- The Solow residual is the "everything else" line on a receipt: what you paid minus the items you can identify.

## Recording tips
- Write the growth accounting equation once and colour-code the weights; swapping α and 1 − α is the top error.
- Do the arithmetic slowly on screen and check that productivity growth plus labour growth equals output growth.
- Connect back to equity valuation: terminal growth above potential GDP growth is a red flag.
